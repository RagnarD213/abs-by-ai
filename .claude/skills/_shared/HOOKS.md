# Hooks: the first five seconds (Dan, 2026-09-21)

Read this before writing the opening of ANY script: shorts, long-form content, ads, VSLs.
Shared by `/shorts-scripting`, `/scriptfromoutline`, `/scriptwriting` and `/shortsideas`.

## Why this exists

Dan's verdict on the 9/23 shoot's short-form outlines: the bodies were good, the hooks were the
weakest part, because they "literally transcribed the points from the long-form video. It didn't
really catch attention in the beginning and give the viewer a strong reason to watch." His rule:
**catch attention and hook them in within the first 5 seconds, otherwise they skip, and if the
rest of the content is good it won't matter.** This matches his ad feedback (`/scriptwriting`,
"THE FIRST MINUTE DECIDES EVERYTHING"). The hook is the part of the script that gets the most
writing effort, not the least.

## The rules

1. **Write the hook fresh. Never lift it from the source.** A section of a long-form video opens
   for a viewer who is already watching. A short opens for a stranger mid-scroll. The failure
   test: if your first sentence would work as sentence one of a section twenty minutes into the
   long-form ("Use a meal prep service." "Number five, buy a Tesla."), it is a transcription.
   Rewrite it.
2. **The first sentence does the stopping on its own. About 15 words, 5 seconds.** It has to be
   one of these, and it has to be concrete (a number, a named object, a named person):
   - a surprising cause that blames a THING: "Stop cooking your own meals. It's the reason you
     still have belly fat."
   - a bold command that sounds wrong: "If you're too busy to work out, cancel your gym
     membership."
   - a first-person proof claim only Dan can make: "I've never written a single line of code,
     and AI built my entire app."
   - a deictic open on something visible on screen: "THIS robot mows your lawn by itself..." Only when
     the same sentence makes a claim. A bare label ("This is the pull-up.", "See this exercise right
     here?") is not a hook: Dan rewrote all 15 of them on 2026-10-19 (section below).
   - a line that picks a fight with an institution: "Your teachers are going to hate this video."
3. **By second 5 the viewer knows what HE gets for staying.** Name the payoff in his terms: hours
   back, belly fat gone, abs, better grades, a price he already pays. A personal story or a topic
   with no payoff attached is not a reason to watch.
4. **Leave a loop open.** Promise something the body pays off ("here's what I do instead, and it
   costs less than Chipotle"), so leaving now means missing the answer.
5. **Banned first sentences:** "In the next 60 seconds...", "Here's an app that...", "Today we're
   talking about...", "If you're a student/professional and you have no time...", any question the
   viewer can answer "no" to and scroll, any greeting, any bio. A time-promise ("in the next
   minute") may appear as sentence two at most.
6. **Blame a thing, never the viewer.** The gym commute, cooking, the lawn, homework, an industry.
   The viewer is the ally being let in on something (`/shorts-scripting` lesson 22). "You're
   wrong" negations are still out; "X is why you're fat" is in.
7. **Open on the picture when the subject is visible.** Cue the first frame so it shows the thing
   the hook names (the app, the robot, Dan hands-off in the car). A strong line over a talking
   head loses to the same line over the thing itself.
8. **Stay true.** A hook may be bold, but never false. Check it against the facts before using it
   (the Tesla is not cheaper to own than a Camry; do not open on "trade in for a cheaper car").

## Process

- Write **three** hooks for every script, each from a different type in rule 2. Put the strongest
  in the script. List the other two directly under the script as **HOOK TAKES**, so Dan can film
  them and the edit can A/B them. (Same format as the /start VSL hook takes.)
- Read only the first sentence of the chosen hook, cold. Would a guy who does not care about the
  topic stop scrolling? Does sentence two tell him what he gets? If either answer is no, rewrite.
- Long-form and VSL: the same rules govern the first 30 seconds, and the stopper still has to land
  in the first 5.

## Exercise and how-to openers: Dan's rewrites, 2026-10-19 (23 of 26 rewritten)

Gym shorts for the 10/23 shoot. Dan: the bodies were strong, the beginnings were "very informational, claudish,
straightforward" and not attention-getting. "Put more effort into the beginning... every word counts in those
first few sentences." What he wrote instead, in four moves. Use them before anything else for a how-to:

1. **Rank the exercise, in absolutes.** "Pull-ups are the king of back exercises." "Dips are the king of triceps
   exercises." "Preacher curls are the best exercise for building peaked, attention-getting biceps." "This machine
   is the key to building a wide back and a V-taper physique."
2. **Attack the popular alternative and name the winner.** "Stop doing squats - the leg press machine is way
   better." "Stop doing bench press. The chest press machine is a way safer and more effective way to build your
   pecs." "Stop doing military presses." "Chest flies are far superior to bench press." "Stop doing flat bench
   press. It's one of the stupidest exercises in the gym." Safety is part of the win: "without the risk of injury",
   "and they're safer".
3. **Name the look the viewer wants, in his words:** a wide back and a V-taper physique, strong, defined, blocky
   abs, thick legs, well-balanced aesthetic legs, peaked biceps, an aesthetic chest, defined six pack abs.
4. **Then a short "Here's how to do them (right)."** "But most guys are doing them wrong" is his bridge when the
   body corrects form.

Never again as sentence one: "This is the X.", "See this exercise right here?", "This right here is...", "Here's how
to do X" with no claim before it, and a "If you want to lose your belly fat..." preamble standing in for a claim.
Before and after (mine, then his): "This is the hamstring curl, and here's how to do it with good form." became
"Hamstring curls are the key to building well-balanced aesthetic legs." "I don't do flat bench press, and here's
why." became "Stop doing flat bench press. It's one of the stupidest exercises in the gym."
