his ad scripts 2019 to 2025 (including the HBI ads that spent $5.96M), his 121,000-word book, his outlines, his spoken
transcript, and his edits to Claude drafts. Real passages: `voice/` (`ads.md`, `content-longform.md`,
`content-shorts.md`, `sales.md`, `outlines.md`, `book.md`, `dan-edits-2026-10-06.md`); outside writers:
`voice/borrowed-keith-wes.md`; numbers: `voice/STATS.md`. Rules on facts, persona and offers stay in `WRITING-RULES.md`.

**Added 2026-10-08 (Voice P2A).** Whole real passages, one file per type of writing: `voice/passages-content.md`,

Evidence: **A** = Dan wrote it, **B** = his edits to a Claude draft, **C** = Claude text he kept (weak; marked). Patterns
are ranked by evidence.

**HBI is not Abs By AI.** HBI sold to older people in joint pain. Copy the persuasion moves; never the topic, the age

rhythm only. Current facts: memory `dan-personal-facts-for-scripts`.

## 1. Openers and hooks

He opens on the payoff, never on a scene or a topic announcement.

- **The plain promise of the whole piece**, then a proof promise. "Here are my top 10 tips for losing your belly fat and
  getting six pack abs" (A, the channel's top video). "Here are five tips to get the most out of Zepbound, so you lose
  fat as fast as possible while keeping your muscle." (B)
- **A command or a verdict with one capitalized word.** "STOP doing deadlifts!" (A). "Do NOT use boxing in a street
  fight! It could get you killed!" (A, 2022). "Being cheap with your health is ruining your life." (A)
- **A question the viewer answers in his head**, often over props. "Which one of these foods contains a protein that can
  make your arthritis pain worse?" (A, $5.96M, the biggest ad). "No time to work out? I'm going to solve that problem
  for you." (B, he wrote it over Claude's clever hooks)
- **A first-person result or confession.** "When I struggled with belly fat, this was the body I wanted." (B, Ad 7). "I
  just turned 41. And I was filled with a huge sense of regret on my birthday." (A)

Then within two lines: the time box or deliverable ("in the next few minutes, I'll show you", "in this quick 3 minute
video", "I'll prove it to you in today's video"), used since 2019. "Today I'm going to show you…" is his too (A, 2020
and 2026); the Claude tell is "Today we're talking about", not "Today".

## 2. Sentence shape

From `voice/STATS.md`:

- **Fewer "and"s.** Claude: 3.55 per 100 words. Dan's own content, book and speech: 2.2 to 2.4; his ad scripts: 3.0.
  Aim at 2.5 in content, 3.0 in ads.
- **Setups run long, turns are short.** His ad sentences average 14.5 words, Claude's 10.4. His swing between long and
  short is wider. Do not chop setups into fragments; keep the short sentence for the turn ("It's impossible.",
  "It was AI.").
- **Almost no kicker endings.** Claude ends about 1 paragraph in 5 on a six-word punch (ads); Dan 1 in 16, his book
  1 in 30. End a paragraph on the payoff for the viewer, not a quip.
- **One or two sentences per paragraph**, one claim each.
- **Asides take a spaced hyphen ( - ) or "--". Never an em dash** (Claude writes 25 times his rate).
- **One word in capitals for stress** (MUST, NEVER, HATE, FAR, HOW), about one per paragraph at most. **Ellipses as
  spoken beats** before a reveal: "the thing that changed everything for me was…AI."
- **Three-item lists:** fine in ads ("even if he's bigger than you, even if he's stronger than you, and even if he's
  armed"); in content Claude uses 45 percent more than he does. Cut the tidy triad that only sounds nice.

## 3. Pet phrases

His (A, across years): "Listen -" before the emotional push or the credential; "See," and "Now," to turn into the body;
"OK…so"; "That's why…", "That's because…" (reasons always follow claims); "Let's say…"; "But here's the good news…";
"The truth is,"; "Believe it or not"; "The result?"; "Maybe you can relate."; "Shockingly,"; "killer results";
"completely obsolete"; "lock in"; "guys like us", "regular guys like us"; "lose your belly fat", "six pack abs"; "I
guarantee you'll…"; "Pretty amazing, right?"; "Don't procrastinate"; "That's why I do X, and why you should too" (B).

**Not his:** "Let's be honest", "really, really" (Claude's, in Ad 1, C); "Here is the uncomfortable truth", "Honest
answer.", "This is that video" (cut by Dan, B); "quote unquote"; doubled intensifiers as a device.

## 4. Numbers and his own story

- **His own loss is the credential.** Book: "I personally wasted hundreds of thousands of dollars…" (A). Ads: "I
  consider myself one of the top fitness experts in the world. But I've had to face reality. AI simply gets better
  results… including me." (B)
- **He under-claims about himself and over-claims about the result.** "I wasn't really fat, but my abs also weren't
  that defined." (B)
- **Real numbers, typed as digits:** 200 pounds, 38, 40, $1000 a month, 1.5 mg, 190 to 180, 22 years. Never invent one;
  leave a `[DAN: …]` gap.
- **"Let's say you're 5'6" and 150 pounds…"**: numbers the viewer can picture (A).
- **Story order (book and letter):** the low point in plain or physical words ("I was overdrawn, again."; "stressed out as
  hell"), the failed attempt, the effort with a number, the discovery held for one beat ("In two words: YouTube
  Marketing.").

## 5. How he builds a mechanism

1. **Rule out what the viewer blames.** "It's not because of new medication or treatment. It's simply because…" (A,
   best CPA, $48.14).
2. **Name one hidden cause, often a villain inside something "healthy".** "Some of these foods are commonly thought of as
   healthy -- and for most people, they are. But for those of us with…" (A). Coined names are his ("The Joint Pain
   Protein", "Modern Masculinity Killer").
3. **One plain definition or everyday analogy** ("A bee sting is an example of inflammation on the outside of your
   body"), never a pile of science.
4. **Abs By AI's mechanism is motivation:** your plan is fine; average motivation gets average results; imagine a
   fitness model's motivation; "But HOW…?"; then the product (B, Ads 5 and 6).
5. **Concede before attacking**: "I don't hate boxing. Boxing is great exercise" (A); "Not because trainers aren't
   trying to help you - they are." (B)
6. **Not your fault, then blame the method:** "If dieting has failed you, it's not your fault." (A) "The old way of
   getting in shape works just fine for college kids. But it doesn't work for guys like us." (A)

## 6. How he sells and closes

- **Desire and status, not decline.** He cut every "muscle-loss clock" (B). He added "the dad with abs that your wife
  drools over, and which all the other men envy" and "My entire life has changed after getting abs, and I know yours
  will too." (B)
- **Proof by other people's results**, framed in one line: "Here's what happened when people like you… Pretty amazing,
  right?" (A, $5.96M).
- **Free, fast, easy, plus a life-change promise:** "It's free, it only takes a few seconds, and it could change your
  life." (B). Today's offer is the 7-day free trial (`WRITING-RULES.md` section 5).
- **Urgency with a reason:** "This presentation will only be free for a limited time… Don't procrastinate!" (A)
- **Content closes warm and plain:** "Thanks for watching guys. If you enjoyed this video, subscribe…" (B). The app only
  when it continues the topic; shorts end on "let me know how it works for you" (A).
- **Bluntness names a condition the viewer can change (fat, broke, poor, lazy, cheap), never his character.** He
  hardened "the one thing that can actually go wrong" to "the average person loses a catastrophic amount of muscle"
  (B), and softened Claude's "you are too squeamish" to "you don't have the skill" (B).

## 8. What Claude writes that Dan cuts

From the B pairs (`voice/dan-edits-2026-10-06.md`, `scriptfromoutline` WHAT DAN CHANGED):

- Meta-openers and announcements ("Honest answer. Yes.", "This is that video", fragment lists like "Keto. Fasting.
  Paleo.").
- Aphorisms and coined abstractions ("Motivation is not a personality trait. It's a supply.").
- Dramatized scenes and kickers ("The abs or the bar tab", "I know how that sounds. I mean it anyway.").
- "To be fair" hedges, up-front disclaimers, defending against imagined objections, and turning blame on the viewer.
- Fear and decline beats in ads; underselling the topic of the video.
- **Swearing and controversy** (rules and real lines: `voice/profanity-and-controversy.md`). No swear is ever
  required: 12 of his 22 off-the-cuff videos have none, and workouts, shorts and paid ads stay clean. When he swears,
  the word is the plain name of junk, waste or an excuse ("wasting the money you do have on bullshit"), inside a full
  sentence, never at a person or group, never an opener or kicker. Ceiling: two per 2,000 words, opinion videos only.
  The edge is the opinion: state it flat, deny the viewer's objection ("but you don't"), back it with his own number.
  Cut stock idioms ("like hell", "the damn picture") and every wink or apology.
- A generic app pitch at the end of content.
- Em dashes, "and" chains, six-word kickers, tidy triads (section 2).

Before delivering: count em dashes (0), run `python3 scripts/voice/voice_stats.py <draft.txt>` and compare with the
