# Type notes: ads (scripts he wrote)

From 20 pairs (12 his own ads, 8 client ads). Numbers are per 1,000 words, his / Claude's cold draft. Client ads
(another presenter reads them) teach structure, not his voice: weigh the own-ad lines first.

- **Paragraphs of three to five sentences** in the body. Claude breaks an ad into one-sentence lines (6 of 12 own
  ads, 5 of 8 client). A single line on its own is for the hook or the reveal, not for every sentence.
- **A linked thought in one sentence**, joined with "because", "since", "and" or "with" (7 of 12). Claude writes
  "The reason is simple." and starts again. 23% of Claude's sentences are 5 words or fewer; his: 5%.
- **He repeats the noun and the verb.** "The book", "businesses", the product's full name, every time (7 of 12, 6 of
  8). "Learn" for what the viewer gets, again and again, where Claude rotates "discover", "find out", "reveal".
- **Starts on the subject or the instruction.** Claude opens with "So", "And" or "Now" (6 of 12). "so" 4.2 / 8.3.
- **The viewer is the subject**: "you'll learn", "you'll be surprised". Claude makes it "I'm going to show you".
  "going to" 2.7 / 5.2; "will" and "'ll" are his.
- **No padding "actually"** (0.4 / 2.8). "many", not "a lot of" (2.2 / 0.3 against 0.1 / 2.8). "need to" 3.6 / 1.7,
  "have to" 2.4 / 0.7.
- **Calls to action are bare:** 'Click "Claim Book" now'. Not 'click the "Claim Book" button right now'.
- **His typing:** "..." for a spoken pause (3 per 1,000 words; Claude never types one), " -- " for an aside, an
  exclamation mark on a reveal or a sign-off, a colon only before a list or a cut-in clip.
- **A notch formal:** "eliminate", "minimize", "procrastinate", "consume". Claude swaps in "cut out", "put off",
  "stay away from".
