## The content, as a neutral brief
Who: Dan, speaking on camera in a YouTube ad, addressing viewers who want to earn money online, especially people in regular jobs.

Points, in order:
- Getting his book will send the viewer's confidence soaring: beyond gaining the skills to earn through YouTube ads, they will feel far ahead of other business owners and believe online income is within their reach.
- Tells them to press the button labelled Claim Book underneath and grab a copy now; he cannot hand out free copies indefinitely, so act before the offer ends.
- Says there are some things the viewer should understand about him.
- He is no smarter than the viewer; he made a great many dumb mistakes early on in online advertising.
- He is no harder working than the viewer; someone with a nine-to-five job is probably working much harder than he is at this moment.
- He began his business with no money and no connections, only determination to succeed and a capacity to learn from experts he came across on the internet.
- So nothing stops the viewer from matching what he has done, or exceeding it.
- The viewer could be lounging in a swimming pool, drink in hand, on a weekday Monday, as he is doing as he speaks, rather than grinding at a job they dislike.
- Admits this will not happen by itself: realising the dream takes time spent learning the skills, and now is the ideal moment to acquire them.
- Repeats the call to press Claim Book underneath for the book, whose title is "15 Steps To" followed by "Profitable YouTube Marketing". It costs nothing apart from shipping, and comes in whichever form the viewer prefers: paperback, ebook or audiobook.

The point: Persuade the viewer that an ordinary person can copy his success and get them to claim the free book (paying shipping only) right away before the offer ends.
