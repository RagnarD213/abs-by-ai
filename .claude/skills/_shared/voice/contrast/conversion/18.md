## The content, as a neutral brief
Who: The speaker (Dan) talks directly to viewers of a video about YouTube marketing, in the first person.

Points, in order:
- When he launched his first business built on YouTube ads he had no money; worse, he owed money, and the creditor was the IRS, which he calls the worst sort of debt.
- No investors backed him, so he began with very little to spend. He clearly recalls his starting ad spend being just $10 per day, and that it was scaled up from that level later.
- Besides lacking money, he knew nothing about advertising online.
- He had no contacts: no friends with internet marketing experience and no mentors to guide him. He learned by working things out alone and by watching videos similar to the one the viewer is watching.
- He congratulates the viewer: watching and learning about YouTube marketing is a smart choice.
- He says his success is not down to being cleverer or working harder than others. The reason he is financially free and speaking to them now is that he had the sense to recognise when luck was on his side. He then explains what he means.
- In 2009 his business tried several marketing channels, including Google search and Facebook ads, among others.
- None of it took off until a video they put on YouTube reached 10,000 views, which was a major event for them then, and it began bringing in one or two sales a day.
- That was the moment he began studying YouTube seriously.
- For several reasons he will cover in the next day's video, he concluded in 2009 that YouTube would be where marketing was headed.

The point: To show viewers that he started with no money, knowledge or connections, so they can do it too, and that his success came from spotting early that YouTube worked; also to keep them watching for the next video.

## Claude, cold, from that brief
When I started my first business with YouTube ads, I didn't have any money. Actually, it was worse than that. I was in debt. And I was in debt to the IRS, which, let me tell you, is the worst kind of debt you can have.

I didn't have investors writing me checks either. Nobody was backing me. So I had to start with almost nothing to spend. I remember this very clearly: my ad spend when I started was $10 a day.

That's it. Ten bucks. Everything got scaled up from there later on. And it wasn't just money I was missing.

I didn't know anything about advertising online. Nothing. I didn't have connections, either. I didn't have any friends who'd done internet marketing.

I didn't have mentors showing me what to do. I learned this stuff by figuring it out on my own, and by watching videos a lot like the one you're watching right now. So first off, congratulations. Sitting here watching this and learning about YouTube marketing is a smart move.

Now, I want to be straight with you about something. I'm not successful because I'm smarter than everybody else. I'm not successful because I work harder than everybody else. The reason I'm financially free, and the reason I'm talking to you right now, is that I had the sense to recognize when I got lucky.

Let me explain what I mean by that. Back in 2009, we were trying a bunch of different marketing channels in my business. We tried Google search. We tried Facebook ads.

We tried a few other things too. And none of it was really taking off. Then one of the videos we'd put up on YouTube hit 10,000 views. I know that doesn't sound like much now, but for us at the time that was a huge deal.

And that video started bringing in sales. One a day, sometimes two a day. That was the moment. That's when I started studying YouTube seriously.

And for a number of reasons, which I'm going to get into in tomorrow's video, I came to a conclusion back in 2009: YouTube was going to be the future of marketing.
