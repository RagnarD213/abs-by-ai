## The content, as a neutral brief
Who: Dan, the course instructor, speaking to paying customers of his Google Ads course in a lesson video about campaign settings.

Points, in order:
- Standard inventory is the stricter option: it keeps ads off sensitive content, such as material with recurring heavy swearing, heavy sexual material, or violent material, though the excluded material stops short of the most graphic violence or sexuality.
- Google's definition of sensitive is tighter than an ordinary person's, so standard inventory cuts reach for no real gain.
- Limited inventory should never be used: it badly cuts ad reach and profitability and, in his view, offers no brand benefit.
- Always select expanded inventory.
- The following setting covered is conversions, meaning the action a campaign is optimized toward; there are a few options.
- Google's default optimizes toward every conversion in the account; never do this, because it performs worse than optimizing toward one conversion.
- He has tested both approaches and found single-conversion campaigns almost always scale better and return better ROI.
- To pick a single conversion, select the option that lets you set the conversion action at the campaign level; his on-screen example campaign is optimized for leads.
- Recommendation for most advertisers: start by optimizing for leads, meaning someone opts in with an email address and possibly a phone number.
- A lead-focused business with no online sales runs lead-optimized campaigns permanently; example: a car dealership that only generates leads for its sales staff would run only that campaign type.

The point: Get viewers to choose expanded inventory, avoid standard and limited, and optimize each campaign for one conversion action (leads to start) rather than all conversions.

## Claude, cold, from that brief
Now, standard inventory is a little bit more strict. So what that's going to do is keep your ads from showing on what Google considers sensitive content. So that would be videos where there's a lot of heavy swearing over and over again, heavy sexual material, violent material, things like that. It's not the most graphic violence or the most graphic sexual stuff, but it's stuff in that direction.

And the problem is that Google's definition of sensitive is a lot tighter than what you or I or any normal person would think of as sensitive. So when you pick standard inventory, all you're really doing is cutting down your reach, and you're not getting anything for it. And then limited inventory, you should never, ever use this. It's going to massively cut down the reach of your ads, it's going to really hurt your profitability, and in my opinion there's no benefit to your brand whatsoever.

So always, always select expanded inventory. Okay, so the next setting we're going to go over is conversions. And what this means is, what's the action that your campaign is being optimized for? And you've got a few options here.

So the default from Google is that it's going to optimize for every single conversion that you have in your account. Never do this. And the reason why is it just doesn't perform as well as optimizing for one conversion. I've tested it both ways, and what I've found is that campaigns optimized for a single conversion almost always scale better and they almost always get you a better ROI.

So to pick one conversion, what you want to do is select this option right here that lets you choose the conversion action at the campaign level. And you can see this campaign is optimized for leads. Now, for most of you guys, what I recommend is that you start out optimizing for leads. So that just means somebody opts in, they give you their email address, maybe their phone number as well.

And if you have a lead-focused business where you don't make any sales online, then you're just going to run lead-optimized campaigns forever. So for example, if you have a car dealership and all you're doing is generating leads for your sales guys, that's the only kind of campaign you're going to run.
