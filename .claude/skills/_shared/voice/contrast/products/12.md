## Claude, cold, from that brief
- Any awards your business has won. If you've made the Inc. 5000 or you've got a strong BBB rating, put it in there.
- Links to any press or media coverage you've appeared in.
- Links to favorable reviews on Amazon and other outside sites where reviews can't be faked.
- Whatever degrees and certifications you hold.

One more thing. If your video has testimonials in it, your description also needs a legal notice. It should say that the testimonials show the best results people got, not average or typical ones, and that you're not promising the viewer those results.

I know, this sounds like a lot of tedious work. But you don't have to retype all of it every time you upload a video.

YouTube lets you save a default description that gets applied automatically to everything you upload. So the only parts you customize for each video are the call to action at the top and the opening paragraph. The rest you type once and never touch again.

It's a small amount of effort, and it could save you from a YouTube strike or a suspended Google Ads account down the road.
