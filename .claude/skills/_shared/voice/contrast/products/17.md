## The content, as a neutral brief
Who: Dan, speaking in a paid course lesson video to his customers (people running Google Ads), on behalf of his agency.

Points, in order:
- Opens by saying this video covers a profit-maximizing campaign strategy called the Exploration-Drilling approach.
- The agency developed it over the last few years; it has performed strongly for raising profit in client accounts, and he expects it to do the same for the viewer.
- Before the strategy itself, he outlines the two main campaign structures available in Google Ads.
- First structure: campaigns built around a single ad group, typically holding just one ad.
- Second structure: campaigns containing lots of ad groups (perhaps 10, 20 or 30), each with several ads under test (perhaps two to four).
- He has reviewed a large number of Google Ads accounts and seen ad managers succeed with each structure, so clearly either can work.
- The agency's own question was which structure is better, since its ad managers were succeeding with each.
- To settle it, they ran a test lasting several months across a number of client accounts.
- The result: neither wins outright; you actually have to use the two structures together.

The point: Set up the lesson by defining the two campaign structures and revealing that the agency's testing showed the most profitable approach uses both rather than picking one.

## Claude, cold, from that brief
Hey, it's Dan, and in this video we're going to be talking about a campaign strategy for maximizing your profits called the Exploration-Drilling approach. Now, this is something that we've developed at our agency over the last few years, and it's worked really, really well for increasing profits in our client accounts, and I think it's going to do the same thing for you. So before I get into the actual strategy, I want to outline the two main campaign structures that you have available to you in Google Ads. The first one is a campaign that's built around a single ad group.

And typically that ad group is just going to have one ad in it. So one campaign, one ad group, one ad. The second structure is a campaign that has lots of ad groups in it. So you might have 10, 20, 30 ad groups in that campaign, and each one of those ad groups has several ads that you're testing.

So maybe two, three, four ads in each one. Now, I've looked at a lot of Google Ads accounts. I mean a lot. And I've seen ad managers be successful with the first structure, and I've seen ad managers be successful with the second structure.

So clearly, either one of these can work. But the question that we had at our agency was, okay, which one's better? Because we had ad managers on our own team who were getting good results with each of them. So to figure that out, we ran a test.

It went on for several months, and we ran it across a number of our client accounts. And what we found is that neither one of them wins outright. You actually have to use the two structures together.
