## The content, as a neutral brief
Who: Dan, the course instructor, speaking to paying students at the end of a lesson video on setting up conversion tracking for Google Ads.

Points, in order:
- Recap of step one: each purchase gets its own conversion, each kind of lead gets its own, and each kind of order-page view gets its own. Set all of that up first.
- Step two: hand the configuration details for Google Tag Manager over to a developer, or, if technically capable, paste them in and configure it oneself.
- Step three: build the test campaign he just demonstrated, used for running a real purchase through a live ad.
- How to build it: a search campaign using a handful of obscure keywords, geo-targeted to within 20 miles of one's home or work address, then carry out a real test purchase through the ad.
- Confirm the correct count and correct value are recorded on at least one mobile phone and one desktop machine, or on however many browsers and devices a QA team can cover, for those fortunate enough to run a larger business that has one.
- When all problems are fixed and everything works, the student is ready to proceed.
- He acknowledges this is complicated, unglamorous, involves code and developer collaboration, and is not the exciting side of advertising on Google and YouTube, but says its importance cannot be overstated.
- Every advanced and creative strategy coming later in the course depends on the data that conversion tracking produces.
- If tracking fails entirely, there is essentially no chance of scaling the account; if it works only 90%, scaling is harder than with 100% of data recorded correctly.
- So give this proper time and run the test transactions; with tracking working, the later strategies become far simpler.
- Start this homework now; he will see them in the following lesson.

The point: Get students to complete the three-step conversion tracking setup and verify it with real test purchases before moving on, because everything later in the course depends on accurate tracking.

## Claude, cold, from that brief
So just to recap, step one is you're going to set up your conversions. Every purchase gets its own conversion, every kind of lead gets its own conversion, and every kind of order page view gets its own conversion. Get all of that set up first. Step two, you're going to take those configuration details for Google Tag Manager and hand them over to your developer.

Or, if you're technical and you know what you're doing, you can paste them in and configure it yourself. And then step three is you're going to build that test campaign that I just showed you, so you can run a real purchase through a live ad. So it's a search campaign, a handful of obscure keywords, geo-targeted to within 20 miles of your home or your work address, and then you actually go through the ad and make a real test purchase. And you want to confirm that the correct count and the correct value are getting recorded on at least one mobile phone and one desktop machine.

Or, if you're lucky enough to have a bigger business with a QA team, then on however many browsers and devices they can cover. And once you've fixed all the problems and everything is working, you're ready to move on. Now, look, I know this is complicated. It's not glamorous.

There's code, you've got to work with a developer, and this is not the exciting side of advertising on Google and YouTube. I get it. But I can't overstate how important this is. Every advanced strategy, every creative strategy that's coming later in this course, all of it depends on the data that your conversion tracking produces.

If your tracking isn't working at all, you have basically no chance of scaling your account. And if it's only working 90%, it's going to be harder to scale than if 100% of your data is getting recorded correctly. So give this the time it deserves. Run the test transactions.

Because once your tracking is working, everything we do later gets so much simpler. So go ahead and get started on this homework now, and I'll see you in the next lesson.
