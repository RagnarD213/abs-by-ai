## The content, as a neutral brief
Who: Dan, speaking to paying customers of his ad campaign management course in a lesson video.

Points, in order:
- Why a spend filter matters: a placement with only $1 of spend can show an inflated ROI simply because one sale landed on almost no ad cost; that is not a truly good result.
- For statistically meaningful data, he filters to placements with over $500 in spend. This is a general guideline; raise or lower it somewhat according to the price of the first product you sell.
- With the filter in place, he sorts by the ratio of total conversion value to cost, in descending order, so the best earners are at the top and the worst at the bottom, then looks at the top entries.
- So he is reviewing placements above $500 spend across the previous 30 days that have proven most profitable.
- Example: a YouTube channel called Amazing Auditions had roughly $1,900 spent and roughly $2,400 returned in that period, an ROI of 1.22.
- His break-even ROI target for the account in question is 1.0, so 1.22 counts as highly profitable.
- Acting on this, he created a fresh ad group aimed straight at that channel, and it turned out to make very good money.
- Caveat: the tactic does not always succeed; sometimes a placement surfaced by the report loses money when targeted directly. But the odds are far better than guessing blindly.
- Instruction: be sure to check the placement report that shows where ads ran, and target a placement directly if it is profitable for you.
- That is only a start. Using the Amazing Auditions example, he could also target audition-related keywords, find and target similar audition and talent-show channels, build custom intent audiences, and look for related topics.
- The report is an excellent source of ideas for growing an account.

The point: Use the placement report, filtered for meaningful spend and sorted by profitability, to find proven winners; target them directly and then use them as a springboard for related keywords, channels, audiences and topics to scale the account.
