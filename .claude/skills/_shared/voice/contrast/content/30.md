## The content, as a neutral brief
Who: Dan, speaking straight to camera in a YouTube Short, addressing people curious about whether the grappling art builds visible abdominal muscles.

Points, in order:
- Opens by posing the question of whether practising jiu jitsu produces abs.
- States his credentials: he holds a black belt in BJJ and has trained for 22 years; many people have put this question to him.
- Says the sport does work the abdominals hard, through two things: the conditioning drills done in class and live wrestling rounds.
- Adds that it may nevertheless be the most difficult route there is to getting abs.
- For people who enjoy the sport, the core training is a welcome extra.
- For people who only want abs and have no interest in wrestling, ab exercises in a gym get results more quickly.
- Reminds viewers that diet accounts for 80% of the result, regardless of whether they practise the sport.
- Says poor eating cannot be overcome by training, and that includes training in this sport.
- Therefore anyone wanting abs should concentrate chiefly on what they eat rather than on their training method.
- Wraps up: those who only want abs should prioritise diet; those who enjoy the sport get additional calorie burn and solid core work as a side benefit.

The point: The sport does train the abs, but it is a hard and slow way to get them; diet matters most, gym ab work is quicker, and the core benefit should be seen as a perk for those who already enjoy the sport.

## Claude, cold, from that brief
Does jiu jitsu get you abs? I'm a black belt in BJJ, I've been training for 22 years, and a lot of people ask me this.

So here's the deal. Yes, jiu jitsu works your abs hard. You've got the conditioning drills you do in class, and you've got live rolling, and both of those really hit your core. But it might be the hardest way there is to get abs.

So if you love jiu jitsu, awesome, the core work is a great bonus. But if you just want abs and you don't care about wrestling anybody, you're gonna get there faster doing ab exercises in the gym.

And remember, your diet is 80% of this, whether you do jiu jitsu or not. You can't out-train a bad diet, and that includes jiu jitsu. So if you want abs, focus mainly on what you're eating, not on how you're training.

Bottom line: if you just want abs, put your diet first. And if you love jiu jitsu, you get some extra calorie burn and solid core work on top of it.
