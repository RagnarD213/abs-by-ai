## The content, as a neutral brief
Who: Dan, speaking to camera to entrepreneurs and general viewers, in a video arguing that entrepreneurs should cancel their health insurance.

Points, in order:
- He concedes up front that his recommendation to go without health cover is not right for everyone. Some people clearly need a policy, and he will go through several reasons for holding one.
- First reason, and the one covering the largest number of people: being employed somewhere that pays a substantial share of the premium.
- Caveat: even when the company pays about half, it may still not pay off for some people.
- For the majority, though, it does pay off when the company contributes substantially, particularly at 60, 70 or 80 percent. That contribution is effectively free money arriving monthly, so anyone with a generous employer contribution should most likely take it and be insured.
- Second reason: no employer coverage but very low earnings. He gives annual incomes of about $10,000, $15,000 or $20,000. Cover is then almost surely worthwhile, because a large subsidy is available through the marketplace set up under the Affordable Care Act.
- Third reason: health risk. This covers people who are already ill, who often have serious medical problems, who smoke tobacco, who drink heavily, who are overweight (obese above all), or who have any pre-existing condition likely to produce high medical bills. They should definitely get insured, and it will probably more than pay for itself.
- Fourth reason: being aged 50 or above. Younger people's payments subsidise older people's premiums, so cover is probably worthwhile.

The point: The advice to drop health cover has exceptions. Viewers should keep or buy a policy if they have a generous employer contribution, a very low income, poor health or risky habits, or are 50 or older.

## Claude, cold, from that brief
Now, I want to be clear about something, because I'm not saying this is right for everybody. It's not. There are definitely people who need health insurance, and I'm going to go through a few reasons why you would want to have it. So the first one, and this is the one that applies to the most people, is if you work at a job and your company pays for a big part of your health insurance.

Now, even if your company's paying, like, half of it, it still might not be worth it for some people. But for most people, if your company's paying a big chunk, and especially if they're paying 60, 70, 80 percent of it, then yeah, it's worth it. That's basically free money they're giving you every month. So if you've got a company that's kicking in a lot on your premium, you should most likely take that and have health insurance.

Second reason. Let's say you don't have a job that covers you, but you don't make very much money. So you're making, like, ten thousand, fifteen thousand, twenty thousand dollars a year. In that case it's almost definitely going to be worth it for you to have health insurance, because you can get a big subsidy through the marketplace, the one that was set up under the Affordable Care Act.

Third reason is your health risk. So if you're already sick. If you're somebody who has serious medical problems a lot. If you smoke cigarettes, if you're a heavy drinker, if you're overweight, and especially if you're obese.

Or if you've got any kind of pre-existing condition that's likely to mean you're going to have big medical bills. If that's you, then you should definitely get health insurance. It's probably going to more than pay for itself. And then the fourth reason is if you're fifty or older.

Because the way it works is the younger people are paying in and that's subsidizing the premiums for the older people. So if you're fifty or above, it's probably going to be worth it for you to have it.
