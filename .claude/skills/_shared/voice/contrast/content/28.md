## The content, as a neutral brief
Who: Dan, a podcast guest, speaking to listeners who are sceptical about buying paid courses or info products, and replying to one person who had raised a worry about being scammed.

Points, in order:
- Contrast with relationships: there you need to find a single good one, and since you only get one, it must be good.
- Products of this type are different. He has bought many hundreds of them.
- He would not call them scams. It has been rare for a seller to take his money and deliver nothing.
- However, roughly 80% of them gave him little value.
- Even if most purchases (about 4 in 5) disappoint or feel like a so-called scam, the remaining 20% that work pay back more than the cost of all the others.
- He urges people to adopt this view: not every purchase will pay off, but you will receive something, and a 20% success rate still makes the spending a worthwhile investment.
- To reassure the person who raised the concern that he is not scamming him now, he offers to send him a book free of charge, with no shipping fee either.
- He repeats that he is not trying to scam anyone.
- He adds an example of disbelief: people struggle to accept that he could type words on a computer and be paid $300 or $500 for an ad script, even though that is a small sum.

The point: Treat buying educational products as a portfolio bet where a few winners cover the many duds, so stop fearing being scammed and be willing to invest; he backs up his own good faith with a free book.
