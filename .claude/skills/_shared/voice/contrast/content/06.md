## The content, as a neutral brief
Who: Dan, talking to camera to YouTube viewers while demonstrating a macro-tracking app on his phone.

Points, in order:
- Choose the app's meal-prep option by tapping it.
- Add photos of the batch: greens first, then confirm the photo; proteins next, get a clear shot, then confirm it.
- Then photograph the olive oil. Spices and vinegar can be skipped because they carry no calories.
- Everything is now captured; more than one photo is allowed.
- The app asks for the number of meals in the batch; he enters 7.
- Next is the step he calls most important: adding extra context.
- Photos alone would partly work, but the app can't tell the exact amount of oil or chicken used. You likely don't eat the whole chicken to the bones, and that matters when tracking batches.
- To add context, he opens the notes field, taps the microphone on his keyboard and dictates a short description.
- What he dictates: 7 salads; every vegetable eaten; 2 eggs in each salad; 80 percent of the chicken eaten; 2 tbsp olive oil in each salad.
- He taps the Analyze Meal button and warns that this takes longer than a normal single-meal analysis because more items are involved.
- The results then appear.

The point: Show viewers how to log a batch of prepped meals in the app, and that describing portions and how much is actually eaten in the notes makes the analysis accurate, since photos alone are not enough.
