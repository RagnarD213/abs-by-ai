## The content, as a neutral brief
Who: Dan, speaking to camera to home-workout viewers, demonstrating an ab exercise.

Points, in order:
- He will demonstrate the no-equipment version, since that is the version most people should use.
- Starting position: lying flat with the body stretched out long, feet and toes on the floor. The movement is a jackknife: fold the body up and bring hands to toes.
- What matters is a strong contraction at the top of the move, and a couple of details make that happen.
- Detail one: lift both head and backside as high from the floor as possible. If they stay down while you reach for your toes, you are only swinging your limbs and the abs are barely working.
- Detail two (he admits it sounds odd): his personal cue is to try to drive his knee into his own face with full force, as if trying to smash his nose.
- Reassurance: you cannot really injure your nose doing this; aiming for that motion simply produces the correct contraction.
- He demonstrates: aiming knee at face raises head and backside a bit higher, which means the abs are doing more work.
- Advanced option: add light weight, such as a 2.5 lb or 5 lb plate, a small dumbbell, or a small medicine ball (he is holding one).
- Weighted version is the same: extend fully so hands and toes reach the floor with the body straight, then crunch up with head and backside lifted at the top.
- Timing: every exercise in the workout is done for 40 seconds.

The point: Teach viewers how to do the jackknife toe touch properly so the abs actually work, by lifting head and backside and aiming knee toward face, with an optional weighted progression, at 40 seconds per exercise.
