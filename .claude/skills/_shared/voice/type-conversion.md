# Type notes: website conversion (sales video scripts and sales letters)

From 18 pairs (12 written scripts and letters, 6 transcripts of him reading his own script). Highest stakes: where
Dan can record a take first, use `/dan-voice` ASSEMBLE instead of writing.

**Written scripts and letters**

- **Block paragraphs of three to five sentences** (10 of 12). Claude gives nearly every sentence its own paragraph:
  27 words per paragraph against his 44.
- **" -- " for an aside and "..." for a pause** (10 of 12). Claude breaks with a full stop or comma instead.
- **Exclamation marks on offers, greetings and sign-offs** (7 of 12): "I'll even cover the shipping for you!"
- **Mid-length to long sentences.** His average is 18 words; Claude's cold draft 11.5. He rarely goes under eight
  words; Claude drops in "Fine." and "Here's the deal." (7 of 12, 6 of 12). 29% of Claude's paragraphs end on a
  six-word punch; his: none.
- **Clauses chained** with ", and", ", but", "because", "since" (7 of 12).
- **"I'll" and "you'll"**, not "I'm going to" ("going to" 1.6 / 6.5 per 1,000 words; "will" 4.0 / 1.4).
- **Plain claims:** "for any reason", not "for any reason at all"; "now", not "right now" (8 and 6 of 12).
- **Unhyphenated compounds and loose ends stay:** "hands on", "easy to watch", "make money with".

**Sales videos he reads (transcripts)**

- Here he does scatter "really", "actually", "just", "definitely" and "obviously" (6 of 6), and he softens an
  outcome with "could", "might", "I think" or "chances are". Claude strips these and states it flat. Keep them at his
  rate: "very" 4.9, "really" 3.9 per 1,000 words.
- The identical phrase several times in a row (5 of 6); a thought run as one long sentence chained with and, or, but.
- No colon before a reveal, no teaser line ("But here's the thing."): he goes in with "So" or "Now".
