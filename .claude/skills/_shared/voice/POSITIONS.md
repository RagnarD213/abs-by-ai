## Part 1: Positions

**Belly fat and spot reduction.** Spot reduction does not exist; fat comes off the whole body evenly. Abs are mostly eating fewer calories: nutrition is 80% and you cannot out-train a bad diet. [Top 10 Tips; The Vacuum; Jiu Jitsu short]

**When not to train abs.** If you cannot see your abs yet (he says 90% of viewers), do no crunches, sit-ups or toe touches. Muscle built under fat pushes the belly out and makes you look fatter. His example: Chuck Liddell. [Top 10 Tips; The Vacuum]

**The vacuum.** The only ab exercise he recommends while you have belly fat: it trains the transverse abdominis and shrinks the waist at rest. Standing, shirtless, in a mirror, fasted, 20-second timed sets. Half the value is being made to look at your stomach daily. Clients with belly fat do it every day; he does it a few times a week, and says he cannot separate its effect from Zepbound, started at the same time. [The Vacuum]

**Ab training once lean.** Train abs every day; they recover faster than big muscles. He does drop sets (a hard, a medium and an easy exercise back to back). Conflict: three drop sets every day [Top 10 Tips] versus three after leg workouts and five after upper-body workouts [Top 10 Tips reshoot]. His top five: ab wheel, Spider-Man plank, cable crunch, V-sit twist, toe touch [Top 5 Ab Exercises short].

**Ab wheel.** $17, and it beats crunches: constant tension, every ab muscle at once. Flat back, locked arms, slow. [The $17 Ab Wheel]

**Deadlifts and high-risk lifts.** He used to preach deadlifts and now does none: no barbell deadlift, barbell squat, flat bench or power clean. Deadlifts injured him more than every other lift combined, and an injury is how men over 40 quit for good. They also build a thick powerlifter look. Instead: T-bar or barbell rows, wide-grip lat pulldowns, leg press, rear delt fly, mostly machines; kettlebell deadlift at home. Standard: zero injuries from lifting. [Stop Deadlifting; Abs At 40 vs 25]

**Train every day, keep it short.** For a beginner, five minutes daily beats an hour twice a week. He admits lab studies say equal volume gives equal results; his answer is that real people skip. Daily means no decision: he trains sick or injured. Add five minutes a month, join a gym at month four, aim for an hour a day within three to five years. You should never be sore. Morning is best, straight after work second, late night worst. He lifts an hour every morning, seven days a week [Work Out Every Day; Abs At 40 vs 25]. Start time conflict: he wakes around 9 [Intermittent Fasting] versus at the gym around 8 [memory].

**Timer or reps.** At home he times rounds on a Gymboss (30 or 40 seconds) because beginners counting reps rush [Work Out Every Day]. At the gym he counts: a warm-up set of 12 to 15, then two working sets of 6 to 10 [memory].

**Home equipment and gyms.** Money is not an excuse. Basic kit, $58: ab wheel $17, jump rope $9, push-up handles $10, yoga mat $22. Add a 35 lb kettlebell (about $45), a medicine ball (about $20) and 25 lb dumbbells (about $29): roughly $150 all in. Buy the cheap versions. Other totals: "$50" [Work Out Every Day], "under $100" [Invest In Health]. He says he gained muscle on it through COVID. Then join the gym with the most ripped people, not the best equipment; he pays for a bodybuilding gym despite owning a $20,000 home gym. [Home Workout Budget; Invest In Health]

**Cardio and jump rope.** Jump rope is the best home cardio. Skip one foot at a time, two minutes on waking or before a workout. On weight loss medication, cardio and walking are not enough: lift. [Jump Rope short; How To Jump Rope 2020; Keep Your Muscle]

**Calories and tracking.** Calories are the number one factor. Track by photographing food with AI. Weigh yourself every morning, vacation included: "if you ain't tracking, you're slacking" (credited to Brandon Carter). Catch two pounds, not twenty. [Intermittent Fasting; Top 10 Tips]

**Intermittent fasting.** His number one method, for about 10 years: skip breakfast, black coffee, first meal about 2 pm, every day, no multi-day fasts. Hunger was nearly gone at 30 days and almost totally at 90. Start by only skipping breakfast, then push lunch back half an hour at a time. Stop eating two hours before bed. Never break a fast with alcohol. He fasts even when not cutting, for productivity. [Intermittent Fasting]

**First meal, carbs, liquid calories.** Break the fast low carb; early carbs breed cravings all day, so carbs go at night. No liquid calories: soda, juice, milk, cream in coffee. [Top 10 Tips]

**His salad.** His most important nutrition habit. Arugula, carrots, grape tomatoes, broccolini, English cucumber, red onion, Costco rotisserie chicken, two hard-boiled eggs. Dressing poured straight on: 2 tablespoons of expensive olive oil, 2 of vinegar, pepper, garlic powder, adobo; never store-bought. About $4 against $20 at the Whole Foods salad bar; 683 calories by the app, about 720 counted by hand. [Daily Salad]

**Protein.** 0.8 g per pound of body weight (200 lb means 160 g); 1 g does no harm, half a gram hurts. Meat at every meal, eaten first, then vegetables, carbs last. Conflict on whey: avoid it and all shakes when leaning out [Top 10 Tips] versus most men should use it [Supplements; Keep Your Muscle]. He takes none: lactose intolerant, uses Thorne aminos.

**Food spending and meal prep.** Buy the best protein you can afford: wild sockeye at about $20 a pound against chicken thighs at about $3. Broke: Costco chicken and vegetables. He uses Clean Eats in Austin every other week, about $120 a week, and cooks the other weeks [Invest In Health]. Memory: about $8 to $9 per meal.

**Zepbound.** Everyone should consider it, ripped men included; he plans to stay on it for life. He says doctors disagree and are wrong: less alcohol, sugar and food outweighs the risk of a small dose. Brand name from LillyDirect, vial and needle (not the pen, not oral), outer thigh, Thursday evening. Step up 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5 mg over a month; never above 2.5 unless obese; step down gradually. Dose conflict: maintaining 2.5 mg [Zepbound Update] versus 1.5 mg a week now, 1 to 1.5 for ripped men maintaining, 2.5 if he starts gaining [memory]. He tried 5 mg "for a week" [Zepbound Update] or "two weeks" [Keep Your Muscle] and backed off. Cost he cites: $400 a month [Invest In Health].

**Zepbound and muscle.** For the average user, 25 to 40% of the weight lost is muscle. Lifting is mandatory (five minutes a day to start), protein comes first at every meal, and falling strength in the gym means lower the dose or raise protein. [Keep Your Muscle]

**Alcohol.** Every drink costs you. Before Zepbound he drank 5 to 10 a week, mostly nearer 10; now one or two when out, and sugar is down 60 to 70%. [Zepbound Update]

**Supplements.** About 5% of results. Choose by AI audit of the research, not influencers. Start with fish oil, vitamin D (he takes 5,000 IU, liquid) and magnesium, then add one a month. Brand: Thorne. Waste: pre-workouts and stimulant fat burners (overpriced caffeine). Conflicts: no creatine because of his stomach [Supplements] versus creatine HCL in his post-gym drink [memory]; at night a Thorne sleep product [Supplements] versus 3 g of glycine nightly [memory].

**Sleep and the Oura ring.** Sleep matters as much as training and food. Buy a tracker: Oura first, Whoop second, never Fitbit; Apple Watch by day only. Ignore the Oura's advice to take it easy after a bad night: train harder, on low-risk exercises. Subscription: "seven a month" [Invest In Health] versus $5.99 a month or $69.99 a year [Oura Review]. Memory: sleeps best at 63 degrees; score 70 to 75 before glycine, 75 to 80 now.

**Spending money on health.** You are not broke, you are spending on the wrong things. Cut bars, restaurants, junk food, vacations, Netflix; put health ahead of the 401k, the college fund and, if you cannot afford a gym, therapy. Middle class: $500 to $1,000 a month on premium protein, a mattress (his is a Purple), a gym, a sleep tracker, a GLP-1, TRT, basic supplements. Earning above $10,000 a month: home gym, meal prep or a chef, a maid. He is not on TRT and plans to start within a year or two. [Invest In Health]

**Trainers versus AI.** AI gives a personalized plan that adjusts daily; human trainers hand everyone the same one. Conflict: human trainers are "completely obsolete" [Ad 3] versus a top 1% trainer at $200 to $400 an hour can beat AI and is worth it for multimillionaires, the lowest return on his list [Invest In Health].

**How he uses AI.** For most decisions: it audited his supplements, rebuilt his workouts, tracks his macros from photos, coaches his sleep from Oura data, and told him to get a spray tan and found the studio. He overrode it once, keeping Athletic Greens for his stomach. [Supplements; First Spray Tan; VSL Outline] Memory: AI saves him at least ten hours a week.

**Motivation and the goal picture.** You already know what to do; you lack motivation. An AI picture of the real you with abs, on your lock screen, beats a vision board or your head pasted on a fitness model. [Top 10 Tips; Ad 1]

**Being 40 plus.** Harder: injuries, recovery, hormones, and above all lifestyle. Easier: discipline, money, experience, technology. Net, he says it is easier now than at 25 if you use the technology. [Abs At 40 vs 25]

**Spray tan and photo shoots.** Cameras flatten definition; a tan adds contrast. Worth it for shoots and events, a lower priority than gym, food, GLP-1 and TRT. For a shoot: golden hour, a pump, fasted. He prefers it to sun, which he says ages you and causes cancer. [First Spray Tan]

**Looks and status.** Looks are not vanity: the halo effect makes people rate you smarter, funnier and more capable; online dating is winner take all, and getting leaner multiplied his matches by 10. [Invest In Health]

**Jiu jitsu.** He is a black belt with 22 years of training. It is a great ab workout and the hardest way to get abs; if you only want abs, gym ab exercises and nutrition are faster. [Jiu Jitsu short]

**Business and YouTube advertising (history).** Paid traffic beats free traffic for a new business; start at $10 a day and reinvest. Test cheap ideas even when you doubt them. Work from home, not an office. [Paid Traffic Rules 2021; Travel Like a Boss (TLAB) podcast 2020]

**College.** A scam unless the degree pays like medicine. Not dropping out of Boston University is his biggest regret; he puts the true cost near $5 million once lost investment returns are counted. [Why College Is A Scam 2021]

**After the sale, and AI.** He started an ad agency [Channel Intro]. In new copy use the sales letter version: an online business where AI coded his website and did his accounting, which led him to try it on his body. [Sales Letter]

**Abs at 40 with AI.** At 200 lb he generated a picture of himself with abs, made it his lock screen and looked at it daily for over a year [Ad 1]. His first ChatGPT plan was generic chicken and broccoli. He then ran five AI models at over $1,000 a month before building the app. [Sales Letter]

**First spray tan.** He uploaded bad shoot photos to AI, which told him to tan. About $80 plus $20 for contouring, ten minutes, eight hours before showering, still good on day five. [First Spray Tan]

**Starting Zepbound.** He first thought it was only for the obese, and changed his mind as data came in and after seeing Kinobody's Greg O'Gallagher [check] use it while lean. Early side effects (gas, indigestion, low appetite) are gone. [Zepbound Update]

**Oura discoveries.** He thought eight hours in bed meant eight hours of sleep. The ring showed that fast late-night drives in his Tesla Model 3 Performance wrecked his sleep, and that his score fell off a cliff during a bad relationship. [Oura Review]
