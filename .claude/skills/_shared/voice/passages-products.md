## 2. Mindset (a claim that sounds backwards, then an everyday analogy) | The Sex God Method, Immersion | 2007 | tier 1 | written | consumer

> When first trying to improve their level of Immersion, many people try replacing these negative thoughts with positive thoughts. Instead of thinking negative things, they think “I’m the man! I can easily rock her world! Really, I am the man!” While these positive thoughts would seem to help your level of sexual confidence, they actually do not. If you’re constantly telling yourself that you’re a bedroom legend, it only emphasizes the fact in your mind that you are not a bedroom legend. Let me explain:
>
> If you woke up and were going to eat cereal in the morning, you would most likely just wake up and eat it without thinking about it. You wouldn’t try to pump your confidence up with positive thoughts like “Yeah, I can totally eat this cereal! I’ve got this! I’m the cereal master!” This would be ridiculous; you know that you can easily eat the cereal, so there’s no need to pump yourself up.
>
> We only think really positive thoughts and psyche ourselves up for something that is hard for us to do. For things that come easily to us, we do them automatically and without thinking about them.

## 5. Teaching (opening a lesson, the mistake most people make) | Black Belt course video, "LTV And Your Advertising Goal" | 2021 | tier 1 | spoken | business

> All right. In today's episode, we are going to talk about customer lifetime value and how to set the right advertising goal. Now, this is going to be a real important one and one that a lot of YouTube advertisers don't pay enough attention to, in my opinion. Now, I know you probably bought this course so that way you can learn tactics to manage your ad account better, to learn better ways to target and manage your campaigns.
>
> And that is important too. But the biggest reason why I've seen that people don't make YouTube profits at scale isn't that they don't know the right tactics because you can learn all that. Actually, the biggest reason why I've seen people not doing well with YouTube advertising is that they don't have the right goal. And let me explain exactly what I mean by that. Now, obviously we all kind of have the same goal in the end, which is to make more money with Google Ads, to grow our businesses, to get more customers.
>
> In that sense, the goal is clear to all of us. But having a goal like that isn't good enough when you're going into Google Ads. I've consulted with many different people with Google Ads accounts. I've seen dozens and dozens of different accounts. And I've noticed one definite trend between the people that are able to scale their accounts and the people who aren't able to scale their accounts.
>
> Whenever I consult with somebody who's spending $100,000 or $200,000 or $500,000 a month in Google Ads, I notice they're able to do that because the goals are extremely clear. The goal will be something like, I'm trying to acquire customers below a $70 CPA. If I am able to acquire customers below a $70 CPA, I scale up that campaign. Or maybe something like, I'm trying to acquire customers at a 1.5x ROAS or return on ad spend. If I'm able to acquire customers above that ROAS, in that case, I would scale my campaigns.
>
> There's always some kind of definite quantitative goal where it's a number. It's not just, I want to make more money or I want to grow my business. There is a definite, definite numerical quantitative goal for when you scale up your campaigns, when you consider things profitable, and when you scale down your campaigns, when you consider yourself to be losing money. So that's the first thing. You need a specific quantitative goal, not just make more money.

## 6. Explanation (answers the reader's objection first) | 15 Steps To Profitable YouTube Marketing, Step 15 | 2020 | tier 2 | written | business

> Why You Should Not Manage Your Own YouTube Ads
>
> It might seem strange that after spending hundreds of pages and dozens of videos teaching you the ins and outs of YouTube marketing, I’m now telling you that you need to hire someone else to run your ads for you.
>
> But if you think about it, you’ll see why this makes sense.
>
> First of all, you need to have a good understanding of YouTube advertising in order to effectively hire and manage someone to do it for you. Most business owners don’t understand the principles behind successful YouTube campaigns, so it’s impossible for them to make a good decision when hiring an agency or employee.
>
> For example, let’s say you don’t know what a Skip Stopper is. You’ll never get good results hiring an agency or in-house employee, because you’ll have no idea how to tell good YouTube ads from shitty ones.
>
> That’s why I recommend everyone who reads this book and get at least a little experience running ads themselves, rather than skipping straight to hiring someone. And it’s why I stressed the importance of doing all the action steps in the previous chapters to heavily.
>
> Now that you know the fundamentals of YouTube marketing, it’ll be easy to hire someone to scale your campaigns up. You’ll know exactly what to look for when you’re hiring, how to evaluate results, and how to improve your campaigns over time.
>
> In the next few pages I’m going to show you how to determine what your personal Core Strength is. This is your unique talent where you can add more value to your business than anyone else can, and it should be where you focus as much of your time as possible.
>
> If YouTube is not your Core Strength, then it makes a lot of sense to outsource or delegate these campaigns as much as you can. Someone who specializes in YouTube advertising exclusively and who is managing YouTube campaigns all day can probably do a much better job than you can. And they’ll be freeing your time up to work on your Core Strength, which is the best way for you to contribute value to your business.
>
> Even if YouTube advertising is your Core Strength, then you’ll still need to hire people or agencies to expand your campaigns beyond a certain point. You can only manage so much yourself, even if you’re spending all day on YouTube marketing.
>
> I’m personally laser-focused on YouTube marketing - but I still used the techniques in this chapter to hire eleven different employees and six different agencies to help me with my YouTube campaigns. So even if you obsess about YouTube ads all day like I do, you still need to know this stuff.
