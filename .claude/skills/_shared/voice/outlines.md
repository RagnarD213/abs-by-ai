## O1. Ad outline: skip stopper, believable promise, mechanism

Source `160O1s3xcUGlVU_BjtZR5u_V2WgE9JSREUftUTPuZQEw` ("Abs By AI ad outlines - batch 1", Ad 1, labelled "- DAN",
2026-08-06). Ad 1 ran in Demand Gen from 2026-09-10; no per-ad CPA on record.

> This picture got me abs.
> And it's not even real!
> I generated this picture back when I was 200 pounds
> I made it my phone lockscreen, and looked at it every day for more than a year
> And this is where I'm at today
>
> If you're not working out at least five days a week and eating healthy now, you don't need more knowledge. You need the motivation to execute what you already know.
>
> More attractive to women, men respect you more, feel better, improved health, live longer

Why: the stopper is a paradox in two short lines. The mechanism is one reframe ("you don't need more knowledge"). The
last line is a note to the writer, not copy: he lists benefits as a comma run, and the writer turns it into speech.

## O2. Ad outline: command stopper, credential that cuts against himself

Same doc, Ad 3, labelled "- DAN". Ad 3 is one of Dan's picks for the trial campaign `24316364155` (2026-10-01).

> Stop wasting your money on personal trainers!
> AI has made human personal trainers completely obsolete. In the next few minutes, I'll show you how you can get better results than any human trainer by using free AI tools
> Listen - I've spent thousands on personal trainers over my lifetime. And I am a personal trainer myself.
> But a few years ago, I had to admit the truth: AI has made personal trainers like me completely obsolete. I'll prove it to you in the next few minutes.

Why: "In the next few minutes, I'll show you" is the promise line he has used since 2019 (see `ads.md`). "Listen -" opens
the credential. "personal trainers like me" makes him the expert who admits he lost.

## O4. Dan's bullet register for an argument

Source `15gg6GP_Huy93ZBfTpbQuUtHGDrgHL-bs6Op-nN2UBr8` (Second Shoot Outlines, STOP Deadlifting). Tier A.

> STOP doing deadlifts!
> I have gotten more injuries from deadlifts than all other lifts combined
> If your goal is to get as strong as possible and you don't care about how you look - or injuries - than deadlift away. But if looks and health matter to you, deadlifts make no sense
