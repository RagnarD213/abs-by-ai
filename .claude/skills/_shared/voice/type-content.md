# Type notes: content (videos, shorts, podcast answers)

From 33 pairs (18 video, 8 business video, 4 shorts, 3 podcast). Numbers are per 1,000 words, his / Claude's cold draft.

**Videos, off the cuff (transcript).** Claude's cold draft is a costume of casual speech; his real speech is plainer.

- Fragments for punch ("None.", "Like, way too much."): Claude in 16 of 18 pairs, him almost never. Say it in the sentence.
- "And" or "And then" opening sentence after sentence: Claude in 14 of 18. He starts on the subject more often. About
  28% of his sentences open with And, So, But, Now or Because; Claude's cold drafts run at 50%. Do not go under 15% either.
- Tag questions ("right?", "okay?"): 13 of 18 for Claude, never him.
- "I've got / you've got": he says "I have", "you have". "got" 1.3 / 7.4. "gonna" 0.6 / 5.9; "going to" 9.4 / 4.1.
- "Like," as filler: Claude 11 of 18. He uses "like" for comparisons and examples only.
- "really" 2.3 / 4.3, "actually" 0.9 / 2.8, "stuff" 0.6 / 2.6, "so" 11.6 / 18.7. "very" 1.3 / 0.5 and "things" 1.6 / 0.7
  are his: Claude underuses them. "need to" 2.1 / 0.5 and "will" 2.6 / 0.7 also his.
- His vocabulary turns clinical where Claude turns slangy: "caloric", "consumed", "excessive", "significant".
- He repeats three-word runs twice as often as Claude (8.1 against 3.7 repeated runs per 100 words): the same phrase
  comes back whole.
- His sentences average 16 words and swing wide; Claude's cold drafts average 12.5 and 21% of them are 5 words or
  fewer (his: 7%).

**Shorts (he writes these).** One-sentence hook on its own line. Then short plain sentences that each start a new
instruction with And, But, Or or Then, where Claude chains them with commas. "very", "should" and "you have" are his;
"really", "actually", "honestly", "gonna", "you've got", "here's the deal" and "Bottom line:" are Claude's. He uses the
plain formal verb: "accelerate", "reduce", "concentrate".

**Podcast and business videos.** Long sentences joined with ", and", ", so", ", but" and "that". He frames a
lesson with "one thing I've learned is that" and "I realized that". No "honestly", no "dude", no "look," before a
point, no echo sentence after a claim ("It just is.").

**Scripts for the teleprompter** are not transcripts: his words and bluntness, in order, with line breaks (CORE, last
section).
