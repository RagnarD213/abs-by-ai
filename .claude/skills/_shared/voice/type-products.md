# Type notes: products (what the customer gets: his books and course lessons)

From 20 pairs (10 from The Sex God Method, 3 from the business book, 7 course lessons). Numbers are per 1,000 words,
his / Claude's cold draft.

**Books (written)**

- **Solid paragraphs of four to eight sentences** (10 of 10). Claude chops the same material into short paragraphs,
  often one sentence each: 37 words per paragraph against his 63.
- **Sentences start on the instruction or on a formal connector**: "However,", "Also,", "In addition,", "On the
  other hand". Not on a bare And, But or So (9 of 10). 4% of his sentences open with one; Claude's cold draft: 17%.
- **Full forms, not contractions** (8 of 10): "will not", "is not", "she will", "cannot". Contractions 1.4 per 100
  words against Claude's 4.1. "will" 11.1 / 4.9.
- **Claim and reason in one sentence, joined by " -- "** (7 of 10): "This takes time -- ..." Claude writes two
  sentences.
- **Straight into the instruction.** No "One last thing", "Here's why", "Look at it this way" (7 of 10).
- **Mid-length, even sentences; the contrast sits inside the sentence.** No five-word punch line (6 of 10).
- **Bookish words where Claude goes plain:** "obtain", "comply", "alleviate", "squander", "excessive". The book is his
  most formal register. He writes "girl" and "girls".
- **He repeats.** Repeated three-word runs: 5.1 per 100 words against Claude's 1.0. The key term comes back whole,
  capitalised if it is one of his named ideas.

**Course lessons (transcripts)**

- No stand-alone emphatic sentence of two to five words ("That's just noise."): the point stays in the longer
  sentence (6 of 7).
- "So" is for wrapping up. Claude opens a third to a half of its sentences with it ("so" 13.0 / 21.3); 33% of his
  sentences open with And, So, But, Now or Because against Claude's 62%.
- The reason is joined on mid-sentence with "because", "but" or "and"; he rarely starts a new sentence with "And".
- Bare labels for list items and section changes: "Next targeting option you have to know about", not "Okay, so the
  next targeting option we have is".
- "will" and "'ll" or a bare imperative for instructions ("will" 3.7 / 0.4; "going to" 4.4 / 11.0).
- A single "really", "very" or "much". Never "really, really" or "way, way".
- "Now," plus the statement. No "look", "I mean", "I get it".
