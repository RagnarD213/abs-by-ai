# Receipts 02 to 11: study sources

Checked 2026-10-21 against the PubMed record (abstract via NCBI E-utilities) and, where open, the full text (PMC / Europe PMC).
Every number on a slide comes from the lines quoted here. kg are kept as the paper reports them (no lb conversions).

## receipts-02-metabolism

- **Citation:** Pontzer H, Yamada Y, Sagayama H, et al. (IAEA DLW Database Consortium). Daily energy expenditure through the human life course. *Science*. 2021;373(6556):808-812. doi:10.1126/science.abe5017. PMID 34385400.
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34385400/ (full text PMC8370708)
- **Design:** pooled doubly labeled water database; n = 6,421 (64% female), 29 countries, ages 8 days to 95 years.
- **Result used:** total expenditure, adjusted for fat-free mass, stable from 20 to 60; declines about 0.7% a year after 60.
- **Source lines:** "using a large (n = 6,421; 64% female), geographically and economical diverse (n = 29 countries) database ... for subjects aged eight days to 95 years". Abstract: adjusted expenditure "remains stable in adulthood (20 to 60 years), even during pregnancy; then declines in older adults." Full text: "Adults (20 to 60 y): Total and basal expenditure and fat free mass were all stable from age 20 to 60"; "Adjusted total expenditure declined by -0.7 ± 0.1% per year".
- **Slide wording:** "Down 0.7% a year" after 60; note says the figures are adjusted for body size and muscle.

## receipts-03-protein-per-meal

- **Citation:** Trommelen J, van Lieshout GAA, Nyakayiru J, et al. The anabolic response to protein ingestion during recovery from exercise has no upper limit in magnitude and duration in vivo in humans. *Cell Reports Medicine*. 2023;4(12):101324. doi:10.1016/j.xcrm.2023.101324. PMID 38118410.
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38118410/ (full text PMC10772463)
- **Design:** randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel groups; 36 healthy recreationally active men aged 18 to 40 (n = 12 per group); a 60-minute whole-body resistance session, then 0 g, 25 g or 100 g of milk protein; 12-hour tracer measurement.
- **Result used:** 100 g gave a greater and longer (>12 h) anabolic response than 25 g; protein ingestion had negligible impact on amino acid oxidation; larger doses take longer to digest.
- **Source lines:** "we show that the ingestion of 100 g protein results in a greater and more prolonged (>12 h) anabolic response when compared to the ingestion of 25 g protein." "Protein ingestion has a negligible impact on whole-body protein breakdown rates or amino acid oxidation rates." Methods: "A total of 36 healthy, recreationally active young men". Results: myofibrillar synthesis "100PRO > 25PRO > 0PRO" over 12 h; "the ingestion of larger amounts of protein requires a more prolonged period to allow full digestion".
- **Caveat (not on slides):** young men after a whole-body workout; the authors say they do not know if it extends to other groups.

## receipts-04-light-weights

- **Citation:** Morton RW, Oikawa SY, Wavell CG, et al. Neither load nor systemic hormones determine resistance training-mediated hypertrophy or strength gains in resistance-trained young men. *Journal of Applied Physiology*. 2016;121(1):129-138. doi:10.1152/japplphysiol.00154.2016. PMID 27174923.
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27174923/ (full text PMC4967245)
- **Design:** RCT, 49 resistance-trained men (23 ± 1 yr), 12 weeks whole-body training; higher-rep group about 30-50% 1RM for 20-25 reps (n = 24) vs lower-rep group about 75-90% 1RM for 8-12 reps (n = 25); all sets to volitional failure.
- **Result used:** lean mass and muscle fiber size rose with no significant difference between groups; strength rose in both, only bench press differed (heavy 14 kg vs light 9 kg).
- **Source lines:** "Fat- and bone-free (lean) body mass and type I and type II muscle fiber cross-sectional area increased following training (P < 0.01) with no significant differences between groups." "1RM strength increased for all exercises in both groups (P < 0.01), with only the change in bench press being significantly different between groups (HR, 9 ± 1, vs. LR, 14 ± 1 kg, P = 0.012)."

## receipts-05-too-old (replaces the fasted cardio candidate)

- **Citation:** Fiatarone MA, Marks EC, Ryan ND, Meredith CN, Lipsitz LA, Evans WJ. High-intensity strength training in nonagenarians. Effects on skeletal muscle. *JAMA*. 1990;263(22):3029-3034. PMID 2342214.
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2342214/
- **Design:** 10 frail nursing home residents, age 90 ± 1 (up to 96), 8 weeks of high-intensity resistance training; single group, no control group; 9 completed.
- **Result used:** strength +174%, midthigh muscle area +9.0%, tandem gait speed +48%.
- **Source lines:** "Ten frail, institutionalized volunteers aged 90 +/- 1 years undertook 8 weeks of high-intensity resistance training." "Strength gains averaged 174% +/- 31% (mean +/- SEM) in the 9 subjects who completed training. Midthigh muscle area increased 9.0% +/- 4.5%. Mean tandem gait speed improved 48% after training." "...among frail residents of nursing homes up to 96 years of age."

## receipts-06-sleep

- **Citation:** Nedeltcheva AV, Kilkus JM, Imperial J, Schoeller DA, Penev PD. Insufficient sleep undermines dietary efforts to reduce adiposity. *Annals of Internal Medicine*. 2010;153(7):435-441. doi:10.7326/0003-4819-153-7-201010050-00006. PMID 20921542.
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20921542/ (full text PMC2951287)
- **Design:** randomized 2-period crossover; 10 overweight adults (3 women, 7 men), mean age 41 (SD 5); 14 days of moderate calorie restriction with 8.5 or 5.5 hours of sleep opportunity.
- **Result used:** fat lost 1.4 kg (8.5 h) vs 0.6 kg (5.5 h); fat-free mass lost 1.5 kg vs 2.4 kg; more hunger with short sleep.
- **Source lines:** "Sleep curtailment decreased the proportion of weight lost as fat by 55% (1.4 vs. 0.6 kg with 8.5 vs. 5.5 hours of sleep opportunity, respectively; P = 0.043) and increased the loss of fat-free body mass by 60% (1.5 vs. 2.4 kg; P = 0.002). This was accompanied by ... increased hunger".
- **Slide wording:** "less than half the fat lost" (0.6 / 1.4 = 43%). "Lean mass" = the paper's fat-free mass.

## receipts-07-six-meals

- **Citation:** Cameron JD, Cyr MJ, Doucet E. Increased meal frequency does not promote greater weight loss in subjects who were prescribed an 8-week equi-energetic energy-restricted diet. *British Journal of Nutrition*. 2010;103(8):1098-1101. doi:10.1017/S0007114509992984. PMID 19943985.
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19943985/
- **Design:** randomized; 3 meals + 3 snacks a day vs 3 meals a day, same energy restriction (-2931 kJ/d) for 8 weeks; 16 obese adults completed (8 women, 8 men).
- **Result used:** no significant differences in adiposity, appetite or gut peptides between groups.
- **Source lines:** "Sixteen obese adults (n 8 women and 8 men ...) completed the study." "there were NS differences between the low- and high-MF groups for adiposity indices, appetite measurements or gut peptides (peptide YY and ghrelin)". "We conclude that increasing MF does not promote greater body weight loss".

## receipts-08-breakfast

- **Citation:** Dhurandhar EJ, Dawson J, Alcorn A, et al. The effectiveness of breakfast recommendations on weight loss: a randomized controlled trial. *American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*. 2014;100(2):507-513. doi:10.3945/ajcn.114.089573. PMID 24898236.
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24898236/ (full text PMC4095657)
- **Design:** multisite, 16-week, 3-arm RCT; 309 overweight/obese adults aged 20-65 randomized (283 completed); told to eat breakfast, skip breakfast, or control.
- **Result used:** no significant effect of the advice on weight loss; adjusted mean changes -0.53 to -0.76 kg in every group.
- **Source lines:** "A total of 309 participants were randomly assigned." "Treatment assignment did not have a significant effect on weight loss". Adjusted changes: skippers -0.71, -0.76, -0.61 kg; eaters -0.53, -0.59, -0.71 kg. "this had no discernable effect on weight loss in free-living adults who were attempting to lose weight."

## receipts-09-protein-kidneys

- **Citation:** Antonio J, Ellerbroek A, Silver T, Vargas L, Tamayo A, Buehn R, Peacock CA. A high protein diet has no harmful effects: a one-year crossover study in resistance-trained males. *Journal of Nutrition and Metabolism*. 2016;2016:9104792. doi:10.1155/2016/9104792. PMID 27807480.
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27807480/ (full text PMC5078648)
- **Design:** randomized crossover over 1 year; 14 healthy resistance-trained men (26.3 ± 3.9 yr); about 6 months normal diet (2.51 g/kg/d protein) and 6 months high protein (3.32 g/kg/d); fasted blood panel on five occasions (BUN, creatinine, eGFR, liver enzymes, lipids).
- **Result used:** no harmful effects on blood lipids, liver or kidney function; no significant differences in the panel between diets.
- **Source lines:** "Fourteen healthy resistance-trained men completed the study". "This significantly increased ... during the high protein phase to 34.37 ± 5.88 kcals/kg/day and 3.32 ± 0.87 g/kg/day of protein." "there were no harmful effects on measures of blood lipids as well as liver and kidney function." Methods: blood drawn "on five separate occasions". Table 4: "There were no significant differences between groups."
- **Slide wording:** "3.3 g" is 3.32 rounded. Verdict says these were healthy men.

## receipts-10-diet-soda

- **Citation:** Peters JC, Wyatt HR, Foster GD, et al. The effects of water and non-nutritive sweetened beverages on weight loss during a 12-week weight loss treatment program. *Obesity*. 2014;22(6):1415-1421. doi:10.1002/oby.20737. PMID 24862170.
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24862170/
- **Design:** randomized equivalence trial, 303 men and women, all in the same 12-week behavioral weight loss program, water vs non-nutritive sweetened (diet) beverages.
- **Result used:** diet beverage group lost 5.95 kg vs 4.09 kg for water (P < 0.0001); reported greater reductions in hunger.
- **Source lines:** "a prospective randomized trial among 303 men and women". "the NNS beverage treatment group losing significantly more weight compared to the water group (5.95 kg versus 4.09 kg; P < 0.0001) after 12 weeks. Participants in the NNS beverage group reported significantly greater reductions in subjective feelings of hunger".
- **Funding:** American Beverage Association (Temple University release and later coverage; not in the abstract; the full text is paywalled and was not read). Slide 2 states it. News summaries quote 5.5 vs 3.8 kg (likely a different analysis set); the slides use the abstract's figures.

## receipts-11-carbs-at-night

- **Citation:** Sofer S, Eliraz A, Kaplan S, Voet H, Fink G, Kima T, Madar Z. Greater weight loss and hormonal changes after 6 months diet with carbohydrates eaten mostly at dinner. *Obesity*. 2011;19(10):2006-2014. doi:10.1038/oby.2011.48. PMID 21475137.
- **Link:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21475137/
- **Design:** randomized; 78 police officers with BMI > 30; 6 months; low-calorie diet with carbohydrates eaten mostly at dinner vs a control weight loss diet.
- **Result used (direction only, no kg figures):** greater weight loss, greater drop in abdominal circumference and body fat mass, and lower hunger scores in the dinner-carb group.
- **Source lines:** "Seventy-eight police officers (BMI >30) were randomly assigned to experimental (carbohydrates eaten mostly at dinner) or control weight loss diets for 6 months." "Greater weight loss, abdominal circumference, and body fat mass reductions were observed in the experimental diet in comparison to controls. Hunger scores were lower".
- **Not verified, kept off slides:** the full text (Wiley) was blocked. Secondary sources give 11.6 vs 9.06 kg and 63 completers, plus equal calories (1,300-1,500 kcal) in both arms from the trial registry (ISRCTN37829376). None of these are on the slides.

## Dropped candidate

- **Fasted cardio (Schoenfeld et al., JISSN, 2014, PMID 25429252).** Accurate null result, but it tested 20 young women (about 22 years old) for 4 weeks, and the raw numbers lean the wrong way on a slide: fat mass fell 1.1 kg fasted vs 0.7 kg fed (Table 2), not significant. Replaced by Fiatarone 1990 (too old to build muscle), which fits men over 40 better.
