I got abs for the first time in my 20s. In my 30s, I got fat and lost them. Now that I'm 40, I got my abs back. Getting abs at 40 is harder in three ways and easier in four. Overall, I think it's easier than at 25 because of technology, if you use it. The rest of this article is what to change.

## What's Harder At 40

- **Injuries.** At 25, you get hurt less doing high risk moves like deadlifts, squats, and flat bench press, and you recover faster and better. At 40, you're more likely to get injured, especially on power cleans and deadlifts, and recovery takes a lot longer. Injuries are the number one reason guys in their 40s, 50s, and 60s quit lifting for good. Not willpower. Not tough times. They got hurt, didn't train the right way to recover, training became painful and not fun, and they quit.
- **Hormones.** Testosterone is lower in your 40s and 50s, and so are other hormones that matter for building muscle and losing fat. TRT can address this, but it's still a challenge.
- **Lifestyle.** This is the biggest one, and most guys underestimate it. It matters more than hormones or injury recovery. At 40, you probably have a serious career, maybe a family, a serious relationship, responsibilities, stress. You're busy. At 25, you had much more time and focus for getting in shape.

## What's Easier At 40

- **Discipline.** At 25, if you were like me or most 20-year-olds, you were probably binge drinking and not doing anything consistently, not even showing up to a job. By your 40s, you've had to become disciplined. You show up to work on time, take it seriously, have family responsibilities. Apply that same discipline to fitness.
- **Money.** You probably have a lot more of it, and intelligently spent, it's a big, big advantage. Weight loss medication, a meal prep service, TRT, a maid to clean your house so you have time for the gym.
- **Experience.** You've probably been in shape and out of shape at least once, so you know you can do it. Do what you did before, adjusted for your age.
- **Technology.** The biggest advantage. In our 20s, there was no AI. You couldn't see a picture of your goal. Training programs were made by human trainers. You couldn't instantly answer hard fitness questions or have your supplement stack audited in a science-based way. You had to weigh your food and count calories by hand instead of taking a photo. No weight loss medication, and TRT was at a much earlier stage.

Because of technology, I think it's easier overall. But you need a different approach. Here are the six changes.


## Change 1: Stick To Low-Risk Exercises

Every exercise has a certain benefit and a certain risk. Deadlifts can build a huge amount of muscle, but they also have a high risk of injury. A cable lat pulldown has very low risk, but admittedly, it will not build as much muscle right away. The older you get, the worse that trade gets.

For this reason, I have totally eliminated every high-risk move. I never deadlift. I never do a barbell squat. I never do flat bench press. And of course, no power cleans, no clean and jerk, no powerlifting.

Now, in the short term, if you stay injury-free, the high-risk moves build more muscle. But in my experience, you eventually get injured. That injury sets you back, and over six months or a year, you gain less. In your 20s, you might get away with it. At 40, you won't. And every injury is a risk that you quit permanently.

With my routine, I train with zero injuries from weight training. That is the standard to aspire to. An injury in your 40s or 50s is not part of the process. It's you doing it wrong. So avoid the high-risk moves and do mostly machines, where it's much harder to injure yourself. More on this in [my top 10 tips for getting abs at 40](/videos/my-top-10-tips-for-getting-six-pack-abs-at-40-years-old/).

This is important in your 40s, even more in your 50s, and critical in your 60s. I want to be in great shape in my 70s, 80s, and 90s, and that's not going to happen with power cleans. So I'm also preparing for the future.

## Change 2: Shorter Workouts, More Often

Ideally, you're doing a short workout every single day. Recovery is much harder after 40. At 20, you could blast a leg day and still train a couple days later. In my experience, guys in their 40s and 50s who get wrecked by a leg day often don't come back. Even if you don't quit, your next workout is worse when you're sore.

Your first half hour lifting, you're strong. By the second, you're dragging. By two hours, it's probably counterproductive and you're risking injury. But you still got to get in the volume. The solution is training every day, or every weekday, or as often as you can.

Think you can't do that in your 50s? Start with five-minute bodyweight workouts. You can do that even if you haven't trained in years, with minimal soreness. Once that's easy, step it up to 10 minutes, then 15. If you're getting sore, you're doing it wrong. Train right and you should never be sore.

## Change 3: Take Your Sleep Seriously

Sleep gets harder as we age. Responsibilities make us wake up earlier or go to bed later. A partner can disrupt our sleep. And physically, it gets harder to get deep sleep and REM sleep. In your 20s, you could get by on a few hours and stay in decent shape. Not now. You have to take sleep as seriously as your workouts and nutrition.

The first step: buy an Oura ring or another sleep tracker. Get a Whoop, or just wear your Apple Watch to bed. I was in denial. I thought eight hours in bed meant eight hours of sleep. But [8 hours in bed is not 8 hours of sleep](/videos/8-hours-in-bed-is-not-8-hours-of-sleep/). For me it was about seven, sometimes six and a half. And deep sleep and REM matter too, not just hours. Not tracking this is like trying to get rich but never looking at your bank account, or trying to lose weight but never looking at the scale.


## Change 4: Start Intermittent Fasting

At 40, the eating problem is lifestyle. If you have a family, dinner at home probably isn't built for abs, and you're around kids eating hot dogs and candy.

So my clients in their 40s and 50s have had the most success with intermittent fasting: no food during the day, most calories at night. I fast until 2 p.m., eat a salad, a light low carb meal, then put most of my calories at dinner. It's [why I skip breakfast every single day](/videos/why-i-skip-breakfast-every-single-day/).

Two reasons this works for older men. During the work week you're in get-shit-done mode, and fasting is easier then than at home around family and friends. And it improves your productivity. The first couple of weeks are tough and you'll get less done, but your digestion adapts. I've done this for years, I'm not hungry during the day, and I'm more productive. Even when I don't need to lose fat, I still fast all day. Struggling to hit your calories and lose weight? Try it.

## Change 5: Use Today's Medicine

At 25, there was no Zepbound, no weight loss medication. TRT was primitive. You probably couldn't afford a DEXA scan to know your body fat percentage. Now you have the money.

If you're overweight, weight loss medication can tremendously help. So can TRT. Go deeper: get blood work, find which vitamins and minerals you're low in, it might surprise you, and supplement those specifically.

## Change 6: Lean Into AI

I believe AI is the transformative technology of our generation, so use it aggressively. Even without my app, do these:

1. **Track your calories.** Before AI this was extremely hard for most people. Now you take a picture of your food.
2. **Audit your supplements.** If you're like me in my 20s, you're taking a lot of bullshit, wasting money or taking something unsafe or that hurts your results. AI does a science-based audit: what to drop, what to add.
3. **Answer the difficult questions.** Don't tolerate creatine monohydrate? Ask about creatine HCL or micronized creatine. Lactose intolerant like me? Ask what replaces whey. Total body workouts or body part splits? When to go to bed and wake up, and how much sleep? Should you start TRT or weight loss medication?

In your 20s, you listened to some bro on YouTube give his opinion. Now you can get a science-based, researched answer to any fitness question.

## It's Easier At 40

Yes, your hormones are lower, you get injured more, you recover slower, and worst of all, your lifestyle is harder. But I think it's easier to get abs at 40 than at 25 if you do it intelligently. Weight loss medication, TRT, AI answering your questions and measuring your macros do a lot of the work. If it's been a struggle, you're probably not using technology the right way.
