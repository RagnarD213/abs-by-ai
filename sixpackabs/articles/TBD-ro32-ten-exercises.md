I was fat at 38. Now I have six pack abs at 41. Here are the 10 exercises I do every week to get here. In this article I'm going to show you why I do these 10 exercises every week and how to do each one right.

One thing I want to be clear about first: I do all 10 of these over the course of a week. I don't do all 10 in one workout. Each workout is five exercises. Over the week, those five-exercise workouts add up to these 10.

## How My Workouts Work

Before we get into the specific exercises, here's how my workouts work.

I train first thing in the morning, seven days a week. Every workout is total body. I do 15 minutes of cardio, then one leg exercise, three upper body exercises, and one ab exercise. That's five exercises per workout.

I do three sets of each exercise, so I'm doing 15 sets all together per workout. I start with one light warm-up set of 12 to 15 reps. Then I move into two working sets of 6 to 10 reps with the most weight that I can lift.

That's the whole structure. It's simple. Now let's get into the exercises.

## Abs

Let's start with my favorite ab exercise.

**Number one is cable crunches.** This is how I built my abs. Weight on your abs makes them grow, and bigger abs show through sooner.

Kneel facing the cable machine with a rope behind your ears. Crunch your ribs down towards your hips. Don't sit back with your hips. Stick your butt out like you're twerking, and focus on crunching down with your abs. Squeeze for a second at the bottom, then come up slowly.

In my opinion, regular crunches and sit-ups are the most overrated ab exercises there are. That's why they're not on this list.

## Legs

Now for legs. I do one leg exercise every workout, and I rotate between these three. So you're not doing all three leg exercises in the same workout. You pick one each time.

**Exercise number two is the leg press,** for your quads and your glutes. Put your feet shoulder width apart in the middle of the platform. Lower the weight slowly until your knees are bent to 90 degrees. Then push back up through your heels. Don't lock out your knees at the top. Keep your lower back flat against the pad.

**Number three is the hamstring curl** on the hamstring curl machine. These work your hamstrings, the muscles on the back of your thighs. Line your knees up with the hinge of the machine. Curl the weight as far as it goes and squeeze for a second. Then let it back slowly until your legs are straight.

**Number four is the glute bridge machine,** for your glutes. Put your upper back against the pad with a belt across your hips. Keep your feet flat on the platform, shoulder width apart. Push your hips up until your body is in a straight line from your shoulders to your knees. Squeeze your glutes at the top, and then lower slowly.

## Shoulders

Next up is shoulders.

**Exercise number five is side laterals.** These build shoulder width, and wide shoulders make your waist look smaller. That's the V-taper. It's what makes a guy look athletic, even with his shirt on. I break this one down more in my video on [side lateral raises](/videos/make-your-waist-look-smaller-with-side-lateral-raises/).

Use light dumbbells and a slight bend in your elbows. Raise them up to the side, up to shoulder height, and no higher. Then lower them slowly.

Most guys go way too heavy on these and swing the weight up. Don't do that. Go lighter and slower and you'll feel your shoulders working right away. Do side laterals every other day to start building that V-taper look.

## Back

Now let's talk about your back.

**Exercise number six is pull-ups.** These build your lats, the muscles that make your back wide. Start with your arms slightly bent. Pull your chest up to the bar and drive your elbows down to your sides. Use a slow, controlled motion.

No kipping like they do in CrossFit. That is a great recipe for injury. You want to stay slow and strict like a bodybuilder. If you can't do clean pull-ups yet, do lat pulldowns instead.

**Number seven is T-bar rows,** for a thick back. With a barbell, hinge at your hips with your back flat. Pull the weight to your lower chest and squeeze your shoulder blades together.

You can also use a T-bar row machine. That makes it easy to lie flat, so you're lifting the weight at the right angle. Either way, don't jerk the weight up with your lower back. If you have to do that, the weight is too heavy.

## Chest and Arms

Last group is chest and arms.

**Number eight is chest flies.** In my opinion, these are safer and more effective than bench press and other pushing exercises. That's my personal preference, and that's why flies are on my list instead of bench.

Lock in a slight bend in your elbows the whole time. Don't let that bend change. Open your arms wide until you feel a stretch, and then squeeze your hands together. I do these on a machine, but you can do flies with dumbbells as well.

**Number nine is cable curls,** for your biceps. Pin your elbows to your sides and squeeze at the top. Lower the weight slowly. Extend your arms all the way straight at the bottom, and get them curled up as much as you can at the top of each rep. Full range of motion, every rep.

**Number ten is tricep pushdowns** on the cable machine. Pin your elbows and push down for a full lockout. Don't lean over the bar.

## The Recap

So let's recap the 10 exercises I do every week.

- **For abs:** cable crunches.
- **For legs:** the leg press, hamstring curls, and the glute bridge machine.
- **For wide shoulders:** side laterals.
- **For a wide, thick back:** pull-ups and T-bar rows.
- **For chest and arms:** chest flies, cable curls, and tricep pushdowns.

And remember, that's the full list for the week, not one workout. Every workout is 15 minutes of cardio and then five exercises: one leg, three upper body, one ab. Three sets each, one light warm-up set and two heavy working sets. Over the week, it all adds up to these 10.

Do these every week and you'll build visible abs and a V-taper without wasting a minute in the gym.
