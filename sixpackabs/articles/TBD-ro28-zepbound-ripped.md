## Losing weight does more than change how you look

First, let's talk about why losing excess weight matters in the first place. A lot of guys think about losing fat because they want to see their abs. I care about that too. Looking better is a benefit, and it's a benefit I wanted.

But losing excess fat can also make a huge difference for your health. In the video, I discuss research on weight loss and diabetes risk, and a long study that followed people with severe obesity. Those are reasons to take weight loss seriously beyond what you see in the mirror.

Now, a lot of guys think they're at a normal weight just because they're thinner than their friends. But when most of the people around you are overweight, being better than average isn't the same as being at your ideal weight.

That's the point I want you to think about. Comparing yourself to your friends doesn't tell you whether you still have excess weight to lose. For me, getting lean was worth doing for my appearance, and the health side made the decision matter even more.

## The research that got my attention

Now, let's start with diabetes. Tirzepatide is the drug in Zepbound. The [SURMOUNT-1 diabetes prevention study](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2410819) studied people with obesity and prediabetes, and found a substantial reduction in the risk of developing type 2 diabetes.

That's a different kind of result from just seeing a smaller number on the scale. It's one of the reasons I became interested in what this medication might do beyond helping someone look leaner.

Next is sleep apnea. That's when you stop breathing over and over in your sleep. In the video, I discuss research in people with obesity and sleep apnea that found major improvements in those breathing interruptions. I also discuss heart failure research in people with obesity, and heart research in people with type 2 diabetes and heart disease.

So when I look at this research, I'm looking at more than how much weight people lost. I'm looking at diabetes, sleep apnea and heart health. Those are the outcomes that made me want to understand these drugs better.

I also discuss research on semaglutide, the drug in Ozempic. What interested me was the suggestion that weight loss wasn't the whole explanation for the heart benefit. That helped convince me there may be more going on than these drugs just making people smaller.

## Why I'm choosing to continue

Now, when I put those results together, they change how I think about staying on Zepbound. I find the research convincing enough to influence my own decision.

My opinion is that there may be value in continuing even after I've lost the weight I wanted to lose. I don't want to wait another ten years for a study on ripped guys before deciding what I want to do myself. That's why I plan to stay on a low maintenance dose for the long term.

There's also a much more immediate reason: I want to keep the weight off. Getting to the weight you want and staying at that weight are two different things. I don't want to get lean and then give back the result.

## Stopping can mean regaining the weight

The [SURMOUNT-4 weight maintenance trial](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2812936) is a big part of how I think about maintenance. After losing weight, some participants continued tirzepatide and others were switched to a placebo. The people who stopped had substantial regain. The people who continued maintained or reduced their weight.

That matters to me because a lot of people think the medication is something you take until you've reached your goal, and then you're finished with it. But reaching the goal doesn't mean the medication has stopped helping you maintain it.

In the video, I said I'd been on my low maintenance dose for a month and hadn't regained any weight. I'm interested in keeping that result, rather than stopping just because I can already see my abs.

## Taking medication isn't cheating

A lot of people think that taking Zepbound for life is cheating, or that you're supposed to suffer to lose weight. I don't see it that way.

Nobody calls it cheating when someone takes blood pressure medication for the rest of their life. Nobody calls it cheating when someone takes statins to help prevent a heart attack. So I don't think using medication is something you should feel guilty about.
