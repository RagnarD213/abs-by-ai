You need to be spending more money on your health. You're being too cheap with your health, and your cheapness and wasting money on bullshit is literally killing you. Stop that right now, no matter how little money you have.

In the last video, I told you why. Being in shape makes you more money and it makes you more productive, and bad health is ludicrously expensive. Here's exactly what to cut so you can spend more on your health.

## The "I'm broke" excuse

I know a lot of y'all guys are thinking, well, damn, that's easy for you to say, you have money. I'm a brokie. I don't have any money, so there's nothing I can do.

But that is wrong. You do have the money. You're just spending it on the wrong things.

One way to find this money is to make a little bit more money. That option is open to you. But even if I accept your premise that you can't make any more, you could just cut bullshit and have more than enough money to buy everything you need to maximize your fitness.

## 3 things you should never cut

1. **Healthy food.** The food that you eat is the number one most important factor in getting in shape. You should be spending the most that you can afford on delicious healthy food.
2. **Your mattress.** Your sleep is going to be important (and [8 hours in bed is not 8 hours of sleep](/videos/8-hours-in-bed-is-not-8-hours-of-sleep/)), so anything that affects your sleep deserves your money as well.
3. **The other necessities of a regular life,** like rent. I'm not talking about rent on a luxury crib. Rent on something basic. A car payment on a very basic car that gets you from point A to point B. Utilities, electricity, the things you need for your life.

Everything else is on the table, and we're going to sacrifice it if necessary to get the money for our health.

## What to sacrifice

The easiest and best thing to sacrifice is spending money on things that make you fat. That kills two birds with one stone. You cut the spending that's fattening you and put that money into something that makes you leaner and more ripped.

That's where we start, and I know y'all guys have a good amount of this stuff in your spending right now.

## 1. Bars and clubs

The number one thing to cut out is going to bars and clubs. You're saying, oh, Dan, I don't have the money for a personal trainer or a nutritionist. But you're still spending $100, $200, $300 per week going to the club or going to the bar or just buying alcohol.

You say you need some entertainment. You don't. I'm not telling you to give up the bar or the club forever. But if you can't afford to do that and spend the money you need to on your fitness, then you're not really going to get very far in life. Redirect that money into your fitness, and within a month or two you're going to be in much better shape and more productive. And I think you're going to figure out a way to make some extra money, so this isn't a permanent sacrifice.

They're number one because it's three bad things: alcohol, usually in a sugary beverage, a ridiculous overpriced fee, and staying up late at night.

Also cut buying alcohol for your house. You won't get as much back as from sacrificing those $20 drinks in the club, but it could still be significant. Some people I know are buying a 30 rack of beer every week. Sacrifice that for multiple reasons.

## 2. Eating at restaurants

A lot of people I know say, I don't have the money for a gym membership, Dan. But you look at their credit card statement and they're eating out at restaurants two or three times per week.

They say, I just need a treat for myself every once in a while. But you don't need a treat.

Restaurants don't care about your long-term fitness goals, only about making you happy in the moment. They want a delicious meal and a five-star Google review. They care about how it tastes, not its nutritional value. So restaurant food is usually loaded with much more butter, unhealthy fats, sugar and unhealthy carbs than you would ever load up your own food with at home.

We can combat this somewhat with Abs By AI. If you do make the mistake of going to a restaurant, you [take a picture of it with your phone](/videos/you-dont-need-a-food-scale-anymore-just-a-photo/) and track the calories with an estimate. But it's only an estimate, not as good as knowing all the ingredients yourself. And there aren't as many good tasting, low calorie options, because restaurants are meant for normal people.

Sacrifice restaurants for just a few weeks and that frees up hundreds of dollars to reinvest in your health. You'll start making more money, and within a few months you'll be able to go back every now and then and get a healthy option. It's a very short-term sacrifice.

## 3. Junk food in the house

Next, the junk food in your house. The chips, the candy, the soda. A lot of people could get $100, $200 per week back just from sacrificing bullshit food.

This probably hits your waistline even more directly than restaurants and bars and clubs. You're only going out maybe once, twice, three times a week. But you're at home eating food like this every day.

So even beyond the money, clear it out of your house. No more junk food until you're spending all the money you can on your health.

## 4. Vacations

The final thing to sacrifice is vacation. I know some of you aren't going to like hearing this. You're like, I need a vacation, I deserve a break because I work hard. But you don't deserve a break. You deserve a break once you're ripped.

On vacation, the usual rules of working out and eating go out the window for most people. I've established a good routine where I work out every morning and [fast until 2pm every day](/videos/why-i-skip-breakfast-every-single-day/). Even so, I usually gain 2 or 3 pounds of fat for each week I'm on vacation. Before I had this routine, when I didn't work out and I ate pancakes for breakfast every morning I would gain 5 or 6 pounds of fat per week of vacation.

So before you establish good health habits, vacation is toxic to your fitness goals. You can enjoy vacations eventually, and they'll be way more enjoyable when you're ripped and your health habits are dialed in.

Sacrificing just one vacation a year frees up $5,000, $10,000, $15,000 to invest in your health. Invest that kind of money and you're going to get tremendous results.

## 5. Therapy and psychiatric medication

This one is a little bit controversial. I'm not a psychologist. This is not medical advice. This is my opinion. And in my opinion, if you don't have enough money for a gym membership, you have no business spending money on therapy or psychiatric medication.

I'm not even claiming that stuff does you no good. That's a whole different discussion. The question is, is it doing you more good than a gym membership if you're broke? I don't think it is. If your health isn't there, no amount of therapy and no amount of medication is ever going to give you good mental health. If you have great physical health, getting to great mental health will be much easier, even if you have to sacrifice the therapy and the medication.

I'm not telling you to sacrifice it permanently. Just until you get in great shape and you have the productivity to make more money. Then you can reinvest in it.

Things that make you fat, psychiatric medication, junk food, going to the club: these are the obvious things to sacrifice. Sacrifice them to the maximum extent you can, even if you're rich. And if you're broke, there's no excuse for you to have any of this in your life.

## Subscriptions, car payments and clothes

If you don't have the money for fitness, a few other things should be on the table too. Your subscriptions, your Netflix. Reducing your car payment. No more money on clothing until you're ripped, and no expensive dates until you're ripped.

It's temporary, just a few months, and you'll be so glad you did.
