If you're struggling to lose weight, there is usually one reason: you're eating more calories than you think. Carbs, insulin, seed oils and meal timing all get argued about, but for most people the answer is much simpler. Eat fewer calories than you do right now, and the weight comes off.

The proof is stronger than you'd expect, so I'll show you the real studies first. Then I'll give you 8 ways to eat less without being hungry all day.

## The studies that prove it

In 2010, a nutrition professor at Kansas State named Mark Haub ate Twinkies, Doritos and Oreos for 10 weeks, but he kept himself under 1,800 calories a day. He lost 27 pounds, and his cholesterol improved. The junk food did not stop him from losing weight, because the calories were low.

In 2018, Stanford put 609 people on either a low-carb diet or a low-fat diet for a full year. Both groups lost about the same amount of weight, because both groups ended up eating fewer calories. And when the National Institutes of Health looked at 32 studies where scientists controlled every bite people ate, low-carb and low-fat burned body fat at almost exactly the same rate.

Now, you might be saying: "Dan, I barely eat anything and I still can't lose weight." They studied people like that too. In 1992, researchers took people who swore they were eating 1,200 calories a day. When they measured it, those people were eating 47% more than they thought, and their metabolisms were completely normal. They weren't lying. It is really hard to know how much you eat when you're guessing.

## 2. Know your calorie number

Telling someone to eat less means nothing if he doesn't know how much he's supposed to eat. You need a daily calorie target. The easiest way is to let an AI calorie tracker set it from your height, weight and goal.

For a rough starting point, take the body weight you want to be at and multiply it by 12. If you want to weigh 180, that's 2,100 calories a day. Then weigh yourself every morning and watch the average for the week. If the average isn't dropping, lower your number a little and keep going.

## 4. Fast until 2 PM

There's nothing magic about intermittent fasting. You are giving yourself fewer hours in the day to eat, so you eat fewer calories without really trying.

I don't eat anything until 2 in the afternoon. Then I break my fast with a big salad and eat dinner in the evening. Say your number is 2,000 calories. If you eat breakfast, a snack and lunch, you've spent about 1,200 of them by the afternoon, so dinner has to be tiny and you go to bed hungry. When you fast until 2, you still have most of your calories left for dinner. You can sit down with your family, eat a real meal, and still be under your number. ## 5. Drink black coffee while you fast

Caffeine takes the edge off your appetite for a few hours. I drink 2 to 4 cups of black coffee every morning, and most days I'm not even thinking about food until 2 o'clock. The effect in studies is pretty small, but for me it is the difference between an easy fast and a miserable one.

It has to be black. Cream and sugar can add 200 calories to a cup, and that breaks your fast. If you can't stand it black, give it a week. You'll get used to it faster than you think.

## 6. Start every meal with a salad

Start every meal with a big serving of something low in calories. Researchers at Penn State gave people a big, low-calorie salad before their meal, then let them eat as much of the main course as they wanted. They ate about 12% fewer calories for the whole meal, even counting the salad, and they didn't feel any less full.

It works because your stomach gets full based on how much food is in it, and it has no idea how many calories that food has. Fill it with 3 cups of salad for 100 calories and there is less room left for the pasta. Vegetables or a broth-based soup work too. Eat that first, then your real meal.

## 7. Snack on protein, not carbs

Protein keeps you full for hours, and carb snacks do the opposite. Chips, crackers and granola bars are designed so that you keep eating them. Keep protein snacks around instead: beef jerky, sardines, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese or deli turkey. Most are 100 to 150 calories with 15 to 20 grams of protein. It's very hard to overeat sardines or cottage cheese. You get full and you stop.

## 8. Stop drinking liquid calories

Researchers at Purdue gave people 450 extra calories a day for four weeks as jelly beans, and for another four weeks as soda. When they ate the jelly beans, they ate less food the rest of the day to make up for it, and they didn't gain weight. When they drank the soda, they didn't eat any less at all. They just added it on top, and they gained weight.

Your body doesn't count liquid calories. That means soda, juice, sweet tea, the coffee drinks at Starbucks and beer. A lot of guys drink 500 to 1,000 calories a day and none of it fills them up. If it has calories, don't drink it. Drink water, black coffee and zero-calorie drinks. For a lot of you, this one change is enough to start losing belly fat by itself.

## The short version

Every study says the same thing: if you want to lose belly fat, you have to eat fewer calories. Know your number, track it with AI, fast until 2, and drink black coffee to make it easy. Start your meals with a salad, snack on protein and don't drink your calories. A GLP-1 medication makes all of it far easier.
