AI can estimate your macros from a photo, but you'll get a much more accurate number if you add a sentence or two describing the food. Say what's in the dish, estimate the portions, and show anything the camera can't see.

## Where photo-only estimates go wrong

Take a bowl of chicken soup with rice. It's hard for AI to estimate exactly from a picture, for a few reasons:

- There are a bunch of ingredients that aren't obvious from the top.
- The foods with most of the calories, like the rice and the chicken, sit on the bottom.
- The camera can't tell how much of them is down there.

AI can still give you a rough number from the picture alone. It's just guessing at what's under the broth.

## Add a short description

Take the picture, then briefly describe it. For the soup, I'd write something like:

"I'm eating a bowl of chicken soup. It's about two cups of broth. On the bottom I have chicken, rice and a little bit of vegetables. I'd estimate about four ounces of chicken and five ounces of rice."

Just estimate as closely as you can. You don't need a scale for this. Your rough guess fills in exactly the part the camera can't see.

## Show a spoonful

If you can, take a photo with a spoonful lifted out of the bowl. That shows what's on the bottom. Do these things and AI gets reasonably accurate even for complicated dishes like stews and soups, where it isn't obvious what's inside.

## Photograph the label when there is one

Most of my meals come from a meal prep service, and every meal has the nutrition facts printed right on it. For those, I don't photograph the food. I photograph the label. If you eat the exact portion the label refers to, those macros are exact.

That way the photo estimates only cover the rest: restaurant meals and anything off the meal prep. It won't be perfect, but it's reasonably accurate and far, far better than not tracking at all.

## The quick checklist

1. Photograph the food, or the nutrition label if it has one.
2. Add one or two sentences: what's in it and roughly how much.
3. For soups, stews and bowls, show a spoonful of what's on the bottom.
