Glycine is the one amino acid that has helped my sleep more than any other supplement I've tried. And I've tried dozens of sleep supplements over the years. Most of them did nothing much for me. So if you wake up at night hot or sweating, glycine is worth trying.

Here's what it did for me. I stopped waking up hot in the middle of the night. And my Oura sleep score went from about 70 to 75 to usually 75 to 80.

## What glycine is

Glycine is not a sedative. It's not a medication. It's not a sleeping pill. It's just an amino acid.

About a third of the collagen in your skin and joints is glycine. You already eat a couple of grams of it a day in your food. So this is not some weird chemical. It's something your body already has a lot of.

What you buy is a little white powder that tastes a little sweet. You mix it with water and you drink it before bed. That's it.

## How it's supposed to help you sleep

In order to fall asleep, your body has to lower its core temperature. That's the reason why it's so hard to sleep in a hot room. Your body is trying to cool down and it can't.

One possible explanation involves how we shed heat before bed. When more blood reaches the skin of your hands and feet, heat can escape there. Researchers have looked at whether glycine affects that process. That's the idea that interested me as someone who keeps throwing the covers off. The human sleep studies tell us more about how people slept than they tell us about why.

## What the research actually shows

There were studies done in Japan where they gave people 3 grams of glycine before bed. The people fell asleep faster. They rated their sleep quality higher. And they were less sleepy the next day.

That sounds great. But let me give you some perspective. These were small studies with a small number of people. And they were paid for by a company that sells glycine.

In [one study](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1479-8425.2007.00262.x), just eleven people took part. They already had complaints about their sleep. That matters if you're deciding how much stock to put in the result. A few better nights in a lab are interesting. I still wanted to know what would happen at home, in the bedroom I actually sleep in.

## Why it interested me as a hot sleeper

If you asked me what kept waking me up, I could tell you exactly. I'd be sweating, I'd kick the covers off and I'd wish I could turn down the AC a few more degrees. That's a pretty specific problem to pay attention to when you're trying something new. I could wake up in the morning and remember whether it had happened again.

If you wake up cold, my experience will probably tell you less about your own sleep. And the research I looked at didn't separate the hot sleepers from everyone else. That's my reason for trying it, rather than a finding about who benefits most.

## What happened when I took it

I took 3 grams an hour before bed. That's the same dose they used in the studies.

I wear an Oura Ring every single night. The first thing I noticed was that my temperature reading came down after I started taking glycine.

Now this is important, so I want to be really honest about it. The Oura Ring does not measure your core temperature. It reads the skin temperature on your finger, and it shows you how far each night is above or below your own normal. So what I saw was a trend on my ring. It was not a measured drop in my core temp.

So why did this matter to me? I used to wake up at night a lot because I was too hot. I sleep best at around 63 degrees. But I live in Texas, and in the Texas heat my AC only gets down to about 67 at night.

And when someone is sleeping next to me, they don't want it that low anyway. So we compromise at around 66 or 67. Even in the winter, when the AC could go colder, we keep it there.

Without glycine, I'd frequently wake up sweating or too hot. With glycine, that doesn't happen anymore.

It's not a huge effect. But it's a real effect. And for about 10 cents a night, it's worth it.

As for my sleep score, it was about 70 to 75 before, and it's usually 75 to 80 now. That's my result on my tracker. It's one guy, not a study. I can't promise you're going to get the same thing.

## What did more for my sleep

I track my sleep every night so I can see what makes the biggest difference. And glycine helped. But it's not a cure-all.

What time I go to bed, how much alcohol I drink, and my routine before bed all matter a lot more than glycine or any other supplement. If you want to understand why, check out my video on why [8 hours in bed is not 8 hours of sleep](/videos/8-hours-in-bed-is-not-8-hours-of-sleep/).

Supplements are about 5% of your results. I go into this more in my video on why [your supplements are only a small part of your results](/videos/your-supplements-are-only-3-of-your-results/). So handle the big things first. Once the big things are handled, glycine is how you get a little piece of that 5%.

## How to take it

1. Buy the powder, not the capsules. Capsules are 1 gram or less each, so you'd be taking a handful every night. The powder is also a lot cheaper.
2. I measured out 3 grams of powder and mixed it into water about an hour before bed.
3. Mix it with electrolyte powder for flavor. That's what I do.
4. Do it every night for two weeks and watch your sleep tracker.
5. If you see no difference after two weeks, stop taking it. If your numbers go up, you've got a new tool.

For me, glycine made a real difference. I think it's worth trying once you have the basics of your sleep handled.
