All right, this is my home arm workout. It hits your deltoids, biceps, and your triceps, and you can do it at home with just a couple dumbbells. One round takes two minutes. I usually do three rounds through, so it takes me about six minutes at home.

## The workout

1. Bicep curls, 30 seconds
2. Side lateral raises, 30 seconds
3. Overhead tricep extensions, 30 seconds
4. Rest, 30 seconds

If you just want a quick pump, one round is good. If you're looking for a quick home workout, then do three or four rounds. That will be optimal.

Like with most home workouts, I recommend timed sets rather than counting reps. That's going to encourage greater time under tension, which is the more important factor than reps in building muscle.

The timer that I use is the Gymboss. They don't pay me anything. The app is free.

## Why these three muscles

So, first of all, your deltoids. This is one of the muscle groups that men neglect the most. The reason you need large shoulders is that they're going to make your waist look smaller. For me, for example, I have a pretty thick waist relative to my height, and if I didn't have bigger shoulders, I wouldn't look that good. So shoulders are important not just because they look good directly, but because they make your abs and your waist look better.

Biceps, you guys probably aren't neglecting that one. That's probably the least neglected muscle group. But if you're genetically deficient in the biceps like I am, it's going to be important to be hitting these regularly with isolation work, assuming you're already getting in your big compound lifts.

What you're probably not hitting enough of is your triceps. Your triceps are the majority of your arm size. It's kind of like your quads in your legs compared to your hamstrings. Because of that, if you want big arms, big triceps are paramount.

## Exercise 1: bicep curls

Let's start with the bicep curls. For these I pick up my medium dumbbells. For me, I use about 30 pounds.

So, there's a couple different ways you can do these. First, you can do them one arm at a time. Hold them at your sides and curl up, twisting your wrist as you go. Your palm faces your side at the bottom, and it faces up at the top.

The second way is both arms at the same time. I do this as well at the gym when I'm counting reps. The advantage is that it's quicker and your two sides stay even. The disadvantage is you'll get a little bit less of a range of motion, and some people who are just starting off find it harder to keep good form this way. Try it out both ways. Whichever way works the best for you is perfectly valid. More on that in [one arm or both arms](/videos/how-to-do-bicep-curls-one-arm-or-both-arms/).

The most common mistake I see with the bicep curls is using a weight that's too heavy and swinging it. If you're swinging, you're not really working your biceps. Stand straight up and down, bicep only. No swinging, no rocking, no momentum.

## Exercise 2: side lateral raises

For this one I pick up my light dumbbells. I'm doing 15 pounds. Most of you guys should be doing 5 or 10.

With the side laterals, you want to keep a bend in your arms, a little more open than 90 degrees. I don't want them locked straight out. I don't want them tucked all the way in.

So, common mistakes. The first one, like all isolation exercises, is the swinging. Guys will get a weight that's too heavy and swing it. That's not what you want to do.

The next mistake is when guys go to the top, they don't get their elbows high. The weights come up but the elbows stay low. If your elbow is not high, then your deltoid is not being worked. So focus on your elbow. Elbow high, and no rocking.

## Exercise 3: overhead tricep extensions

All right, the final exercise is your overhead tricep extensions. For this one you take one dumbbell with both hands. Your thumbs are on one side of the handle and all your fingers are on the other side. Put the dumbbell on your chest, get your hands ready, and lift it above your head. Keep your elbows tucked in tight. Go down behind your head and then press back up.

Now, the most important safety consideration here is that this is a high risk exercise if you don't do it right. The mistake I see people make is they go down too far and too close to their neck, and they spike their vertebrae with the dumbbell. If you do that with a real weight at full speed, this could seriously injure you.

So in order to combat that, don't hold the dumbbell directly over your head and lower it onto your neck. Hold it a few inches back, so there's already tension on the triceps. Then go down back and away from your neck.

This is the type of exercise that's going to take a few times doing it before you can do it with full weight safely. So do it with baby weight a couple times. Just learn the form and you'll be doing full weight soon enough.

Now, I also see the opposite mistake. A lot of people are so afraid to spike their vertebrae that they get a little baby range of motion, and that's not going to be effective. What you want is all the way up, locked out at the top, and then down as far as you safely can, with the weight behind you and away from your neck.
