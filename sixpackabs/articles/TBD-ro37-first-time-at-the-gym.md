If I walked into a gym for the first time today, I wouldn't bench press, I wouldn't squat, and I wouldn't do a two hour workout. I'd work out for 15 minutes a day, every day, on three machines. Here's exactly what I would do instead.

## Why listen to me

I've been going to the gym religiously for more than 20 years. I've also trained dozens of beginners. I know what guys like you are going through, and I know what works to get someone off to a strong start and what doesn't.

## Rule 1: Only work out for 15 minutes

Not an hour, not two hours. Just 15 minutes.

Now, I know that probably sounds way too short. Most guys think they need to be in there for an hour or two, just killing themselves, in order to lose belly fat. But 15 minute workouts done every day beat hour long workouts done twice a week. Here's why.

First, you won't get sore, even if you do them every day. You're not going to wake up the next day so sore you can barely walk. So nothing stops you from coming back tomorrow.

Second, you won't dread the next workout. An hour in the gym feels like a chore. You're sitting at work thinking about it, and you're already looking for a reason not to go. 15 minutes doesn't feel like a chore. 15 minutes is easy.

Third, you can actually do 15 minutes every day. Even on your busiest day, you can find 15 minutes. But an hour twice a week is the plan that gets skipped. Something comes up at work, or you're tired, or you get home late, and all of a sudden you haven't been to the gym in two weeks.

You need to train your mind just like you train your body. You wouldn't go to the gym your first day and try to bench press 300 pounds. That's a recipe for getting injured and quitting. It works the same way for your ability to psychologically endure a workout. Train your mind to get comfortable with 15 minute workouts, then gradually step it up to 20, then 25, then 30. If you try going an hour and a half your first time in, in my experience training beginners, that's a recipe for failure.

## What if 15 minutes is a struggle

Maybe you had a terrible day. Maybe you're just not feeling it. Then do five minutes. Do 10 minutes. That's fine.

But never, ever allow yourself to miss a day. Once you start skipping days, it gets a lot easier to skip the next one, and the one after that. Whatever happens, [find the time](/videos/how-to-make-time-to-work-out/) to get your workout in, even if it's only five minutes. Five minutes still keeps the habit going. Zero minutes breaks it.

## Your first workout

1. Get on the bike and ride for five minutes to warm up.
2. Go to three machines: the leg press, the chest press machine and the seated cable rows.
3. On each machine, do one easy warm-up set of 10 to 15 reps.
4. Then do two working sets of 8 to 12 reps.
5. On every rep, take two to three seconds on the way down, and then explode up.

That's your whole workout, and it takes about 15 minutes. Then come back tomorrow and do it again.

## Same three exercises every day

Now you might be thinking, wait a minute, the same exercises every day? Yes.

Is that ideal physically? No. But right now, keeping it simple is more important than doing the perfect workout. These three exercises give you an effective total body workout in just a few minutes, and it's easy to get started.

When you do the same three machines every day, you're not constantly learning new exercises and new machines. You don't have to figure out how to adjust the seat on some machine you've never used before. You don't have to look up how to do an exercise on your phone in the middle of a workout. There's nothing to figure out and nothing to slow you down. You just walk in, do your 15 minutes, and go home.

Once they get boring and easy, you can switch to other exercises. But the key is staying consistent. Keep it real simple, and keep it real short.

## Weeks 2 to 4

Add a second workout and alternate between the two. You still do the same five minutes on the bike to warm up.

Workout A is the workout I just gave you: the leg press, the chest press machine and the seated cable rows. Workout B is lat pulldowns, the shoulder press machine and cable crunches. Day one, you do workout A. Day two, workout B. Day three, workout A again, and so on.

It's still only about 15 minutes. You're not adding time. You're just adding a little bit of variety.

Once the habit is locked in, you can start gradually training harder and longer. But if your 15 minutes is still a struggle, don't add more. I know that sounds crazy when you want to lose belly fat. But locking in the habit of working out every day is far more important than working out longer. If you can't get yourself to do 15 minutes a day, you're definitely not going to do an hour every day.

## Why this works when most beginners quit

Most beginners drastically overestimate the importance of long, hard, complex workouts. And they drastically underestimate the importance of training consistently every single day.

Most beginners walk in, do a long workout and go straight to the heavy barbells. Then they switch programs every week because they saw some new workout online. And what happens? They get sore, they get overwhelmed, and they quit. I've seen it happen over and over again.

The guy who does a crazy two hour workout on his first day usually isn't in the gym three weeks later. But the guy who does 15 minutes every day is still there, and he's the one who ends up losing his belly fat.

In my experience, about 80% of beginners who start a workout routine the traditional way quit within six months. When they use the short daily workout approach, the majority of them stick with it long term.

## Machines and cables only

Machines and cables are the lowest risk, simplest way to start. You can add barbells and dumbbells later if you want to, but you don't need them to get started. I've been lifting for 20 years and I still never do high risk barbell lifts like [deadlifts](/videos/why-i-stopped-deadlifting-at-40-do-these-4-exercises-instead/). Never.

Here's why this matters so much when you're new. An injury when you're first getting started is probably going to be the end of your fitness journey. I'm just keeping it real with you. That's the most common reason people quit or take a long time off. You get hurt, you're out for a few weeks, and the habit you just built is gone. And a lot of guys never come back. So when you're new, the safest exercises are the right exercises.

Before you do an exercise for the first time, watch a few tutorials on YouTube. Don't just read the label on the machine. I've got short how-to videos on my channel for every machine in this plan: the leg press, the chest press machine, seated cable rows, lat pulldowns, the shoulder press machine and cable crunches. Watch those before you go in.

## If you're self-conscious

Nobody is watching you. Everybody at the gym is self-conscious, just like you are. You're focused on how people see you, and everyone else is thinking the same thing about themselves, so they don't have any attention left to notice you.

And walk in with a plan, so you're never standing around wondering what to do. You already have one. It's the plan in this article.

## Walk between sets, stretch after

Get up and walk around between every set. It keeps you warm. It raises your step count, so you burn more fat. And you'll perform better on your next set.

Then stretch after every workout, not before. After your workout you're warm, and that's when you actually get more flexible. Stretching before, when you're still cold, doesn't do that much for you.

## The whole plan

- Every day, 15 minutes.
- Alternate workout A and workout B.
- Machines and cables only.
- Walk between your sets.
- Stretch after.

Keep it real simple and keep it real short. Don't worry about going hard, just worry about showing up. Get into the gym, do your 15 minutes, and then do it again tomorrow.
