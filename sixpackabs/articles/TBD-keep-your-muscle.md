If you want to lose fat without losing the muscle you worked to build, you have to keep resistance training, prioritize protein, and track your strength as well as your body weight. A lower number on the scale is not the whole goal. You want to like the physique you have when the fat comes off.

Weight loss medication has changed my life. But a suppressed appetite creates a problem if it means you stop eating enough protein or stop giving your muscles a reason to stay. In this video, I share my own Zepbound experience and the habits I use to keep muscle while losing fat.

## Why I care about keeping muscle

I do not want to lose weight, discover that I lost muscle too, then spend months bulking to rebuild what I already had. That puts me back into the cycle of gaining weight and cutting it again. I would rather preserve as much muscle as I can while the fat comes off.

This matters beyond how you look shirtless. Losing strength makes everyday tasks harder. For me, a successful cut means getting leaner while keeping the muscle and strength that make me feel capable.

That is why I do not judge progress by the scale alone. If my weight is falling but every lift is getting weaker, I want to know about it while I still have time to respond.

## What happened when my appetite became too suppressed

In the film, I explain what happened when I increased my Zepbound dose. I had more side effects, found it difficult to hit my protein, and struggled to eat enough. Within a few weeks, my strength was dropping. That was the warning sign I paid attention to.

My takeaway from that experience is that more appetite suppression was not automatically better for the physique I wanted. It made eating the food I needed more difficult. I discuss my dosing experience because it explains why I put so much emphasis on protein and tracking.

The medication sections are my personal experience. They are not a dosing plan for you. In the video, I explain that my approach differs from the studied dosing schedule and is not suitable for most users. Talk with your prescribing doctor before changing how you take your medication.

## Plan for what happens after weight loss

I also talk about coming off medication. One thing I have seen is that people can lose weight successfully, then regain it when their appetite returns. Being able to enjoy all your favorite foods again feels good, but it can make maintaining your weight harder.

The point is to think about maintenance before you arrive there. Keep recording your body weight. Pay attention to whether your eating habits still work when your appetite changes. Discuss medication decisions with your doctor instead of treating the end of a cut as the end of the process.

The film also covers the injection methods I discuss in relation to my own approach. That is another part of the medication conversation to have with your prescriber. The everyday habits below are the part of my routine I want you to understand clearly.

## Keep resistance training in your routine

Walking, cardio and sports can be good exercise. But in this video, I emphasize resistance training because keeping muscle is the goal. You need to keep using the muscles you want to preserve.

I train in the gym, but a beginner does not need to copy my full routine to get started. My suggestion in the film is to begin with five minutes of home bodyweight resistance training, then increase the work as you progress. The useful part is making it a habit you actually do.

Five minutes is a starting point, not a guarantee that you will never lose muscle. As you get stronger and more experienced, the training needs to progress too. Keep a record so you can tell whether you are improving or just repeating the same easy work.

If you want to see a practical home session, my [dumbbell arm workout](/videos/home-arm-workout-shoulders-biceps-triceps-with-just-dumbbells-do-it-with-me/) shows the kind of resistance training I teach. Choose a routine you can perform consistently and build from there.

## Put protein first at every meal

When your appetite is low, the order you eat your food matters. My simple habit is to eat the protein first, then the vegetables, then the carbs. That way, if I become full before I finish the plate, I have already eaten the protein.

In the video, I use chicken, eggs and steak as examples. I want a substantial protein portion with the meal, rather than a little protein beside a large serving of everything else. The goal is to make protein a deliberate part of the day when eating less feels easy.

I also explain why I do not personally rely on whey shakes and bars: I am lactose intolerant, and they cause digestive problems for me. If you tolerate them, they can be a convenient way to add protein. Convenience matters if it helps you follow through.

I give a 200-pound example in the film: at 0.8 grams of protein per pound, that works out to 160 grams a day. The reason for the example is to make the amount concrete. Think about how the protein portions across your meals add up, rather than assuming that eating some chicken at dinner covers the whole day.

## Track your lifts, not just your weight

You do not need to record every detail of your life. The basics I talk about are your body weight and the weight and reps you perform in your sets.

If your body weight looks good but your lifts keep dropping, that deserves attention. Are you eating enough protein? Has eating enough become difficult? Those are the questions my own experience made me ask. Medication questions belong with your doctor.

I show how to record weight and sets in Abs By AI. You can also use the notes app on your phone. The tool matters less than having a record you can compare. Without one, it is easy to let a problem continue for weeks before you notice the pattern.

## Start with the next meal and the next workout

Put protein first at your next meal. Keep resistance training in your routine. Record your body weight, then write down the weight and reps from your next workout. Those are the habits I keep coming back to because they help me see whether I am losing fat while holding on to what I want to keep.
