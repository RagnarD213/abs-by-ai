You don't have belly fat because you lack knowledge. You have belly fat because you don't have the time to work out and eat right. If you had time to work out every day and meal prep every week, you'd be in shape.

But you're busy. You have a job, maybe you have kids, and finding the time feels impossible. You can find it without sacrificing anything. Here are six ways to do it, and if you use all six, I'd say most of you will get back 10 hours a week or more.

## 1. Install Wispr Flow and stop typing

This is the easiest one, and it only takes a couple of minutes to set up. You only need five minutes a day to start working out, and this app alone will save you at least 20 minutes a day.

Wispr Flow is an AI dictation app. You hold down a button, you talk, and it types out what you said into whatever app you're using: your texts, your emails, your prompts to ChatGPT, everything.

I know your phone already has voice to text, and it's a piece of crap. Wispr Flow is nothing like that. It uses AI to figure out what you meant to say, so it takes out the ums and puts in the punctuation for you. If you change your mind halfway through a sentence ("make me a reservation at 6, no, make it 7"), it just writes the corrected version.

The average person types about 40 words a minute on a phone, but you talk at about 150 words a minute. So everything you write gets done three to four times faster. There's a free version, the paid version is $15 a month, and it works on your phone and your computer. I use it for almost everything I write.

Most people type for at least 30 minutes a day between texting, email and work. Do that three or four times faster and you just got 20 minutes of your day back. That's more than enough time for a great workout.

## 2. Work out at home, not at a gym

Going to the gym means packing a bag, driving there, changing, working out and driving back. For most people that's an extra 20 to 40 minutes every time. A 30-minute workout with a 15-minute drive each way doubles your workout time.

At home there's no commute. You roll out of bed and start. And you don't need much equipment: a yoga mat, push-up handles, a pair of light dumbbells, a kettlebell, an ab wheel and a jump rope. You can get all of it for under $100. If you want something to start with, do my [3 minute total body home workout](/videos/3-minute-total-body-home-workout-follow-along-no-talking/).

I'll keep it real with you. I personally pay for a gym membership, even though I spent $20,000 building my own home gym, and I work out seven days a week despite the commute. A real gym is the best option if you can go every day. But if time is the reason you're not working out, the gym is the wrong choice for you right now. Start at home with quick daily workouts, and join a gym later once working out every day is a habit. I go deeper on this in [how to make time to work out](/videos/how-to-make-time-to-work-out/).

## 3. Use a meal prep service and stop cooking

Add up the time you spend on food right now. You drive to the grocery store, shop, drive home, cook, and wash the dishes. The average American spends over 5 hours a week on all of that.

With a meal prep service, your meals arrive already cooked and portioned. You put one in the microwave for 2 minutes and throw the container away when you're done. That saves you 2 or 3 hours every week, and every meal has the calories and macros right on the label, so tracking your calories gets a lot easier too.

Meals run about $8 each, and many services deliver. That's less than ordering Chipotle and only slightly more per meal than cooking yourself. If you're making decent money and still cooking all your own food, you're wasting your time. Pay someone else to do it and put those hours into working out. Same logic as [hiring a maid instead of a personal trainer](/videos/hire-a-maid-instead-of-a-personal-trainer/).

## 4. Buy a robot lawn mower

If you have a house with a yard, you're probably spending 2 to 4 hours a week mowing, every week, for six or seven months of the year. A robot lawn mower does all of it for you.

No more burying a wire around your yard: the new ones use GPS, cameras and AI. You map your yard once in the app, and after that it goes out by itself, cuts the grass and drives itself back to the charger.

They cost about $1,000 to $2,500 depending on your lawn and the brand. A lawn service runs $100 to $200 per visit, which adds up to $3,000 to $6,000 a year or more, so the robot pays for itself in a year or two. And a normal lawn mower costs about the same as the robot, so compared to mowing yourself, it costs you nothing extra. For a lot of suburban dads, this alone frees up hours every week to work out.

## 5. Let the car drive: a Tesla or a robotaxi

The average American spends about an hour a day in the car. That's seven hours a week where you can't do anything except stare at the road. Once your car drives for you, you get that time back. You can take work calls and send prompts to AI so it's working for you before you get to the office.

Tesla does check that you're watching the road, so do all of this with your voice. That's where Wispr Flow comes in again.

A Tesla isn't just a rich guy's car. A new Model 3 starts at about $37,000 and a new Toyota Camry at about $30,000. But the Tesla uses no gas and barely needs maintenance. According to Kelley Blue Book, over 5 years the Tesla costs about $4,600 less to fuel and maintain than the Camry. The real gap is a lot smaller than the sticker price.

If your city has robotaxis, you might not need to own a car at all. They're in Austin, where I live, and in a bunch of other big cities, with more added all the time. You sit in the back, there's no driver, and you can work on your laptop the whole ride. You'll have more focus and energy once you stop spending it on driving.

## 6. Use AI to do your work for you

This is the biggest one. If you have a job, start aggressively figuring out how to use AI to do your job. Pretty much every programmer already uses it all day, but a lot of people in non-technical fields don't realize AI can do 90% of their work.

Here's my own example. I'm not a programmer, and I've never written one line of code. AI built the entire Abs By AI app, and it does my bookkeeping too. Now I'm using AI to edit videos for my channel. AI saves me at least 10 hours a week that I put into working out, eating right and sleeping.

And this is the worst AI will ever be. If you figure out how to save five hours a week with it now, you'll probably save 20 hours a week next year. So here's what to do:

1. Subscribe to Claude.
2. Give it the most boring, time-consuming parts of your job.
3. Add one new task every week.

I'd say within a few months you'll be getting the same work done in half the time.

## Add it all up

- **Wispr Flow:** 20 minutes a day.
- **Working out at home:** 2 to 3 hours a week.
- **A meal prep service:** another 2 to 3 hours a week.
- **A robot lawn mower:** another 2 to 4 hours a week.
- **A car that drives for you:** up to 7 hours a week.
- **AI doing your work:** could be bigger than all of those put together.

That's well over 10 hours a week. And you only need about 2.5 hours a week to work out for 20 minutes every single day. You're not too busy to get in shape. Use AI as aggressively as you can to get your time back, and put that time into working out and eating right.
