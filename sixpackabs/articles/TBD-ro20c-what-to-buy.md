Here's exactly what you should be buying for your health, whether you're dead broke, middle class, or making over $10,000 a month. You need to be spending more money on your health no matter how little you have right now, because money does you no good if you're dead or if you're sick.

The last video covered what to cut to find the money. Now let's spend it.

## If you're dead broke

Even if you're living in a cardboard box, you still need my [basic home workout setup](/videos/how-to-work-out-at-home-on-a-budget/). Beg, borrow, do whatever it takes to get it, because your life depends on it.

You need a yoga mat, push-up handles, a pair of light dumbbells for curls and side laterals, a kettlebell, an ab wheel and a jump rope. All of that you can get for under $100. If you don't have it, there is literally no excuse. Go buy that stuff right now.

Next is basic healthy food. I hear so many people saying, I can't afford healthy food, Dan. Wrong way to think. Healthy food is going to save you money compared to eating the standard American diet.

You don't need luxury or organic. Get a Costco membership. Buy chicken thighs or drumsticks or chicken breasts, a little bit of rice, and a cruciferous vegetable like Brussels sprouts, broccoli or cabbage. To save even more, skip the rice. Chicken and vegetables are cheap as dirt, and they'll make you more ripped. Rice and beans if you're even poorer than that.

But don't say you don't have enough money for healthy food. Start thinking instead, I don't have enough money to buy junk food.

## If you're middle class

### 1. The best protein money can buy

My favorite is wild-caught Alaskan sockeye salmon, about $20 a pound, compared to about $3 a pound for chicken thighs. Filet mignon at Costco is about $25 to $30 a pound. When you're eating premium protein, your meal prep feels like a privilege, not a sacrifice. And fish has a better balance of omega-3 to omega-6 fatty acids, so your heart health is better.

A lot of people say they can't afford salmon, but they're making a 401k contribution every month. Which produces better returns? In my opinion, the salmon. Skip it and you're more likely to get heart disease in your 60s or 70s, and that 401k money evaporates on hospital visits and medication.

### 2. A better mattress

Your sleep is just as important as your nutrition and your workouts. Yet a lot of people are sleeping on a 10 year old mattress sagging in the middle, giving them back pain and terrible sleep.

A better mattress only improves your sleep a little bit. But even two or three percent better sleep is worth two or three thousand dollars over the ten years a good mattress lasts. Every workout gets better, and you're more productive.

My pick is the Purple Mattress. My sleep substantially improved when I bought one. It's a gel grid, not springs, so even a denser, thicker guy like me never wears an indentation into it. And a regular mattress soaks up years of sweat and grows bacteria. The Purple absorbs far less, so it's cleaner and you may get sick less.

### 3. A gym with the best people

Not the gym with the best equipment. The gym with the best people. At home it's hard to afford a leg press, a leg curl machine, a full set of dumbbells. Middle class, you have the funds to go beyond getting the job done.

But the people matter more. I pay for a membership even though I spent $20,000 on my own home gym. Training around bodybuilders and fitness models changes my mentality. There's levels to this shit. I absorb their energy even when I'm not talking to them. $80 to $120 a month. Go for the gym with the most ripped people.

### 4. A sleep tracker

I wear the Oura Ring every night. In my opinion it's the most accurate, and I'd rather pay the mandatory subscription and get better data. The finger is more accurate than the wrist. The Whoop is thin and more comfortable in bed than the Apple Watch, which is bulky to sleep on, and a silicone band slips around. So do what I do: Apple Watch in the day, ring at night. Shorter on funds, the Whoop gets the job done.

### 5. GLP-1 medication

Tirzepatide, retatrutide, Ozempic. Without insurance it's about 400 bucks a month, and people say, I guess I'll just be fat the rest of my life. Totally the wrong way to think.

If you're overweight in your 40s or 50s, this is crunch time. You have very limited time before serious, serious medical bills.

It reduces the food noise so much. That's one of the main reasons I, with six-pack abs, plan on staying on Zepbound. On the right dose, you don't feel hungry when you're fasting, and your focus goes way up. The bar, the club and restaurants don't seem as appealing either. For most people, the $400 comes back in restaurant meals, junk food and alcohol, so it nearly pays for itself.

### 6. TRT

I haven't started yet. I plan to within the next year or two. But I know many people who went on it, and they say their productivity got so much higher. If you're a man in your 40s or 50s, get tested.

This is my opinion, not medical advice.

### 7. The big three supplements

I take 10 supplements a day. You only need three, and they're cheap.

1. **Fish oil or an omega supplement.** If you take only one supplement, take this one. Heart, brain, joints, workout recovery.
2. **Vitamin D.** A lot of people are low, more so if you're older or have darker skin. I use a liquid vitamin D with a little dropper.
3. **Magnesium glycinate.** Right before bed. Better sleep, more relaxed.

## If you're making over $10,000 a month

Now the ballers.

### 1. A home gym

About $10,000 inside the house, $20,000 to $30,000 as a separate structure. I had a shitty old unfinished shed in my backyard. During COVID, with the gyms closed, I finished it: insulation, air conditioning, a rubber floor, the equipment.

No commute, so you work out more often. Your own music through the speakers. And it drops that $100 to $200 a month membership, so in the long run it can pay for itself. The best of both worlds is what I do: a home gym and a gym membership. The gym for a regular workout in a good environment, the home gym when you're strapped for time.

### 2. A meal prep service or a personal chef

Making over $10,000 a month and cooking? You're wasting your time. Every other week I use Clean Eats, a meal prep service here in Austin. About $120 a week for a week of meals portioned at 300 to 500 calories each, so tracking your calories is far easier and more accurate. The ripped multimillionaires I know have a full-time personal chef.

### 3. A maid and a laundry service

So many rich guys say, I'm a lawyer, Dan, I don't have time to work out. But they're doing their own laundry, cooking, cleaning and dishes. That's where you find the time. [Hire a maid instead of a personal trainer](/videos/hire-a-maid-instead-of-a-personal-trainer/) and put those hours into working out.

### 4. A personal trainer and nutritionist, last on purpose

A human one, and it's last for a reason. If you haven't bought everything else on this list, don't buy this. It has the lowest return of anything here. If money is no object, it's worth it: a human keeps you accountable and shows you details of form. Somebody in the top 1%, at $200 to $400 an hour, can build you a program even better than what AI can do. Most of them, the 99%, don't rise to that bar.

## Beyond the list

Past this, the evidence is a lot more limited. Red light therapy machines, exotic peptides, things like that are more speculative. If you've done everything on the list already, check out Bryan Johnson's YouTube channel. Most of you, just stick to the list.

You're being too cheap with your health, and your cheapness and wasting money on bullshit is literally killing you. Broke: a basic home workout setup and healthy food, no excuse. Middle class, like most of you guys: $500 to $1,000 a month on this list, before you add to savings or a retirement account.

Money doesn't do you any good if you die. If you've got the money, spend it.
