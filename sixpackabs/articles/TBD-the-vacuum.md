If you have belly fat, stop doing ab exercises. No crunches, no sit-ups. The one ab exercise I recommend for people with stomach fat is the standing stomach vacuum, and you can do it in about 20 seconds a day.

Here is why I say that, and exactly how I do it.

## Why ab exercises will not burn your belly fat

Crunches and sit-ups train the rectus abdominis, the muscle that makes up the visible six-pack. Training it only puts muscle on the front of your stomach. That is great if you are already lean, because the muscle shows. If you still have belly fat on top of it, a bigger muscle just pushes your stomach out more and can make you look fatter.

A lot of people still believe in spot reduction, the idea that a thousand crunches burn the fat right off your stomach. It does not work that way. When you exercise or burn calories in any way, you burn fat across your whole body, not from the spot you trained. If you want a leaner waist, the real levers are total body workouts and nutrition. For the one ab move that is worth doing, keep reading. If you are already lean and want to build your abs, my [ab wheel video](/videos/the-17-ab-wheel-beats-every-crunch/) is where to start.

## The muscle you want to train instead

While your abs are still hidden, the muscle to train is the transverse abdominis. It is a hidden sleeve of muscle beneath your six-pack. It is not the muscle that crunches. It stabilizes you when you squat, and it is the one you are working when you suck your stomach in for a vacuum.

Bodybuilders have used the vacuum since the 60s and 70s to keep a tiny waist, which is part of why old school guys like Arnold Schwarzenegger and Frank Zane had smaller waists than many modern bodybuilders with a big bubble gut. Almost nobody with belly fat does it, and they are the people who would benefit most.

## The two things the vacuum does

Physically, doing the vacuum the way I describe trains your transverse abdominis, and over time it can shrink your waist at rest. I saw my own waist come down significantly after I started. I also started Zepbound at the same time, so I can't tell you how much was the vacuum and how much was eating less. I do believe the vacuums helped, which is why I still do them a few times a week, and my clients with belly fat have had even better results.

Psychologically, it makes you look at the problem. Most people with stomach fat avoid the mirror and cover up with baggy clothes, which lets you drift into denial. A daily vacuum puts you in front of the mirror, shirt off, looking at your stomach. That is uncomfortable, and it is exactly why it works.

## Standing, not on your hands and knees

There are three versions: on your knees, on your hands and knees (which works against gravity and is a bit harder), and standing. I only recommend and do the standing vacuum:

- You can watch yourself in the mirror and challenge yourself to hollow your waist as much as possible.
- You can do it anywhere with no equipment and no floor.
- It is quick and realistic to add to a routine.
- It is like a real-life AI transformation picture. For 20 seconds you see yourself with the smaller waist you would have all the time if you lost the weight.

## How to do the vacuum, step by step

1. Do it in front of a mirror so you can see how far you are hollowing.
2. Do it shirtless, with at least your belly visible.
3. Do it on an empty stomach. First thing in the morning, or any time in your [fasting window](/videos/why-i-skip-breakfast-every-single-day/). A full stomach is uncomfortable and you cannot hollow as far.
4. Consciously slump and let your stomach bulge out.
5. Suck it in as hard as you can, like you are trying to pull your belly button through your spine. This should be a challenge.
6. Use a timer and hold for 20 seconds. I use a Gymboss. Without a timer, it feels like more time has passed than it has.
7. Keep breathing. Take short breaths through your nose. Do not hold your breath, or you will be struggling by the end of the set.

## Your first weeks

If you have never done vacuums, start with one timed 20 second set when you wake up. That alone makes a difference. When it is comfortable, do two sets of 20 seconds with a minute of rest between them, then three sets. In the rests, take deep belly breaths so you recover your oxygen before the next set. First thing in the morning is the best time. After a workout is the backup, though you will be more tired.

## The takeaway

If you have belly fat, stop the crunches and sit-ups until you are leaner, and focus on total body workouts and nutrition to burn the fat. Add 20 seconds of vacuums every morning and see what it does for your waist and your mindset.
