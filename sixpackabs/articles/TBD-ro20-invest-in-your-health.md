You need to be investing more in your health, no matter how little you have right now. Here are the four reasons why.

## Reason 1: The halo effect

A lot of people drastically underestimate the value of how they look. They think, making money is my first priority, so I'll just be a fat guy who has money and be happy with that.

That is totally the wrong way to think. If money is your top priority, you need to be prioritizing your looks.

It has been scientifically proven that people are more likely to think you're intelligent, good at your job, funnier and have a better personality purely based on how you look. Even very intelligent people you do business with judge you this way unconsciously.

Applying for a new job? Your odds are drastically, drastically better if you look better. Yes, even if you're a man. The effect is more powerful for women, but it's still extremely impactful for men.

Partnering with somebody in business? Overweight and sloppy, you're less likely to get the partnership. Take care of your appearance and you send the message that you get the job done and can be trusted.

## How your weight changes your relationship

Let's say you're in a relationship and you're getting a little overweight. She's not going to consciously say, my husband's getting fat and sloppy, I'm going to divorce him. She'll think, he doesn't care about me like he used to. He isn't as funny. I'm not enjoying sex with him as much. The spark isn't there. Those are the reasons she gives for leaving you lonely. A lot of the time the real reason is you got fat, you got lazy, and because of that she perceives all these negative traits about you.

Guys who are ripped with six-pack abs get the opposite. She won't say, my man has a six-pack. She'll say, my man is so funny, he cares about me so much, we have the spark. Both guys could be doing the exact same thing and be perceived totally, totally differently.

So if your family comes first, you have to care about your health. It's better to spend a little more time in the gym, a little more money on your health, and a little less time and money on your relationship.

A lot of people meet on dating apps now, and online, looks are paramount. In the real world, a good talker can get away with not looking that good. Online, no. If you're a little fat, a little sloppy, especially combined with being short like me, they swipe left. They never see your personality or your money.

## Reason 2: You get more done

A lot of guys think, I don't need to spend more on my health, I just need to focus on being productive. Totally the wrong way to think.

I work out an hour every morning, and I get more done in a day than the average person, despite working fewer hours. It's hard to grind if you're not in peak physical condition.

You see it before you even get in shape. Invest in a good meal prep service, or hire a personal chef to make you a salad every day, instead of going to Taco Bell for lunch. Your productivity might dip for a couple of days. But within two or three days of eating a low carb lunch and fasting all day, no more lunchtime slump. Your afternoon becomes the most productive time of your day. Here's [why I skip breakfast every single day](/videos/why-i-skip-breakfast-every-single-day/).

## Gym time or doctor's office time

Health issues show up sooner than you think. I know people my age who go to the doctor every week. Multiple problems, all kinds of prescription medications, arthritis, joint problems from being overweight.

If you don't spend the time in the gym, you spend it in the doctor's office. You make the appointment, wait for it, go to it, get the prescription filled, drive to pick it up. And you're out money that was preventable.

Most people won't give up a few days of productivity to get more in the long term. That short-term thinking is the reason why you're poor right now. If you had been thinking long term five years ago, you would be richer and in better shape today.

## Reason 3: Better mental health

Being in great shape tremendously improves your mental health, and this is scientifically proven. The people with the worst mental health problems usually are not ripped. Physical health and mental health go hand in hand. If your body is unhealthy, your mind will be too.

Mental health problems are expensive to fix. Therapy every week drains hours out of your productivity. Medication is expensive, and a lot of it can make you fat. Being fat makes the mental health problems worse, which means more therapy, more medication, getting fatter, a spiral of failure many people are trapped in. That's why your physical health is your first spending priority, even above therapy and medication.

## Reason 4: Bad health is ludicrously expensive

Even if you only care about money, bad health is ludicrously expensive, especially in the United States, where I live. The worse your health, the more you pay, even with insurance.

The lawyer or doctor working 100 hours a week stacks millions by their 50s and 60s, then gets diabetes, heart issues, cancer. Most people who neglect their health spend all their money and more trying to fix these problems, usually without success.

I know somebody personally who made millions over his lifetime, neglected his health, and got diabetes. Insulin injections and doctor visits all the time. Low blood sugar emergencies: an ambulance to the hospital, five, ten, fifteen grand per trip, and ten or fifteen grand per hospital stay. He had a toe amputated. He gets injections in his eyes because he's at risk of losing his vision, and you lose your vision, your money making days are over.

If he could go back 20 years, he'd have made that investment instead of desperately spending millions now. By the time your toes are getting cut off, you're throwing the Hail Mary.

You don't have these issues yet. You will if you keep going down your current path. So stop putting that money in your 401k. Put it into your health. A much, much better investment.

## Money does you no good if you're dead

Money doesn't do you any good if you're dead. Pass millions on to your heirs and you did not benefit from that at all.

People say at least the kids will benefit. I really don't think they will. On both sides of my family, my extended family started suing each other and wasted more money on the legal fight than they inherited.

And the problems kick in way, way earlier than you think, so money stops doing you any good even before you're dead. Lose a toe and walking vacations, the pool, paddle boarding and the beach get a lot harder. Lose your vision and the most beautiful vacation in the world doesn't matter. Get joint problems and that gigantic house won't matter, because the stairs hurt so much you stay on the first floor like an old person.

Your kids would much rather have a healthy parent who can take care of them than a debilitated one leaving them a pile of money. Your wife or girlfriend would far rather have a man who is alive, healthy and in great shape than someone she has to take care of who has a little more money.

Money does you no good if you're dead or sick. Spend whatever you have right now on your health. In the long run, investing in your health makes you more money.
