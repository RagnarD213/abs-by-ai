This 3 minute workout hits your total body at once, and all you need is a kettlebell and a towel. Five exercises, 40 seconds each: kettlebell deadlifts, push-ups, jump squats, towel rows and toe touches. Do one round on the days you can't make it to the gym, or follow along with me here for all three rounds.

Listen: I used workouts like this during quarantine, when the gyms were closed and I couldn't train the way I normally did. I expected to lose muscle and gain fat. Instead I gained muscle and lost fat. Workouts like this work, as long as you're dedicated to them.

## The workout

Each exercise runs for 40 seconds, back to back, with just a few seconds to switch. We're not counting reps in this workout. We're counting seconds, and that makes sure every body part gets good time under tension.

1. **Kettlebell deadlifts**, 40 seconds
2. **Push-ups**, 40 seconds
3. **Jump squats**, 40 seconds
4. **Towel rows**, 40 seconds
5. **Toe touches**, 40 seconds
6. **Jog in place**, 50 seconds, then start the next round

That's one round: five exercises, a little over three minutes of work. In the video I run you through three rounds, with a 50 second jog in place after round 1 and round 2. The whole follow-along takes about 13 minutes.

## What you need

- **A kettlebell** for the deadlifts
- **A towel** for the rows
- **A mat** for the toe touches
- **Optional:** push-up handles, and a small weight for the toe touches if you're advanced

## How to do each exercise

### Kettlebell deadlifts

I love this exercise, especially for beginners, because it's significantly safer than a barbell deadlift and it can be just as effective.

Take a wide stance, feet slightly wider than your shoulders, with the kettlebell between your legs, not out in front of you. Squat down with your legs, keep your back straight, grab the kettlebell and stand up with it. At the top, squeeze your glutes. You don't need to swing it.

The biggest reason people get injured on deadlifts is rounding their back instead of bending their legs. My trick for good posture when you're tired: look at the ceiling, or the sky if you're outside. Look at the ground and you naturally fall into bad posture. Look up and it's hard to.

### Push-ups

Go down slow and controlled, touch your nose to the ground, and push back up. That's one rep.

To go the extra mile, set a pair of inexpensive push-up handles about shoulder width apart. They give you extra range and more stretch in your chest.

If you don't have the upper body strength to do push-ups for the full 40 seconds yet, do them from your knees. Same form: nose to the ground, then back up.

### Jump squats

Do one regular squat, then one squat with a jump. One regular squat plus one jump equals one rep, and you keep going for the full 40 seconds.

If you're a beginner and you're not comfortable jumping yet, skip the jump and do regular squats. If you're advanced, don't do a little jump like I do. Do a real big jump, and you'll get an even better workout.

### Towel rows

This is a great way to hit your back without equipment. People who think towel exercises are a scam just don't know how to do them.

The key is tension. If you hold the towel limp, you're just waving your arms around. Grab it with both hands and pull it tight, so you're straining before the towel even moves. Then row it in toward you while you keep it tight, and you're working even harder. Hold that tension the entire 40 seconds.

Here's the test we used in group workouts: have someone karate chop your towel. If it stays tight, you're doing it right. If it collapses, you don't have enough tension. Done right, it's strenuous even if you're strong and athletic.

### Toe touches

Lie flat on the mat in a straight line, with your arms overhead and your feet and toes on the ground. Then jackknife your body and touch your hands to your toes.

Get your head and your butt as far off the ground as possible at the top. If they stay down, you're just waving your arms and legs around. It sounds a little weird, but try to knee yourself in the face on every rep. Aiming for that motion lifts your butt and head higher, and that means your abs are working more.

To make it easier, put a slight bend in your arms and legs. To make it harder, hold a 2.5 or 5 pound plate, a small dumbbell or a small medicine ball. This is the same toe touch I use in my ab workouts, and I break it down in [the toe touch, a killer six pack abs home exercise](/videos/killer-six-pack-abs-home-exercise-the-toe-touch/).

## How to scale the workout

- **Busy day:** one round. You can't always make it to the gym, and you can't always spend an hour working out, but you always have three minutes.
- **Step it up:** do all three rounds with me. That's about 10 minutes of work, and it does a lot more for you.
- **Beginner or advanced:** use the easier or harder version of each exercise above.
