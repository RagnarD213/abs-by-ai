You have four ab muscles, not just a six pack: the rectus abdominis in front, the internal and external obliques on your sides, and the transverse abdominis, a deep sleeve of muscle that holds everything together.

Listen: when most people think of abs, they only think of the six pack. That's one of four. A lot of the workouts I used to do only worked that one muscle group, and I've since learned it's important to work all of them.

## The 4 ab muscles

1. **Rectus abdominis:** the six pack in front. Crunching motions.
2. **Internal obliques:** on your sides. Rotation and core stability.
3. **External obliques:** on your sides. Twisting and bending.
4. **Transverse abdominis:** the deep core. Holds everything together.

## 1. Rectus abdominis: the six pack

The rectus abdominis is the six pack, the muscle in front. When most people think of your abs, this is what they're thinking about. It's what you see when someone lean has a six pack, and it's the muscle crunches and toe touches train. But it's only one of the four.

## 2 and 3. Internal and external obliques

Your obliques are on your sides, and there are two parts: the internal obliques and the external obliques. They handle twisting and bending motions. It's important to train your obliques too.

## 4. Transverse abdominis: the one you can't see

You can't see the transverse abdominis from the outside. It's a sleeve of muscle beneath your rectus abdominis that holds everything together.

When you do a plank, your transverse abdominis is what enables you to hold it. When you do a squat and need to stay stable, it's what holds it all together. And when you do a vacuum and suck your stomach in, that's your transverse abdominis doing it.
