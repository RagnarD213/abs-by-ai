You can get fat eating healthy foods. Some so-called healthy foods are high in calories and carbs, and it doesn't matter if they're organic or natural or if you bought them at Whole Foods. These foods will make you fat if you eat too much of them, and they're very, very easy to eat too much of.

High calorie so-called healthy foods were one of the biggest reasons why I struggled to lose the last 10 pounds before I saw my abs. For years my meals were clean and I still wasn't lean. It wasn't my dinner that was the problem. It was the so-called healthy snacks I was eating in between meals. Once I cut them out, I finally got the abs I was trying to get for years, and making that one change did more for my abs than any workout program I ever followed.

Here are the three healthy foods I stopped eating because they didn't fit my calorie budget. At the end I'll show you what I snack on instead.

## 1. Trail mix

Trail mix has a great reputation. It's nuts and fruit. It's what people eat when they go hiking, so everyone assumes it has to be good for you.

But go look at the label. A serving of trail mix is 3 tablespoons, and those 3 tablespoons are about 170 calories and 14 grams of carbs. Now go and measure out 3 tablespoons. It's one small handful.

And let's keep it real, you're not eating one small handful of trail mix and then putting the bag away. I know I wasn't. What really happens is you're watching TV or you're working at your desk and you keep reaching into the bag. One cup of trail mix is about 700 calories and 65 grams of carbs. That's more calories than a Big Mac. And that was just a snack in between meals, and you thought you were being healthy the whole time. That's how you become the average American, eating 700 calories of healthy trail mix at your desk and wondering why you're still fat.

It's nuts, which are one of the most calorie dense foods in the entire grocery store. It's raisins, which are basically sugar. And most brands throw in M&M's or chocolate chips on top of that. So really what you're eating is candy mixed with nuts, and they're selling it to you as a health food.

Now if you're hiking up a mountain for eight hours with a backpack on, trail mix makes sense because you need the calories. But most of you are eating it sitting at a desk, and you're not burning any of that off.

## 2. Dried fruit

Real fruit is fairly low in calories and high in fiber. I'm not telling you to stop eating fresh fruit. But dried fruit is a totally different thing.

When you dry a piece of fruit, all you're doing is taking the water out. All of the sugar stays in there, so you end up with the same amount of sugar in something that's a quarter of the size.

A quarter cup of raisins is about 120 calories and 31 grams of carbs, and 26 of those grams are sugar. That's about the same as a big bowl of grapes. But the bowl of grapes takes you a while to eat, and all the water in it fills you up. You can eat them in three bites and you're just as hungry as you were before you ate them. So you end up eating three or four times that much, and now you're at 400 or 500 calories from a little bit of fruit.

And it's even worse with dried mango, because a lot of brands add more sugar on top of the sugar that's already there. Let's say you eat the whole bag, which is very easy to do. That's more sugar than a can of Coke.

So if you want fruit, eat the real thing. Eat an apple or eat some berries. With real fruit, the fiber and the water are going to fill you up before you can eat too much of it.

## 3. Coconut water

Coconut water is sold as a natural, healthy way to hydrate because it has electrolytes and it has potassium. But really it's sugar water.

One of the normal sized cartons is about 90 calories and 20 grams of sugar. And a lot of people buy the big one liter carton and drink it throughout the day. That's about 180 calories and 40 grams of sugar. So in my opinion it's just as bad as soda.

You should be avoiding [all liquid calories](/videos/jelly-beans-beat-soda-heres-the-study/). Liquid calories don't fill you up, so you don't eat any less to make up for them. You just add the drink on top of everything else you ate for the day.

And you don't need it. Unless you're running a marathon in the heat, you're not so low on potassium that you need to drink sugar to fix it. Just drink water. If you want the electrolytes, get a zero calorie electrolyte powder and put a scoop of that in your water.

I drank large amounts of coconut water for years thinking I needed it to stay hydrated. These days I stay just as hydrated or better from a zero calorie electrolyte powder.

## These foods aren't bad for you. They're fattening.

I'm not saying these foods are bad for your health if you eat small amounts of them. I'm saying these foods make you fat because they're high in calories, and because it's very difficult to eat small amounts of them.

If you have belly fat, the number one most important thing is eating fewer calories. You only get so many calories a day if you want to lose fat, and I think about that like a budget. Let's say I spend 700 of those calories on a snack that didn't even fill me up. Now I've got almost nothing left for dinner.

Just because it says healthy on the bag doesn't mean it's going to help you lose fat. That's marketing, and the food companies are making a whole lot of money off of it.

## What I snack on instead

The first thing I changed is I eat larger meals now, so I feel less need to snack. In my experience, if you're snacking all day, a lot of the time it's because your meals are too small.

And if I do snack, I snack on something that's high in protein and low in carbs. Here's what I eat:

1. **Sardines.** A can of sardines is about 18 grams of protein and zero carbs, and you're getting your omega-3s at the same time. They're about $2 a can and you don't have to cook anything. If you've never eaten sardines, try a can. You might be surprised to find you really like them, like I do.
2. **Hard boiled eggs.** Two eggs are about 150 calories and 12 grams of protein. You can boil a dozen of them on Sunday and you have snacks for the whole week. Or buy pre-packaged hard boiled eggs like I do to make it easy.
3. **Rotisserie chicken.** It's $5 at Costco and it's already cooked. You just pull some off and eat it.
4. **The protein cup from Chipotle.** It's just a cup of chicken or steak with nothing else in it. The chicken one is about 180 calories and 32 grams of protein, and it costs about $7. If you're out running around and you get hungry, I'd say that's the best fast food snack out there.

These foods are lower in calories and higher in protein, and they fill you up far, far better. But the biggest difference in my opinion is that they're not addictive like the carb heavy snacks are. You can eat half a bag of trail mix and not even notice you did it. Nobody has ever binged on sardines. You eat one can and then you're done.

Here's what surprised me the most. After about three weeks of snacking like this, I stopped wanting the carb snacks at all, and that made hitting my calories so much easier. Carbs and sugar are addictive. The less you have, the less you want. That's one of the most powerful reasons why cutting out these so-called healthy foods will help you lose weight. ## Throw them out

If you have belly fat, go throw out the trail mix, dried fruit and coconut water in your pantry today and replace them with sardines, eggs and chicken.
