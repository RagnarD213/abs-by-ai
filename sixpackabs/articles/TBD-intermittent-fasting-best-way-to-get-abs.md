Intermittent fasting is the number one most powerful way I've found to get six pack abs. I've been doing intermittent fasting for about 10 years and it has totally changed my life. It's made it easier to get lean. It's made it easier to keep my abs. It's made me more productive and I feel better.

## What Intermittent Fasting Is

Intermittent fasting is just not eating during a certain part of the day and using fasting as a way to cut your calories and get lean.

So the most important thing in getting lean is your nutrition. And the most important part of your nutrition is the amount of calories that you eat. If you burn more calories than you eat, you're going to lose fat. If you eat more calories than you burn, you're going to gain fat.

Of course, there are other things too, like micronutrients and protein. But the number one reason most people don't have abs is they are eating too many calories. Until you fix this calorie problem, you're always going to have belly fat.

So how do you cut your calories while still eating the food you enjoy? You fast the first part of the day.

## My Daily Fast

What I personally do is simply [skip breakfast and eat a late lunch](/videos/why-i-skip-breakfast-every-single-day/). I typically wake up around 9 AM. I don't eat breakfast and then I fast until about 2 PM. So that's about a five hour fast every day at the start of my day. That's what's worked best for me, and that's what I recommend for you as well.

There are other kinds of intermittent fasting too. Some people do a long fast three or four days a week and eat normally on the other days. Some people even go entire days without eating. I've tried long fasts on certain days and not fasting on other days. But what's gotten me the best result is a short fast that I do every single day.

All I do is skip breakfast, eat a late lunch, and keep my lunch fairly low carb. That's what got me abs. That's what's kept me lean.

## Why Intermittent Fasting Beats Cutting Calories All Day

### 1. It's The Simplest Way To Eat Less

You're simply not eating during a certain part of the day. If you're reducing your calories at every meal, that involves complexity. Smaller portions, substituting lower calorie foods, and if you didn't substitute at one meal, you've got to make up for it at another. It's a lot of additional work making a breakfast you wouldn't have to make.

And for a lot of you guys working a 9 to 5, these decisions are being made under duress. You're tired, you're stressed out, you're rushing out the door.

Mike Tyson once said, "Everybody has a plan until they get punched in the face." When you're trying to get lean, life is going to punch you in the face. In my experience, complex plans just don't work out in real life, and people end up overeating when they didn't intend to.

Intermittent fasting you can execute when life punches you in the face. You're sick, there's an emergency, you got in an argument with your girlfriend. You still fast your five hours.

### 2. Health Benefits Beyond Fat Loss

This won't make an immediate difference for how your abs look. But if you want to live a long and healthy life, if you want to avoid heart disease and cancer, I believe this makes a tremendous difference long term.

The reason is autophagy. When you're fasting, your cells run out of fuel. So they break down the most damaged, most diseased and oldest parts of your cells for fuel. That renews your cells.

### 3. It Saves Your Mornings

That alarm goes off, you've got to hit your morning workout, get ready for work. Making and eating breakfast on top of that is a lot of additional work. Me, I just drink my black coffee and go out the door.

### 4. You'll Be More Productive

I've become so much more productive since I started fasting. Only in hindsight did I realize the donuts and muffins I used to eat for breakfast were destroying my productivity.

A heavy breakfast makes your body spend energy digesting. That's the food coma. Just black coffee, your energy is actually higher. Keep it going all day with a salad or a very low carb lunch. In my experience, the real problem during your workday is the carbs. Nobody is grinding out productive 12 hour days on donuts and pizza.

### 5. Your Body Adapts

Here's the most important one. People try fasting for a day or a week or two, they're shocked how hungry they get, they think, I'm not cut out for this, and they quit.

But your digestive system adapts just like your muscles adapt to workouts. Nobody quits the gym because their first hard workout left them sore. The first couple weeks are going to be hard.

I haven't had breakfast in many years. I don't get hungry in the morning at all, and fasting is nearly effortless for me. But it wasn't that way at first. The first 30 days I was really struggling. By the end of those 30 days the hunger was nearly gone, and 90 days in it was almost totally gone. It's hard at first, but long term, intermittent fasting is far easier than cutting your calories evenly throughout the day.

## How to get started

The easiest way to start intermittent fasting is to skip your breakfast and keep everything else you eat the same.

To help with the hunger, drink black coffee. I drink cold brew black coffee. It's a little less acidic, so it's easier on your stomach when you're fasting.

If you really want to take the fat loss to the next level, you can add a GLP-1 like Zepbound, Ozempic, tirzepatide, semaglutide, or even retatrutide. These can eliminate hunger nearly entirely in your fasting window, so fasting is nearly effortless from the get-go. You don't have to do that, though. Plenty of people started fasting without one, myself included.

Here's the game plan:

1. **Month one.** Skip breakfast, have a little more black coffee, and eat your normal lunch and normal dinner. Truly keep the rest of the day the same. You can eat a little more later and it still works. But if you eat all your breakfast calories later, you're defeating the purpose.
2. **Push lunch back.** If you eat lunch at noon, move it to 12:30. Stay there until you're comfortable and not hungry, probably about 30 days. Then push it back again. Keep going until you're fasting five hours after you wake up. If you wake up at 9 AM like me, lunch is at 2. If you wake up at 7 AM, lunch is at noon. A five hour daily fast is more than realistic in a couple of months if you stick to it.
3. **Next level.** Break your fast with a salad or a low carb meal. It's one of my best nutrition habits, and I feel so much better than when I eat normally for lunch.

## Three mistakes to avoid

### Binge eating late at night

This sabotaged my results for a while. When you don't eat for part of the day, [you'll be tempted to eat more later](/videos/the-ai-trick-that-killed-my-late-night-snacking/). It's less than you'd think, because your stomach adapts. But if you binge on junk food late in the day, fasting won't work for you. Eat reasonable portions of reasonably healthy food. Some spaghetti or a piece of pizza is fine. Keep it small, not full of sugar, and a reasonable portion. No milkshakes, no jumbo ice cream sundaes.

### Eating within two hours of bed

It [disrupts your sleep](/videos/8-hours-in-bed-is-not-8-hours-of-sleep/). It also stretches your fast. Eight hours of sleep plus two hours before bed is already 10 hours. Add five in the morning and that's a 15 hour fast. You sleep better and lose more fat.

### Breaking your fast with alcohol

This is the most important one. No alcohol with your first meal, and God forbid not before you've eaten anything at all. After a fast, alcohol absorbs faster and your body is more sensitive to it. It hits harder, you feel more drunk, and it does more damage to your fat loss and your health. Wait 30 minutes after breaking your fast. And avoid alcohol while you're working.
