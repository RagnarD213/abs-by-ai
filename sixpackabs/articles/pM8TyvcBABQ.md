Rear delt flys train your rear delts, which are the back of your shoulders. And it's one of the most skipped muscles there is. Here's how to do them: hinge forward and raise a pair of light dumbbells out to your sides, leading with your elbows. Then lower them back down slowly.

## Why your rear delts matter

Your rear delts are the back of your shoulders. A lot of people skip them and just focus on the muscles in the front of their body. But training your rear delts pulls your posture back so you stop looking slouched.

That means your chest, arms, and shoulders will all look better from training rear delts. Good posture from rear delt training makes the entire front of your body look better. So even if what you really care about is your chest and arms, rear delts are worth training.

## How to do rear delt flys at home

Here's how to do the dumbbell version at home.

1. **Grab a light pair of dumbbells.** You don't need a lot of weight for this one. Light dumbbells are all you need.
2. **Hinge forward at your hips.** Keep your back flat and your chest up. Keep your back as flat as possible, and you should feel a stretch in your legs, in your hamstrings.
3. **Raise the dumbbells out to your sides.** Lead with your elbows, and squeeze your shoulder blades together at the top.
4. **Lower them slowly.** Bring the dumbbells back down slowly and under control. Don't just let them drop.

## The most common mistake

The most common mistake is using a weight that's too heavy and bouncing it up with momentum. If you're bouncing the weight, the momentum is doing the work, not your rear delts.

Rear delts are a small muscle. So go light, and make every rep slow and strict. Slow and strict with a light weight is going to do a lot more for you than fast and sloppy with a heavy one.

## Sets and reps

Do three sets of 15 at the end of each workout. It doesn't take long, and you can add it on to whatever workout you're already doing.

Give it a couple of months of consistent work, and you should start to see a difference.
