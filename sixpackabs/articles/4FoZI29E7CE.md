Hammer curls work your brachialis, which is a separate muscle from your biceps. So if you want bigger arms, you need to do hammer curls and regular curls.

Here's how to do it. Hold the dumbbells vertical and curl them up like a regular bicep curl, keeping them vertical the whole time. Use a lighter weight and go at a slow, controlled pace so the tension never comes off the muscle.

## Why you need hammer curls and regular curls

Hammer curls work your brachialis. The brachialis is a separate muscle from your biceps in your arms, and it's not the same muscle you're working when you do a regular curl.

So regular curls alone don't cover it. If you're only doing regular curls, you're missing a whole muscle. If you want bigger arms, do both types of curls.

If you want to see the other half, here's [how I do regular bicep curls](/videos/how-to-do-bicep-curls-one-arm-or-both-arms/).

## How to do a hammer curl

1. Grab the dumbbells in both hands and hold them vertically.
2. Curl the weight up like a regular bicep curl, but with the weight held up and down.
3. Keep the weight as vertical as possible the whole time.
4. Lower it slowly, under control.

You can do one arm at a time or two arms at a time. Either way works.

## The most common hammer curl mistake

The most common mistake is letting the weight get horizontal at the top and at the bottom of the rep.

When the weight gets horizontal, that takes the tension off your muscle. And it basically gives you a rest between each rep, which is exactly what you don't want.

So here's the fix. Use a lighter weight, and keep it as vertical as you can the entire rep. That's how you maintain good time under tension, and that's how you get the most benefit from the exercise. And [the same thing goes for swinging your curls](/videos/stop-swinging-your-bicep-curls/).

## Light, vertical, slow

So remember, use a light weight, hold it vertical the whole time, and go at a slow, controlled pace. Never let the tension off your muscle.
