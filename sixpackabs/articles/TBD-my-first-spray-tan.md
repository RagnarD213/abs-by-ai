## What a spray tan costs

I bought a premium spray tan. The base tan was about $80, and the contouring, which is extra shadowing on the abs, was about $20. So this was about $100 altogether. You can get a spray tan for about $50 if you skip the contouring and go to a little bit of a cheaper studio.

## Drying time and the first shower

One downside of the spray tan is that you can't take a shower for a while after you get it. For the type of tan I got, a rapid drying tan, I needed to wait 8 hours. For other types of tans, you'll need to wait a full 24 hours. That means you're going to sleep with your tan on, and that means you're going to destroy your sheets.

So I recommend the rapid dry tan. Get it early in the day and then shower before you get into bed at night.

Here's how the showering works:

1. In that first shower, no soap, no washcloth, no scrubbing. Just rinse off.
2. There's going to be a bunch of brown water that comes off. That is normal. That's just the cosmetic color they use so they can apply it evenly. It doesn't mean your tan is going away.
3. After that, you can start showering normally with soap.
4. For the life of the tan, no washcloths, no exfoliating face scrubs, and no chemical exfoliants like glycolic acid or salicylic acid.
5. Stay as gentle as you can with the soap you're using and with how you scrub.

## How long it lasts

From what I've been told, these tans last about 7 days. On day five, mine still looked about as good as the day I got it.

Very pale people who go much darker than what's underneath, it's going to wear off faster, in three to four days, because those small patches that wear off are noticeable. On the other hand, if you have naturally tan skin and you keep the tan subtle, it's going to last a little bit longer.

Water wears it off faster too. The pool, the lake, the beach. For me, this is the biggest downside, because I'm in the water all the time.

## The biggest pro: no sun damage

The most important pro of spray tanning, in my opinion, is that you get tan while avoiding sun damage. Even though a natural tan can look healthy, it is not healthy. It is sun damage. Skin cancer is one of the most common cancers. And over the years, the sun makes your skin look older and more leathery.

A spray tan gets you the look without any of that.

If you have that type of ultra pale white skin, tanning naturally is probably not in the cards for you. You're just going to get a sunburn, you're just going to get freckles and blemishes. So if you want more definition, you have to get a spray tan. Just keep in mind that you only need to go a little bit darker than you are right now.

Now, if you're strapped for funds, a spray tan is a lower priority than a great gym membership or a home gym or a meal prep service. Buy that stuff first. At least do the tanning wipes or the tanning spray so that way you can look acceptable with clothes on. Then later on, once you already have the body to show off, invest in the spray tan.

If your money substantially depends on your appearance, if you're going on camera, if you're dealing with clients in person all the time, then that small investment will pay dividends.

Most of you guys are going to be in the middle. So do the spray tan sometimes, maybe in the winter when you can't naturally tan, maybe for a photo shoot or a special occasion like a wedding.
