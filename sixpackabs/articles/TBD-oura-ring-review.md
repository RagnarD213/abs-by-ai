Is the Oura Ring worth it? For most people, yes. I've worn mine for about a year and a half, I paid for it myself, and nobody sponsored this review. The short version: everybody needs a sleep tracker, and if you can afford one, the Oura Ring is the best one I've tried. It has real downsides, and WHOOP is a fair alternative, so here is the honest good, bad and ugly.

## Why I started tracking my sleep

When I was in my 30s, sleep was a big struggle for me. I'd go to bed at 1 a.m., wake up at 7 or 8, get five or six hours, and then lean on caffeine all morning. I thought that was normal. In my 20s I got away with it. Past 38, when I was getting back in shape, it stopped working: my workouts and nutrition were decent, and my sleep was the weak link.

Sleep is as important as your workouts and your nutrition, and it shows up in how you look. Sleep badly and your cortisol runs higher, you eat worse, you train less intensely and everything feels harder. Now that I sleep better, working out and eating right are both easier. Tracking is how you take it seriously.

## What a sleep tracker shows you

Sleep is not just a matter of hours in bed. A tracker makes three things obvious:

- **Timing.** When you go to bed matters. Being asleep between about 10 p.m. and 2 a.m. gets you more out of your sleep, so for most people an earlier bedtime helps, even with the same total hours.
- **Deep sleep.** This is where you recover, mostly early in the night.
- **REM sleep.** Important for your body and your brain. Things like alcohol can wreck your REM sleep without changing how long you sleep.

Buying a sleep tracker is like going from never stepping on a scale to weighing yourself every day. You can finally see what's affecting your sleep: alcohol, eating late, stress. So buy one, even if it isn't an Oura.

## What the Oura Ring does well

**It's on your finger, not your wrist.** I tried my Apple Watch as a sleep tracker for a night. It tracked my sleep accurately, but the bulky watch itself woke me up, and I sleep on my side. I considered a WHOOP, but it's still on the wrist. Studies I've seen say finger-based trackers are more accurate than wrist-based ones, partly because a ring can't slide around the way a watch does.

**Wear it on your index finger or thumb.** I bought mine for my ring finger, which was a mistake. Index finger or thumb is more accurate and more comfortable, because neighboring fingers aren't pressing against it when you flex your hand. With this generation it's comfortable enough to wear all day and lift weights in.

**No screen, no light.** A watch screen can wake you up at night, especially if you check your texts. The ring has nothing to look at.

**The app is the best I've used.** You pay a subscription, but you get clear sleep metrics, heart rate variability (HRV, a recovery marker), calories burned and your accumulated sleep debt. An Apple Watch alone won't give you all of that.

## The downsides

1. **Cost.** The newest ring is about $400. My view: if it improves your sleep even one or two percent, that's worth it. If $400 is a stretch, buy the previous generation used. People sell them for roughly $150 to $250, and it tracks nearly as well at night. Spend something, because the data is the point.
2. **The subscription.** It's $5.99 a month or $69.99 a year, and I take the yearly plan. I'm fine paying for the best data, the best app, and the device that disrupts my sleep the least.
3. **Bulk on the older generation.** The last generation was uncomfortable enough that I wore my Apple Watch during the day. The newest one fixes most of that.
4. **The advice after a bad night.** The app tells you to take it easy after poor sleep. I disagree. I'd train harder, but stick to low-risk exercises so you don't get hurt. Skip the high-risk lifts like cleans on days you're short on sleep.

## Oura Ring vs Apple Watch vs WHOOP vs Fitbit

- **Fitbit:** I wouldn't buy one for sleep. It's built mainly for workouts, it's on your wrist, and the sleep data and app are weaker.
- **Apple Watch:** the best daytime fitness tracker I know: live heart rate, zone two cardio, steps and workouts. But as a sleep tracker its accuracy, screen and bulk work against you. My setup is both: Oura at night, Apple Watch by day.
- **WHOOP:** the best alternative. It's thinner than a watch, has no screen, can be worn on your bicep (handy for jiu-jitsu, where I can't wear a watch), has a lower upfront cost. Some people prefer it, even for sleeping.

If you want a sleep tracker, pick one of those two. I prefer the Oura Ring for accuracy and a more useful app, and I'd recommend it to most people if money isn't a constraint.

## What it showed me about my own life

Two surprises. First, my sleep was clearly worse on nights I drank, especially more than two drinks close to bedtime. Second, fast driving late at night, which I used to do dropping off a girlfriend, spiked my adrenaline and hurt my sleep. Now I let the car drive, listen to a boring podcast and wind down.

The most useful discovery was about stress. During one rough relationship, my sleep score fell off a cliff, and it was among the worst I'd recorded. I realized the stress was costing me real sleep and ended it. Your data may point somewhere different, but it's worth noticing which people and habits show up on your worst nights.

## So, should you buy one?

For most people, yes. Everyone should have a sleep tracker, and the Oura Ring and WHOOP are my top two. Oura wins on accuracy (finger placement) and the app. WHOOP wins on workout tracking, comfort when gripping or typing, and a lower upfront price.
