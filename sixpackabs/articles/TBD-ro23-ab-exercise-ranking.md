Regular crunches and regular sit-ups are at the bottom of my ab exercise list. Regular planks and reverse crunches are in the middle. Above those are the stronger ab exercises I actually do myself, and my number one is a three-exercise workout done back to back.

If you've done hundreds of crunches and you still can't see your abs, start by looking at what you're doing. Doing more and more of an easy exercise isn't how I'd choose to train my abs.

## The worst: regular crunches and regular sit-ups

Crunches and sit-ups are the most popular ab exercises in the world. But regular crunches and regular sit-ups are the two worst on my list.

The problem is that they're easy for guys who are intermediate and advanced. Once you're doing more than 20 reps, you're grinding out easy reps instead of giving your abs a challenging exercise. As I put it in the video, they start just being shitty cardio.

I want my ab exercises to build muscle. If I can keep doing an exercise over and over because it's too easy, I'd rather replace it with something harder. Every other exercise on this list beats regular crunches and regular sit-ups.

So if those are your whole ab routine, replace them. You don't need to keep doing them just because they're the exercises everybody knows.

## The middle: regular planks and reverse crunches

A regular plank is a fine place for a beginner to start. Reverse crunches are another beginner option, especially for working the lower part of your abs.

But there's a reason I put them in the middle. An exercise can be good for a beginner without being the exercise I'd pick for an average or advanced guy.

With the regular plank, I don't want to keep progressing by holding the same position longer and longer. I'd rather move to a more challenging ab exercise.

So regular planks and reverse crunches are mid. They're pretty good, and they're a place to start. But they're not the top tier.

## The top-tier ab exercises

These are the ones I actually do myself. If you're choosing ab exercises, these are the ones I'd have you concentrate on instead of regular crunches and regular sit-ups.

### Cable crunches

I love cable crunches because they put continuous tension on your abs. With other ab exercises, you get a break at the top and the bottom of each rep. With cable crunches, your abs keep working. That's why they're up here in the top tier.

### Leg raises

Leg raises hit the lower part of your abs, which is the hardest part to get to show. They're another exercise I'd pick over regular crunches. If you're choosing from this list, leg raises belong with the stronger ab exercises.

### Decline sit-ups

Decline sit-ups are one of my favorites. I know I just put sit-ups at the bottom, but doing them on a decline bench makes every rep far harder. That's a different exercise from grinding out ordinary floor sit-ups because those have become too easy.

### The ab wheel

The ab wheel is one of the hardest ab exercises you can do, and it's inexpensive. You don't need an expensive piece of equipment to make your abs work hard. I've also got [the ab wheel guide](/videos/the-17-ab-wheel-beats-every-crunch/) if you want to see more about this one.

### Toe touches

For toe touches, lie on your back, put your legs straight up and reach for your toes. It's a simple movement, and it's one of the exercises I picked for the workout I'm going to show you next.

### The vacuum

Push the air out, suck your stomach in as far as it goes and hold it. This trains the deep muscle that pulls your waist in. It belongs on this list along with the exercises working the front and sides of your abs.

Pick two from this tier and do them at the end of your workout. I do at least one every day. You can also use [the follow-along ab workout](/videos/the-ultimate-1-minute-ab-workout-follow-along-no-talking/) to see the exercises together.

## Number one: my three-exercise ab workout

Number one on my list is toe touches, V-sit twists and Spider-Man planks, back to back as drop sets. First you do the toe touches, then you do your V-sit twists, and then you finish it off with the Spider-Man planks. Go straight from one exercise to the next.

This is the ab workout I do the most. If I could only do one set of exercises for my abs, this would be it.

Here's why I love putting them together. Toe touches work your rectus abdominis, the six-pack muscle in the front of your abs. V-sit twists hit your obliques, the muscles on the sides. And Spider-Man planks hit your obliques plus the deep muscle underneath, the transverse abdominis.

So instead of hunting for one perfect exercise, this workout hits those muscles back to back.

### Why the order matters

The hardest exercise comes first. With the toe touches, you're exhausting the six-pack muscle in the front of your abs. Then you go to the V-sit twists, which put more of the work on your obliques.

That exercise is easier on the six-pack muscle, but it's still challenging because you've already worked it with the toe touches. You're changing which muscles you're concentrating on as your abs get tired.

Then you finish with the Spider-Man planks. These put more of the work on the deep abdominal muscle. Your six-pack muscle is doing less than it was at the beginning, but after the other exercises, it's still super challenging.

That's why I put these exercises in this order and do them immediately back to back.

## Building abs and revealing them

Remember, none of these exercises will burn the fat covering your abs. Losing your belly fat is what reveals them. These exercises build the abdominal muscle underneath, and once the fat comes off, that muscle is what makes your abs show up thick and defined.
