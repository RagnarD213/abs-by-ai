If I woke up 30 pounds heavier tomorrow, I'm confident I could lose it and get my abs back within 3 months. Here is the exact plan, in the order I would do it.

The short version: take as much time off as I can, start Zepbound in week one (with my doctor), work out every single morning, use a meal prep service, track my calories with AI, eat at least 0.8 grams of protein per pound, weigh myself every day, and then build a plan so I never have to do this again. I'm not guessing about this. Two years ago I was about 200 pounds at 5'7" with no abs. Today I'm 175 and I have abs at 40. It took me a lot longer than it should have, and this is how I would do it if I could start over.

Some of this will sound extreme. Most fitness channels tell you to make small, sustainable changes and lose a pound a week. I think that advice is bad, and it's the reason most people following it are still fat. With 30 pounds to lose, a pound a week is seven months, and almost nobody stays motivated that long. It is far easier to go all in for 90 days and get it over with.

## 1. Quit my job, or take the longest vacation I can

Most people try to squeeze fat loss into whatever time and energy is left after work, driving, and family. There usually isn't any left, so it never happens. If I were 30 pounds overweight I would flip it: fat loss becomes the number one priority for 90 days and everything else fits around it.

Think of it as an investment. When you're carrying extra fat you're tired every afternoon and you think less clearly. If you give up a few months of work and come back lean, I believe you'll be productive enough to make up for it within a year. If you can take a sabbatical, take it. If you have vacation days saved, spend every one of them on this.

## 2. Get a Zepbound prescription in the first week

Zepbound makes rapid fat loss much easier. It is a once-a-week injection that slows how fast food leaves your stomach, so you get full on less food and stay full longer, and it works on the part of your brain that drives appetite, so the constant thoughts about food mostly go away. To lose 30 pounds in 90 days you have to eat a lot less than you burn every day, and without help that takes a huge amount of willpower.

I take it myself. I went from 192 to 175 in about two months. I also think starting an injection makes people take their workouts and diet more seriously. I'm not a doctor and this is not medical advice, and many doctors are conservative about it, so talk to yours before you decide. My personal opinion is that a low dose has big benefits for most men. I intend to stay on a small dose for life.

## 3. Work out first thing every morning, 7 days a week

Short daily workouts beat occasional long ones. When you work out every day there is no daily decision about whether today is a gym day, so you just do it, even on weekends and on vacation. If all you can manage at first is 10 or 15 minutes, that's fine. It's the hour and a half sessions that get skipped.

Do it first thing. At 8 in the morning nobody is calling you and the kids are asleep. By 6 at night you're tired and something came up at work. I work out every morning, seven days a week, for exactly that reason.

## 4. Get a meal prep service

I order from a meal prep service in Austin for about $120 a week. You skip groceries, cooking, and cleanup, which gives you hours back. The bigger reason is that every meal comes with a nutrition label and lands between 300 and 500 calories, so tracking is easier and more accurate and you never wonder what's for dinner. The trade-off is you're not eating premium protein, but it's convenient, portioned, and costs only a little more than groceries. Meals run about $9 or less, and plenty of guys spend more than that on a burrito every day. If you don't live in Austin, ask Claude or ChatGPT to find a service in your city.

## 5. Track your calories with AI

To lose about two and a half pounds a week you need a big deficit, and you won't hit it by guessing. If you never look at your bank account you'll probably be poor, and calories work the same way. I used to be against tracking because weighing food and looking up ingredients was too much work. Now you take a picture and get the calories, protein, carbs and fat in seconds. It isn't perfect, but it's far better than not tracking. With meal prep, you just photograph the label. [You don't need a food scale anymore, just a photo](/videos/you-dont-need-a-food-scale-anymore-just-a-photo/).

## 6. Eat 0.8 grams of protein per pound every day

At 210 pounds that's about 170 grams a day, so every meal needs a big serving of meat, fish, or eggs, around 8 ounces at lunch and dinner, plus protein snacks like hard-boiled eggs, sardines, rotisserie chicken, or protein cups. Keep carbs limited so it fits your calorie budget.

There are two reasons. First, you don't want to lose muscle. When you lose fat this fast your body burns muscle too if you let it, and it's an even bigger risk on Zepbound because you aren't hungry. In a big study on the medication, about a quarter of the weight people lost was lean mass. Hit your protein and lift weights and you can hold on to your muscle. Second, protein fills you up more than anything else, and your body burns roughly 20 to 30 percent of its calories just digesting it.

## 7. Weigh yourself every single day

Weigh in every morning after using the bathroom and before you eat or drink, same scale, no exceptions, even on vacation. It's the only way to know whether the plan is working. In one study, people who weighed themselves daily lost about 13 pounds more over six months than people who didn't.

Your weight will bounce 2 or 3 pounds day to day from water and salt, so ignore any single day. Compare your 7-day average to the week before. If it's dropping by about 2 pounds or more, change nothing. If it stalls for a full week, cut 200 calories a day and keep going. Most guys never adjust anything and hope the scale moves.

## 8. Make a plan to never do this again

Maintaining is far easier than losing. Once you have your abs you can eat more, have a drink on the weekend, and relax a little. And the way people end up 30 pounds heavier is by not weighing themselves, so they gain 5, then 10, then 20 pounds before they notice. Keep weighing in daily. If you ever get more than 3 pounds above your goal weight, go straight back to cutting calories. Losing 3 pounds takes a week or two. Losing 30 is 90 days of sacrifice.

With my doctor, I would also stay on a very low dose of Zepbound, around 1 to 1.5 milligrams, to help keep the weight off, and bump it up and lower calories if the scale ever creeps. That is what I'm doing now, and I haven't regained anything since I lost my belly fat.
