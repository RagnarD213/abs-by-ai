You can still hold onto your belly fat while eating in a calorie deficit. Calories decide how much weight you lose, but seven other things decide whether that weight is fat or muscle, and how fast the fat comes off. If you screw these up, a perfect deficit gets a lot less out of you.

Here are the seven, in order, with the studies behind each one.

## 1. Sleep

This one has the best proof of the seven. Researchers at the University of Chicago put people on the same diet with the same calorie deficit. For two weeks they slept 8.5 hours a night, and for another two weeks only 5.5. On short sleep they lost 55% less fat and 60% more muscle. Same people, same food, same calories.

If you're dieting on 5 or 6 hours of sleep, you're giving up more than half of your fat loss. I keep my bedroom at 63 degrees, I keep a consistent bedtime and I take glycine every night. Get 7 to 8 hours and you get the full fat loss you're already working for.

## 2. Alcohol

Your body treats alcohol like a poison, so it burns it off before it burns anything else. In one study, about two drinks' worth of alcohol dropped fat burning by 73% for the next several hours. Alcohol also wrecks your sleep and lowers your testosterone, which are two other factors on this list. The less you drink, the faster your belly fat comes off at the same calories.

## 3. Hormones

For most guys, hormones are not the main reason they're fat. But two of them matter. When testosterone is low, your body stores more fat around your belly and holds on to less muscle, even at the same calories. And high cortisol, your stress hormone, tells your body to store fat in your belly specifically.

If you think your testosterone is low, get a blood test. It's cheap, and you'll know your real number instead of guessing. The normal range doctors use is very wide: a guy at 300 and a guy at 900 are both called normal, and those two guys don't feel the same. I'm not a doctor, so talk to yours.

## 4. Meal timing

Researchers at Harvard's teaching hospital had people eat the same meals either early in the day or about four hours later. The late eaters were about twice as likely to be hungry the next day and burned about 60 fewer calories a day. So stop eating about three hours before bed.

Fasting helps for a different reason: it changes your behavior. When University of Illinois researchers limited obese adults to a 4 or 6 hour eating window, they ate about 550 fewer calories a day without trying. In a year-long study, the group that fasted until noon ate about 425 fewer calories a day and did just as well as the people counting every calorie. I fast until 2 every day.

## 5. Protein

Your body burns about 20 to 30% of the calories in protein just digesting it, versus 5 to 10% for carbs. So 200 calories of chicken and 200 calories of bread are not the same thing inside your body.

The bigger reason is muscle. Researchers at McMaster University put two groups of young men on the same brutal diet and the same hard workouts for four weeks. One group ate twice as much protein. The high-protein group lost about 10.5 pounds of fat and gained 2.5 pounds of muscle. The lower-protein group lost about 8 pounds of fat and gained no muscle. If you're on a weight loss medication like Zepbound, this matters even more, because those drugs take muscle off along with the fat. Get a big serving of meat, fish or eggs at every meal and eat the protein first.

## 6. Exercise

Cardio does less than you think. A 30-minute jog burns about 300 calories, roughly one protein bar, and your body makes up for it with more appetite. In a Pennington Biomedical study, the group doing the most cardio burned about 1,700 extra calories a week, which should have meant about 9.5 pounds lost. They lost about 3.5, because 9 out of 10 of them ate roughly 120 extra calories a day to make up for it.

Keep doing cardio for your heart. But the exercise that matters for fat loss is lifting weights, and not because of the calories you burn. Lifting while you diet tells your body to keep its muscle and burn the fat instead. Lose muscle and you'll have to bulk again later, gain fat, and cut again. Lift religiously while you lose fat and you avoid that cycle.

## 7. Daily movement

The Mayo Clinic overfed a group of people by 1,000 calories a day for eight weeks. One person gained less than a pound of fat and another gained over nine pounds, from the same extra food. The difference was how much they moved when they weren't exercising: walking, standing, taking the stairs and fidgeting.

When you cut calories, your body quietly makes you move less, and that can cancel out a big piece of your deficit. Set a minimum step count and hit it no matter what. 8 to 10,000 steps a day is a good target. Walk after dinner, take calls on your feet and park at the back of the lot.

## The short version

Calories are the most important factor in how much you lose. These seven decide whether it's belly fat or muscle and how fast it comes off. Sleep 7 to 8 hours in a cold room, cut way back on alcohol, get your testosterone checked if you think it's low, stop eating three hours before bed, eat protein at every meal, lift weights and keep your steps up. Do those while you eat in a deficit and your belly fat comes off a lot faster.
