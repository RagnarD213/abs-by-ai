## A. Eat Protein First To Keep Muscle While Losing Fat

Food rule · about 57 seconds · 5:18.672–6:07.331 + 6:16.252–6:24.611

One concrete food rule, a clear chicken-versus-bread comparison, and a muscle-retention payoff.

Let me give you two reasons why protein matters outside of calories. The first one is that your body burns about 20-30% of the calories in protein just digesting it. With carbs, it's 5-10%. So 200 calories of chicken and 200 calories of bread aren't the same thing once they're inside your body. The second reason is muscle. Researchers at McMaster University put two groups of young men on the same brutal diet with the same hard workouts for four weeks. The only difference was protein. One group ate twice as much as the other. The high protein group lost about 10.5 pounds of fat and they gained 2.5 pounds of muscle. The lower protein group lost about 8 pounds of fat and they didn't gain any muscle at all. Those were the same calories and the high protein guys ended up with a completely different body.

*[Skip the medication aside at 6:08–6:15.]*

So every meal you eat needs a big serving of meat, fish or eggs. Get your protein in first then fit your carbs into whatever calories you have left.

## B. Eat Less Without Counting Every Calorie

Food rule · about 52 seconds · 4:18.299–5:02.198 + 5:05.499–5:13.738

One tactic: shorten the eating window. It has a complete study explanation and a practical close.

Researchers at the University of Illinois gave a group of obese adults one rule. They could only eat inside a 4 or 6 hour window each day. Nobody counted calories, and nobody told them what to eat. After two months, they were eating about 550 fewer calories a day without even trying. And they lost about 3% of their body weight compared to the group that ate whenever they wanted. Then they ran a bigger study for a full year. One group fasted until noon every single day and never counted a single calorie. Another group counted every calorie they ate. The fasting group ate about 425 fewer calories per day and they lost about 10 pounds more than the people who changed nothing. They did just as well as the calorie counters.

*[Skip “So fasting changes your behavior.” at 5:02–5:05; the following sentence explains it.]*

When you've got fewer hours to eat, you eat less and you don't have to fight yourself all day to do it. That's one more reason why I fast until 2 every day.

## C. Keep Your Steps Up While You Diet

Other: daily movement · about 58 seconds · 8:14.985–8:41.425 + 8:48.905–9:20.444

Explains why movement falls during a diet, then gives a step target and several easy ways to hit it.

The Mayo Clinic overfed a group of people by a thousand calories a day for eight weeks. Everybody got the same extra food. But the fat they gained was wildly different. One person gained less than a pound of fat. Another person gained over nine pounds. That's a ten times difference from the same extra calories. So what was the difference? It was how much they moved around during the day. I don't mean exercise. I mean walking, standing, taking the stairs, and even fidgeting.

*[Skip 8:42–8:48, including the transition “And here’s the part that gets you when you’re dieting.”]*

When you cut your calories, your body quietly makes you move less. You sit more, you take the elevator, and you don't even notice it happening. That can cancel out a big piece of your deficit. So here's what I would do. Set a minimum number of steps per day and hit it no matter what. I would say 8-10,000 is a good target for most of you guys. Walk after dinner, take your phone calls on your feet, and park at the back of the parking lot. Work at a standing desk. It all adds up and it protects your deficit you're working so hard for.

## D. Stop Eating Back Your Cardio

Other: exercise and appetite · about 57 seconds · 6:29.692–7:27.151

Explains compensation after cardio and ends on the food-tracking mistake viewers can fix. This is an alternative to the first three, not a form tutorial.

A lot of guys are doing cardio thinking that this will make them leaner, but if you do the math, cardio doesn't make that much difference unless you're doing huge amounts of it. A 30 minute jog burns about 300 calories, which is about one protein bar. And on top of that, your body makes up for it by burning less the rest of the day and by increasing your appetite. Researchers at Pennington Biomedical proved this. They put overweight adults on supervised cardio for six months and nobody told them to change their diet. The group doing the most cardio was burning about 1,700 extra calories a week. Based on the math, those people should have lost about 9.5 pounds, but they only lost about 3.5. The researchers measured where the rest went, and it came from eating more. 9 out of 10 people in that group made up for their cardio by about 120 extra calories of food a day. Cardio makes you hungrier, and it also makes you feel like you earned a treat. So if you're not tracking your calories, you eat a big piece of that workout right back.
