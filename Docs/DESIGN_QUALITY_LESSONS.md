# Design quality lessons from the cart comparison

Recorded October 2, 2026. Dan preferred Opus's Healthy Back Institute cart from Cart Mockups R1 over Codex Astra's version. He described the difference as close but meaningful, especially given Astra's higher cost to him. This is a design preference verdict, not a measured conversion result. Opus is the current benchmark for this work. Astra has not demonstrated a premium worth paying for on this task.

- Opus source and notes: `Media/research/cart-mockups-r1/` and `Media/research/cart-mockups-r2/`, especially `project/B-HealthyBack.dc.html`, `project/B-Desktop.dc.html` and `project/Notes-B.dc.html`.

The tasks were not a controlled model comparison: the briefs differed and the Opus task received a correction to the entry flow. Nevertheless, Dan's chosen result is the working benchmark. Differences in briefs do not excuse failing to verify the current customer journey.

## What to improve

1. Reference fidelity. Opus retained the recognizable offer banner, yellow notice, large circular step numbers, email/order/payment sequence, explicit trial explanation and prominent action. Astra retained blue headings and a value column but weakened several distinctive relationships. When asked to closely model a reference, first map its actual structure, proportions and visual emphasis. Distinguish the faithful version from creative adaptations.
2. Product-specific persuasion. Astra's phrases such as "Your next step" and "A practical place to begin" were generic. Explain the actual appeal with concrete actions and benefits. Dan's chosen headline direction is: "See yourself with abs, then get an AI fitness plan to make it real. Claim your free 7-day trial today!" Use that as evidence of his preference, not a universal headline template.
3. Entry-flow accuracy. Dan clarified that the cart follows the /start buy button. Visitors have not uploaded a photo, generated a goal image or provided body measurements. Astra's prepared-for-Alex goal recap should not become the baseline for that flow. Inspect the preceding screen before designing, even when a handoff supplies a sample profile.
4. Structure and brand are separate decisions. Dan liked Opus's faithful HBI structure and then requested Abs By AI branding. Preserve the selected structure while adapting typography, colors, spacing and imagery to the sales page. A polished alternative structure is not a substitute for the requested one.
5. Mobile needs its own content decisions. Dan removed the second benefit box from phone while retaining it on desktop. Do not simply stack every desktop panel on mobile. Examine what must be visible before payment and what adds repetition.
6. Functional completeness is necessary but insufficient. Plan selection, simulated payment states and screenshots met the delivery brief. They did not establish superior design. Reserve attention for visible hierarchy and copy before expanding shared code, galleries and packaging.

## Process for the next authorized design task

1. Establish the brief in a short internal note: preceding screen, what the visitor knows, exact offer, chosen reference, approved brand assets and required screens. Resolve these from available evidence before asking Dan.
2. Map the reference into sections and specify what each section does. Identify which features are required to make the reference recognizable. Keep product facts independent of competitor offer terms.
3. Develop the composition before the component system. For every requested concept, inspect the opening phone screen and the complete desktop buying area. Each concept needs a distinct structural idea, not just different colors. Finish all requested options before asking Dan to choose.
4. Critique against the best approved example at matching viewport sizes. Compare reference fidelity, brand continuity, product appeal, offer clarity, mobile sequence and visual finish. For each category, write one observable weakness and fix material weaknesses before delivery. Self-scores alone are not proof.
5. Run a separate refinement pass on headline specificity, type scale, dominant action, form spacing, image proportions and unnecessary repetition. Re-inspect the changed views. Preserve real product terms and features while improving presentation.
6. Then verify interactions and responsive behavior and package the result. Reuse existing capture and gallery tooling. Avoid broad research restarts or elaborate infrastructure that does not improve the visible result.
7. Deliver the strongest recommendation with its concrete reason and tradeoff. Record Dan's selection and revisions as reusable preferences, rather than relying on a promise to remember.

## How Astra earns future use

Recommend Opus for similar cart and page design work on the evidence available, unless Dan chooses otherwise. This is scoped to these design tasks, not a blanket routing change for coding, images or video.

If Dan authorizes another comparison, use one bounded representative design with the same brief, assets, viewport sizes and time or usage budget. Show anonymous versions where practical. Dan judges initial quality and required revisions. Record elapsed work, available usage/cost evidence and correction count. Do not invent a dollar estimate when usage is unavailable.

Matching Opus at a higher cost is insufficient. Astra needs a clear visual win, materially less revision effort, or another concrete benefit Dan values. A single win is preliminary; broader routing should follow repeated useful results. Do not launch an unsolicited redesign or paid model contest to prove this plan.
