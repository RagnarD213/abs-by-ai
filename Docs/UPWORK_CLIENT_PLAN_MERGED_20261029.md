## 1. Decision and goal

Use Dan's Upwork profile, plus direct outreach to brands, to get the first paying clients for direct-response video ad work as fast as possible. Then move the work to AI step by step, until Dan spends about 2 hours a day approving what the AI prepared.

**Success by month three:** repeat orders that make money after costs, with Dan's measured time at or under 2 hours a day.

## 3. Facts both plans rest on (Upwork's own pages, checked 2026-10-29)

- **Bots are banned.** The only allowed AI route is Upwork's official connector (`mcp.upwork.com/mcp`). It can search jobs, draft and submit proposals, and read and send messages, but **every write is a draft Dan confirms**. Offers and money are accepted on upwork.com. Searches must be specific tasks, not continuous monitoring. Rate limits: searches 150/hour; proposals 60/hour. AI origin must be disclosed where a client would assume a human wrote something.
- **No feedback building.** No free or discounted work in exchange for reviews, no fake jobs from friends. Upwork deletes "insider" feedback between closely connected accounts. A real, paid trial at a low price is fine.
- **Money:**
  - Pricing: minimum fixed price $5. Service fee 0 to 15% per contract.
  - Connects: $0.15 each; the number per proposal varies by job.
  - Plans: the Basic plan now gives **0** Connects a month. Freelancer Plus is $19.99 for 100 Connects, job alerts, bid insights and 0% fee on Direct Contracts. Dan's account showed both on 2026-10-29.
- **When money is usable:** after Dan submits fixed-price work, the client has up to 14 days to approve (it auto-releases if they do nothing). Then a 5-day security hold applies before withdrawal. **A first sale in week 1 is usable cash in about week 2 to 4.**
- **Testimonials from outside clients are gone.** Upwork's help page says: "We no longer accept new testimonial requests." Codex caught this; Claude's plan was wrong. Outside case studies go in the **Portfolio** instead.
- **Direct Contracts give no reviews.** Bringing an outside client onto Upwork through a Direct Contract protects the payment but adds no feedback, earnings or job count to the profile (Upwork community staff; confirm in setup).

## 5. The offer ladder

|---|---|---|---|
| Trial A | **Ad Hook Fix:** review one ad or script; 5 new hooks, 1 rewritten opening, short reasons; 1 revision | $75 | 2 business days after inputs |
| Trial B | **New Version of Your Ad:** 1 variation up to 60 seconds from supplied footage; 1 format; captions; 1 revision | $150 | 3 business days after inputs |
| Core | Script $350 / New versions $450 / Audit $750 / Finished ad $950+ | as approved | as approved |
| Recurring | **Weekly ad pack:** new hooks, cuts and formats every week for one product (Greg Isenberg's "always-on ad studio"), from the New versions tiers billed monthly | $800 to $1,400 a month to start | weekly |
| Top | Start-to-finish ads plus Google Ads management | $950+ and $2,500/month | as approved |

Rules:
- Trials exclude new filming, multiple formats, strategy and ad management.
- Each trial lists the inputs it needs before work starts.
- The trial price is credited toward any core package ordered within 14 days.
- When a job post asks for full service, bid the full package (Dan's preferred job), not a trial.

**Add a fifth Project Catalog listing** ("Ad Hook Fix and New Version") with the two trials, so cold prospects and Upwork browsers can buy without a proposal. The profile-finish task should add it. Upwork reviews every listing; confirm it accepts a $75 tier.

## 6. Engine 1: Upwork bids (most of week 1 and 2)

- **Finding jobs:** Claude searches through the official connector 3 times a day as a defined task, and also on Freelancer Plus job alerts.
  - Search terms: direct-response video ads, ad scripts, supplement ad scripts, YouTube ad scripts, VSL, UGC ad variations, Meta ad hooks and creative variations, ad editing, ad thumbnails, AI video ads, Google/YouTube Ads management.
  - Skip vlogs, weddings and general channel editing.
- **Scoring (decision aid, not a validated model):** fit 30, clear scope and usable inputs 20, economics 20, client signals 15, repeat potential 15. Shortlist at 75 and above. Unknown facts stay unknown, and credible new clients are not penalised.
  - Reject or clarify: unlimited revisions, big unpaid tests, sales guarantees, a whole marketing job for a tiny fee, and bans on AI methods.
- **Each proposal:**
  1. One specific, true observation about their ad or brief. Claude watches their live ads first; they are public in Google's Ads Transparency Center and Meta's Ad Library.
  2. Three new hook ideas for their product.
  3. The one most relevant portfolio piece, with Dan's exact role and its number.
  4. Deliverable, price and timeline: the trial, or the package that fits.
  5. One question, plus direct answers to screening questions.

  Keep it short. Lead with money results. Never invent an observation, and never claim no AI is used.
- **Dan's part:** read and confirm each proposal in Upwork. Claude reopens the proposal to confirm it is live, and never blindly retries an uncertain submission.
- **Volume:** about 5 strong proposals a day. Boost only the top-scored jobs.

## 7. Engine 2: direct outreach to brands (starts week 1 at low volume)

- **Who:** fitness, supplement, health and info-product brands, plus ad-buying agencies, that clearly spend on YouTube or Meta ads but whose ads all make the same argument. Greg Isenberg's test: the same hook, background and format across most of their live ads.
- **What they get:** a short email with a 2-line teardown of their current ads and 3 new hook ideas taken from their own customer reviews.
  - For the top 2 or 3 a week: a personal video (section 8) and 3 watermarked concept mockups.
- **The ask:** "Try it with a $75 hook fix or a $150 new version," linking the Upwork trial listing. If they would rather pay directly, invoice them outside Upwork (no review, no fee). Do not use a Direct Contract.
- **Free work:** one or two prospects may get free work in exchange for a case study, because this is off Upwork. That case study goes in the Portfolio, never as an Upwork review.
- **Agencies:** one agency can bring several brands, so pitch the weekly ad pack to them.
- **Sending:** a new domain (about $12 a year) with one Google Workspace mailbox (about $7 a month).
  - Start at 5 a day and build to 10. Claude drafts each one.
  - Dan approves the first batch (list and text) once; later batches follow his batch-approval rule.
  - Never send from absbyai.com or his main Gmail. Add a sending tool only if volume needs to pass 20 a day.

## 8. Personal video method

- Use the desk webcam and mic, not the studio set. Being specific beats picture quality.
- Use the Loom free plan or a Mac screen recording uploaded unlisted. The screen shows the prospect's current ad, then the 3 new hook ideas, with Dan's face in the corner.
- Aim for about 60 seconds, one take, no editing.
- Claude writes a 3-line card for each video:
  1. "Hi [name], I watched your [ad]," plus the one problem.
  2. The 3 new angles.
  3. One next step.

  About 5 minutes per video.
- No reused generic opener, and no AI copy of Dan's face or voice presented as personal.

## 11. Checkpoints (review points, not proven thresholds)

**After about 20 Upwork proposals have had a few days for replies, or day 10, whichever is first:**
- **No useful conversations:** change targeting, opening lines or the portfolio piece used.
- **Conversations but no sales:** change scope, trust signals, price or the trial offer.
- **Sales but heavy revisions:** fix intake and delivery before adding volume.
- **Repeat orders that make money:** release up to $150 of reserve for more Connects or a capped 2-week Availability badge test.
- The same check applies to outreach after about 50 emails.

**Day 30:** compare cost per paid contract and Dan's minutes per order across both engines. Put month two's effort where revenue per Dan-hour is highest.

## 12. Training loop and the path to 95% AI

- **From job one,** log every correction Dan makes to a pick, proposal, sample or delivery, plus every missing input, revision and failure.
- **Turn each one into** a rule, an approved example, an intake question or a quality check, kept in a new skill (`.claude/skills/upwork-pitch/`). No custom model is needed. Corey Ganim's report drafts went from 60 to 70% right to near copy-paste by about job 4 to 6 this way.
- **Repeat clients are the strongest route to 95%.** A weekly pack for the same product has the same inputs and standards each week, while new strangers bring new exceptions. That is why the recurring weekly ad pack is the main goal after the trials.
- **What can run reliably now:**
  - **Proven in-house:** hook and script drafts in Dan's voice, ad edits from supplied footage, captions, thumbnails, AI B-roll, Google Ads API reporting.
  - **Least automated:** casting and new filming, and judgement calls on taste.
- **Month two (Dan about 2 hours a day):**
  - **Routine:** a scheduled Claude routine searches 3 times a day, drafts proposals and samples, drafts outreach and follow-ups, and prepares delivery drafts. Dan clears one approval queue a day.
  - **Alerts:** only for replies, offers, deadlines, approval-ready top jobs and failures.
  - **Safeguards:** one submission owner, spending and retry limits, and a pause switch.
- **How "95%" is measured:** Dan's minutes per order and per week across everything: finding, reviewing, client messages, production review, revisions, fixes. Never "AI wrote the first draft". Upwork's required confirmations stay even when everything else is automated.

## 13. Tracking

- **One record per Upwork job ID or outreach prospect:**
  - The opportunity: link, date found, offer type, fit reasons, Connects, fee.
  - The pitch: proposal version, sample, whether a video was sent, submission confirmation.
  - The result: reply, contract value, delivery minutes, revisions, costs, next action.
- **Separate states:** proposal sent, conversation, contract funded, work submitted, accepted, released, available, withdrawn.
- **Weekly:**
  - **Activity:** proposals and emails sent, reply rates, paid contracts.
  - **Money:** revenue, Connects and costs, cost per paid contract.
  - **Time:** Dan's hours.
- **Rough benchmark to beat** (vendor data, not a forecast): about 7 replies per 100 proposals.
