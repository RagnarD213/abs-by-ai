Dan, 2026-10-07, after editing the 40 scripts for the 10/17 shoot: occasionally a personal example improves a script, but overall "these facts in the scripts actually made the scripts worse". Claude had "wedged in a fact about me where it wasn't really relevant", in "a very clumsy, rule-based, deterministic AI slop type of way". His example: in a section about going to bed at a consistent time, giving his own bedtime. He wants the facts used the way he uses them: only when relevant, where it flows and makes sense.

**Why:** the facts on record ([[dan-personal-facts-for-scripts]]) were being treated as a checklist to work into every script. The same fact (glycine and the Oura score, the $8 to $9 meal prep price, the salmon meal, the injection day) showed up in five or six scripts and read as a template.

**How to apply:** the facts are context first, script material second. Before keeping one, ask whether the paragraph loses its proof or its how-to without it. If not, cut it. Do not repeat one fact across a batch of scripts. Claude's own reading of his feedback: a fact earns its place when it answers "how do you know?" or "what exactly do I do?" for the point being made. Rule text lives in `.claude/skills/_shared/WRITING-RULES.md` section 2 and `/scriptfromoutline` section Q. Related: [[watch-this-video-if-sparingly]], [[script-zero-edit-lessons]].
