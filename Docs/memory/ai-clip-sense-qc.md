Dan, 2026-10-13, reviewing the RO-08 round 2 film (he rejected three of eight AI clips that the editor and two independent reviews had passed): "when he grabs the muffin out at the end, all the donuts just disappear, vaporize, and go nowhere. It doesn't make sense. We need better quality control on these clips, so things that look like AI slop like this get caught in quality control and don't make it to my review." On water leaping out of glasses for a stomach rumble: "It makes no physics sense what's happening in this video. It doesn't support what I'm saying. These types of clips should be caught in QC going forward." On a third: "I don't think that illustrates the idea well, or the joke really lands here."

Same reply: "Maybe a max of 5 clips per video, but keep coming up with lots of ideas. I'm just going to reject more of them." And: "If we hit budget but the clips are still having obvious problems, then I would rather you ask for authorization for more budget than ship something with shitty clips like this." He also raised the standard AI budget for long-form content to $15; he said "$15 per clip", recorded as $15 per long-form video until he confirms (asked 2026-10-13).

**Why:** I had written both oddities on the review page and shipped them anyway, and the reviewers were only asked about frame faults, labels and lip sync.

**How to apply:** Every AI take, at playback speed, gets three questions from the editor and again from the independent reviewer (in its brief, one answer per clip): does everything obey cause and effect (nothing vanishes, appears or moves by itself); with the sound off would a stranger say what it shows; does it show the line, and does the joke land in one viewing. A time jump is a cut between two natural shots, never a fast-forward inside one take. A clip that fails is regenerated, trimmed to the part that works, or dropped for the camera scene.
