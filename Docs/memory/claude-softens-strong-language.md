On 2026-10-16 Dan took the Round 2 blind voice test (17 of 20 right) and named his top tell: Claude moderates his
language. Where he writes "ugly" and "grossly obese", Claude writes "unattractive" and "severely overweight";
"lied" becomes "overstated"; "worship the ground you walk on" becomes "love you". His words: being afraid to use
authentic language "is one of the biggest Claude tells", Claude is "basically inhibited" around words like "slut"
and "whore" in the context his book uses them, and defaults to a feminist point of view instead of his manosphere
one. Second tell: weak direct-response language. His copy says "whopping 50% savings", "BLOWN AWAY", "the pounds
melt off", "a single cent", and he wants Claude's weaker versions corrected. The one exception he named: careful
wording on disease claims in ads (a sensitive Google Ads category), never on fitness results.

**Why:** these words are what make copy read as his and as strong selling. Softening them reads as AI and as weak.

**How to apply:** in any content plan, outline, brief or script for Dan, keep his exact charged and hyperbolic words;
never neutralize a label or a claim in an intermediate step (the writer copies whatever wording it is given). My own
finding, not his words: on the bench every softened word he flagged came from Claude's neutral content brief, so
the softening happens in the in-between steps as much as in the final draft. **Confirmed 2026-10-16:** right after the test, Dan had Claude write a short script, "never tolerate a fat wife or
girlfriend", in his language. Verdict: not exactly his, but "significantly better", the Claude tells removed, "a good
example of how to use my language when dealing with this type of topic". The script and what made it work are in
`.claude/skills/_shared/voice/profanity-and-controversy.md` (last section; Claude's text, never a bank sample).
