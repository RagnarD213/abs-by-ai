I want to keep the words 'drugs' and 'needles' off the page, and also any other potential poison words for Google Ads that might trigger a compliance problem."

What was applied to the sales letter (Draft v2, Doc `1klwTUdTl5q_17MTA5c1Dtv-Agm_-B3rBpxGQPOj1SCI`, Part F lists every change):

- "testosterone" became "T" everywhere, including subheads and study cards.
- Also taken off as poison words (my list, Dan asked for "any other", he has not reviewed it line by line): prescription, injections, TRT / replacement therapy, estrogen, aromatase, steroid, disease names (cardiovascular disease, diabetes, osteoporosis), dying, "depressed", "Chinese sweatshop".
- Cut from the page, kept in the film: the two risk statistics with their study cards, and the whole TRT question.
- The study list shows the 31 studies without paper titles (the titles carry the words): authors, journal, year, volume, pages, DOI, PMID.
- Left in on purpose: cortisol, hormone, the love-life wording, "FDA-registered", the footer's Food and Drug Administration sentence.

Compliance pages (Dan, 2026-11-17): FAQ, How It Works, Contact Us, Privacy Policy, Terms, Shipping and Returns, About Us, Ingredients and Disclaimer (nine pages) are written "purely for compliance", for the Google Ads policy team only, informational and never salesy. Draft Doc `1QErPnqlCvhgdwfXYut6JL-h6O_iLspkjw2FFznYQeLs`; Google's rules and the build rules are in `Docs/GAT_COMPLIANCE_PAGES_20261117.md`. **Why:** Google Ads compliance on a paid-traffic page.

**How to apply:** every Great Again T page a paid click can land on (sales page, quiz, checkout, upsell pages), their captions, alt text and text inside screenshots.
