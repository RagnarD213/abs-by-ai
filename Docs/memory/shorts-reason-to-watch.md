A Short must hand the viewer something they can use — a tactic, a correction, a
technique, a myth busted. **A clip whose only content is Dan's own result ("I got
leaner with no gym") is not a Short, it's a brag, and Dan will kill it.**

Established 2026-08-17: `v6-short1_gained-muscle-in-quarantine` was cut, captioned,
and scheduled as the first Instagram/Facebook post. Dan watched it and pulled it —
*"I don't really think it has any value or has any reason to watch it. It's just more
me bragging."* Replaced with `v3-short6_vacuum-exercises` ("DO THIS INSTEAD OF
CRUNCHES"), which hands the viewer a specific exercise that replaces one they're
already doing.

**Why:** Dan's own transformation is the *proof* behind the product, not the payload
of a piece of content. A viewer scrolling past has no reason to care that he got
leaner; they have every reason to care what to do instead of crunches. The brag also
reads worse on Instagram and Facebook than on YouTube, where the audience arrived
already interested in him.

**How to apply:** when picking segments to cut (`/shorts`) or choosing which Short to
post first, ask "what does the viewer walk away with?" If the honest answer is "that
Dan is in shape," don't cut it and don't post it. Note this overrules a source doc's
own annotation — the V6 notes called `v6-short1` the "best standalone hook," and it
still failed this test. Related: [[shorts-production-style]], [[video-outline-style]].
