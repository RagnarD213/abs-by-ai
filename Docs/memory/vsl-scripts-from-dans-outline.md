On 2026-11-09 Dan read Claude's Great Again T scripts (Script A v4.1, Script B v3.1, built from Claude's own outlines and AI "operator panels") and called them "awful, terrible, terrible, terrible ... absolutely unacceptable output ... it would lose all my money." He tried every other AI too and concluded **AI cannot write a script like this without an outline** (his outline). He then wrote the full outline himself (Doc `1JA4ICZK7zh2wB73Acegteaq46SAMjludhXXLF5eAyes`).

The rule:
- **Discard the default Claude approach to VSL writing.** Write like Dan writes, like V Shred writes, like TestoGreens writes. Not standard Claude output, not "AI slop."
- **Script only from Dan's outline.** Keep his lines nearly verbatim; expand only where he left an instruction ("give 1 more food swap example of your choice", "use similar language to V Shred").
- **The intro must catch attention in the first 30 seconds or he skips the rest.** It needs two things at once: (1) what is proven (the winners' food angle), and (2) an ORIGINAL idea viewers have not seen in TestoGreens, V Shred or other testosterone VSLs. Claude's intros were "just a weaker version of the proven ideas." Dan's own answer: "eat this, not that" food swaps, which combine the proven food angle with another proven format (the Healthy Back Institute "do this, not that" commercial and others he knows work) and are still new in this category.
- **Sell, don't hedge.** Dan: "you're worrying too much about being compliant and not offending ... but you just don't care at all about this: actually selling the product ... All your effort put into compliance is for nothing because nobody ever even buys it." He puts the odds that any new offer fails at 8 in 10. Write at the winners' claim strength and urgency; no softening, no caveats, no disclaimers in the copy. Keep studies and Dan's facts real (they are the proof on screen), but never let caution weaken the sell.
- **Brief only what the outline asks.** 2026-11-09: Claude's contest brief told the writers to deliver "the eight strongest food swaps" (3 hook swaps plus 5 more) because the deep hook promises "five more simple food swaps". Dan's outline asked for the egg example plus ONE more swap; the five are a tease, as the winners tease foods without walking through them. All three scripts followed the brief and paid off eight swaps; Dan: "that wasn't in the outline." Never add items, sections, lengths or lists the outline doesn't call for; when a line promises something the outline never delivers, leave it as a tease.
- AI "operator panels" rating our script top 10% were not evidence: Dan's read overrules them. Don't present AI-panel odds as quality proof.

- **Contest result (Dan, 2026-11-10):** blind test of his own outline: Fable 5.2 xhigh 1st (most natural language, smoothest transitions, the counterintuitive potato-vs-protein-shake swap, strongest ingredient and nutrient breakdowns), Opus 5.5 xhigh 2nd (clunky transitions; orange juice vs water read as common YouTube knowledge), Codex GPT-6 Astra xhigh 3rd ("by far the worst ... awful": rewrote his good lines into weak AI phrasing). - **Every proof section sells an ingredient in the bottle.** 2026-11-10: Claude's research brief picked the "X nutrients" by testosterone research (zinc, vitamin D, magnesium, boron, fat), so all three scripts promoted vitamin D and magnesium, which Great Again T does not contain. Dan: "The purpose of that section was to promote our formula." Choose studies, nutrients and foods that set up our own ingredients (for Great Again T: zinc, boron, L-arginine, the herbs), never by evidence strength alone.
- **Never rewrite Dan's own lines into softer ones** ("we helped 4.4 million men", not "we reached 4.4 million subscribers"; no "One thing that deserves your attention is..."). Counterintuitive beats common knowledge: a swap the prospect already knows (sugar, orange juice) signals "repeating YouTube", not insight.
