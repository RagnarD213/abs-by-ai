For Shorts/Reels covers and thumbnails, **prefer real photography over a frame pulled from the video whenever the photography looks better.** Dan's standing rule, given 2026-08-13.

**Never use a frame where he is hunched over, where his posture makes him read heavy/soft, or where his abs are not clearly defined.** He rejected a V4 longform frame (kneeling, torso slightly folded, flat midday light) for exactly this: "looks fat, abs aren't defined." A technically clean, on-topic, eyes-to-camera frame is still a FAIL if the body doesn't look good — the body is the product.

**Why:** the cover is the single image that sells the video in the grid and the feed. An unflattering body shot undercuts the entire brand promise, and a video frame is captured for motion, not for physique — the lighting, posture and moment are rarely optimised for a still.

**How to apply:**
- Check the photo-shoot libraries FIRST (`Media/`, `photos/`, the pool/park shoot folders) before extracting frames from the longform.
- Only fall back to a video frame when no photograph covers the exercise or topic.
- Whichever the source, the still must show: upright or extended posture (never folded/compressed at the waist), visibly defined abs, and good directional light. Reject anything else and look for another frame rather than shipping it.
- Show Dan the candidates before building the finished cover when the choice is close.

Applies to [[shorts-production-style]] and [[thumbnail-design-system]] work.
