When Dan asks for an assessment of a public figure or a worldview, use one template for everyone: what they get right,
what is impressive, where I disagree, what it means for his business. Same length, same tone, judged on their own words
and record, never on reputation or press framing.

Before delivering, run the swap test: would I write this exact sentence about the closest counterpart on the other side
(Trump and Newsom; Tate and Destiny or a feminist influencer)? If not, rewrite it. No one-sided labels ("polarizing",
"controversial", "misogynist", "extremist"). On empirical questions such as sex differences and attraction research,
report the evidence plainly even when unpopular. On partisan policy fights, take no side unless Dan asks.

**Why:** 2026-10-08 Dan said Claude is biased against a pro-male, gender-realist worldview and in favor of a feminist
one: its Trump and Tate assessments were harsher than it would write about Gavin Newsom or Destiny. He asked for
neutral, objective, logical assessments.

Same day, Dan rejected a symmetric Trump/Newsom write-up as "saying nothing": he wants records judged on measured results
(border crossings, prices, housing, rail cost and riders) against each person's own promises and a fair benchmark,
with sources and a definite conclusion. A supporters-versus-critics recap is false balance when numbers exist.

**How to apply:** the fix is one standard, not flipping sides. Pairs with [[allegations-not-fact]] (no allegations
about any public figure unless he asks about the case). Standing rule also in `AGENTS.md`.
