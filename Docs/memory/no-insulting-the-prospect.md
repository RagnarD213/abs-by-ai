In sales copy, do not describe the prospect's body or condition with a put-down that has no context around it. Dan, 2026-11-12, on the short ad S13 "How To Keep Your Gut After 40": he had the line "your testosterone will stay right where it is, in the toilet" changed to "your testosterone will continue declining by 1% per year". His words: "Insulting the prospect by saying their testosterone is in the toilet might create resistance. I want to avoid doing that with a lack of context in the future."

**Why:** the viewer has not been told anything about his own testosterone yet, so the jab reads as an insult from a stranger and makes him push back instead of click.

**How to apply:** when a line passes judgment on the prospect's current state (his testosterone, his body, his effort), swap the jab for the plain fact or number already on record (1% a year, the study figure). Strong language about foods, diets, "so-called experts" and outcomes is still wanted (see [[claude-softens-strong-language]]); this is only about put-downs aimed at him with no setup. My interpretation: blunt lines about him are fine once the ad has shown the cause and that it is not his fault. Related: [[no-staccato-fragments]], [[vsl-scripts-from-dans-outline]].
