# Voice bench: setup `set5-crit`, 2026-10-10

set5, then one revision pass: the dan-voice-critic agent (sees only the draft and 8 real pieces) marks what gives the draft away, the checker script (voice_check.py) reports the numbers outside his bands, and the writer rewrites only the marked spans.

Writer: claude-opus-5-5. Judges: a fresh claude-opus-5-5 and gemini-3.1-pro-preview, each shown 8 real reference passages of the same type and one real / generated pair in random order. A judge is "right" when it picks the AI piece. Chance is 50%. The finish line is 60% or less.

**Judges picked the AI piece 66 times out of 80: 82%** (95% range 73 to 89%).

In the judges' words (most confident right calls, one per cue):

- **structure too organized** (content, gemini): Passage B is far too neatly structured for off-the-cuff speech, using rigid transitions like 'The first topping' and 'The second topping'. It also uses complete, grammatically correct sentences, whereas Passage A features the natural dropped words and looser syntax typical of someone speaking while demonstrating a task.
- **punctuation formatting** (conversion, gemini): Passage B uses Title Case for all its subheadings and link text, which is a common AI formatting habit, whereas Passage A uses standard sentence case like the reference texts. Passage B also combines sentences into longer, smoother structures ('The audio version is $47 on its own and the eBook is $27 on its own, but when you upgrade...'), losing the punchy, fragmented rhythm seen in Passage A and the references.
- **sentence rhythm** (ads, gemini): Passage A uses slightly more rigid, textbook-like transitions and rhetorical questions that feel formulaic. Passage B contains a distinct capitalization typo ('youR') which is a strong indicator of human typing rather than AI generation.
- **too clean grammar** (ads, claude): A is tidied into short punchy beats with a stock rhetorical question and clean parallel phrasing. B keeps human slips such as "found out what most books" and "a lot of kind of advice", the redundant "$214 million dollars", and the repeated "See," and "out there".
- **vocabulary** (ads, gemini): Passage B includes filler phrases like 'Now, I know what you're thinking' and 'sounds complicated and expensive' which are typical of AI trying to sound conversational. Passage A matches the direct, punchy, and repetitive structure seen in the reference texts.
- **performs the voice** (content, gemini): Passage B tries too hard to sound conversational by adding filler phrases like 'guys' and 'All right, let's talk about', which feels like an AI performing the persona. Passage A matches the reference texts' straightforward, slightly repetitive, and unpolished transcript style without overdoing the casual markers.
- **over explaining** (products, claude): A writes the four elements in lowercase and then lists all four by name, where Dan consistently capitalises 'Four Elements' and would not re-explain them; it also adds a direct-address advice line and a 'So what do' opener. B keeps his capitalisation, the looser repetition ('more, more, more') and the slightly awkward restating in the last paragraph that matches his real prose.
- **tidy ending** (products, standin): B lists the four elements by name and adds a direct 'If this sounds like you' address, which makes it feel like a polished summary. A has the clumsier, more repetitive phrasing ('more, more, more', 'deficient in one of the Four') that matches the reference passages.

The ceiling and floor columns come from 2,500-word samples, so a bigger pooled sample can beat the ceiling. Under 1,500 words a number is noise and is marked ~.
