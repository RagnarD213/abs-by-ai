# Voice bench: setup `set5-fable`, 2026-10-10

set5 with Fable 5.1 as the writer instead of Opus 5.5. Same passages, same prompt. Tests whether the writer model matters (the hard judge is Opus).

Writer: claude-fable-5-1. Judges: a fresh claude-opus-5-5 and gemini-3.1-pro-preview, each shown 8 real reference passages of the same type and one real / generated pair in random order. A judge is "right" when it picks the AI piece. Chance is 50%. The finish line is 60% or less.

**Judges picked the AI piece 65 times out of 80: 81%** (95% range 71 to 88%).

In the judges' words (most confident right calls, one per cue):

- **safe generic content** (ads, gemini): Passage B includes the placeholder 'CTA TEXT', which is a dead giveaway of an AI template. It also over-explains the motivation aspect with conversational filler like 'Now, this picture is more important' and 'It worked for me', whereas Passage A stays punchy and direct like the reference texts.
- **over explaining** (content, gemini): Passage B over-explains every physical action with repetitive, formulaic phrasing rather than natural speech patterns. It also lacks the dropped subjects and conversational shorthand found in the real transcript.
- **too clean grammar** (ads, claude): A has genuine slips ("found out what most books", "a lot of kind of advice", "sales for YouTube ads") and rambling repetition typical of a human draft, while B is cleaned up, uses double-hyphen dashes, adds punchy flourishes like "not a typo" and closes with a neat contrast line about theory versus what worked.
- **performs the voice** (content, gemini): Passage B tries too hard to sound casual ('got to worry about', 'really, really light weight', 'little tiny movement'), whereas Passage A matches the speaker's actual rhythm and vocabulary ('baby weight', 'baby range of motion', 'time under tension'). Passage A also includes the speaker's characteristic slightly formal phrasing mixed with casual speech ('most important safety consideration', 'encourage greater time under tension').
- **vocabulary** (content, gemini): Passage B uses 'skipping rope' instead of 'jump rope', which feels less natural for an American speaker like Dan Rose. It also has slightly too clean grammar and lacks the repetitive, slightly messy sentence structure seen in Passage A and the reference texts.
- **structure too organized** (content, claude): A neatly enumerates a first and second topping, each with its own tidy explanatory sentence, and frames the takeaway as a clean 'not X, the takeaway is Y' contrast. B rambles with dropped subjects, loose run-on fragments and off-the-cuff phrasing like 'here's our finished product'.
- **sentence rhythm** (content, claude): B breaks everything into short, tidy, well-formed sentences and adds neat explanatory extras (the point about a location shutting down, 'on my own terms', 'My theory is'). A rambles in long, loosely joined sentences with tangled clauses that trail off, which is how he actually talks off the cuff.
- **tidy ending** (products, claude): B smooths everything into short, evenly balanced sentences, adds explanatory glue and closes with a neat payoff line about her opening up, while A keeps the author's clunkier phrasing, double-dash asides, repeated 'always, always' and ends abruptly without a wrap-up.
