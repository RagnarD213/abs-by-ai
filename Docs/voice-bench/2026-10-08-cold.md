# Voice bench: setup `cold`, 2026-10-08

Cold Claude: no guide and no examples. The writer sees only the content brief and one line on who Dan is. This is the floor every other setup is measured against.

Writer: claude-opus-5-5. Judges: a fresh claude-opus-5-5 and gemini-3.1-pro-preview, each shown 8 real reference passages of the same type and one real / generated pair in random order. A judge is "right" when it picks the AI piece. Chance is 50%. The finish line is 60% or less.

**Judges picked the AI piece 69 times out of 80: 86%** (95% range 77 to 92%).

In the judges' words (most confident right calls, one per cue):

- **structure too organized** (ads, gemini): Passage B groups the text into longer, chunky paragraphs and uses colons to format its list, which is a classic AI structural habit. Passage A mimics the author's actual style of using short, punchy, one-to-two sentence paragraphs and matches his exact phrasing from the references.
- **performs the voice** (content, gemini): Passage B inserts artificial conversational fillers like 'man,' 'hey,' and 'like' to sound casual, which the real transcripts don't use. It also over-dramatizes the phrasing to perform an off-the-cuff persona, whereas Passage A matches the straightforward, slightly repetitive structure of the references.
- **too clean grammar** (conversion, gemini): Passage B uses perfect punctuation and spells out 'number one', whereas Passage A uses the author's characteristic double dashes, double dots, '#1', and occasionally drops periods at the ends of paragraphs.
- **tidy ending** (conversion, gemini): Passage B adds a neat concluding summary at the end and uses overly structured lists, whereas A matches the author's exact repetitive phrasing and capitalization habits.
- **sentence rhythm** (products, claude): A chops the material into punchy one-line paragraphs with a rhetorical "Why? Because" setup, a "The fix is simple:" colon and neat paired contrasts, which the references never do. B keeps his dense paragraphs, capitalised coined terms like Four Elements, a comma splice and the slightly clumsy "more, more, more" repetition.
- **over explaining** (products, claude): B pads and explains everything (restating low hopes, 'who was my partner', 'That's not life-changing money') and performs candor with 'I'll be straight with you: I lied', in evenly clipped tidy sentences. A has marketing-copy quirks like ellipses, caps emphasis, the '(lied)' parenthetical, 'even broker than I was' and a tense slip, which read as genuine.
- **vocabulary** (ads, claude): A reuses Dan's own stock phrasing (completely free, dream body, life gets in the way) and has an inconsistent brand capitalization, while B paraphrases into generic synonyms like custom/diet/ideal body. B also adds performed casual filler (stuff life throws, something always comes up) and neat "Tip number one:" labelled blocks.
