# Voice bench: setup `set5`, 2026-10-10

Step B2 and B3 alone: no guide and no rules. The writer sees 5 whole real passages from the sample bank, chosen by format and length (never by topic, never from the same source as the test passage), and writes the next piece in the set.

Writer: claude-opus-5-5. Judges: a fresh claude-opus-5-5 and gemini-3.1-pro-preview, each shown 8 real reference passages of the same type and one real / generated pair in random order. A judge is "right" when it picks the AI piece. Chance is 50%. The finish line is 60% or less.

**Judges picked the AI piece 66 times out of 80: 82%** (95% range 73 to 89%).

In the judges' words (most confident right calls, one per cue):

- **too clean grammar** (content, gemini): Passage A uses perfectly parallel sentence structures and overly formal phrasing that feels written rather than spoken. It performs the off-the-cuff voice by simply tacking filler words onto rigid, highly organized sentences.
- **sentence rhythm** (ads, gemini): Passage B breaks the text into overly neat, single-sentence paragraphs and removes the conversational conjunctions (like starting sentences with 'And') that are highly characteristic of the author's actual style in Passage A.
- **structure too organized** (content, claude): B is tidied into neat signposted steps ("A good example of this", "So here's what I recommend") with varied, polished wording like "primary selling tool" and "wide range of products". A keeps the looser, repetitive spoken habits of the references, hammering "e-commerce store" over and over and piling up intensifiers like "really, really" and "heavily recommend".
- **over explaining** (products, claude): B is tidied into short, evenly balanced sentences, restates the not-the-whole-LTV point twice and adds a neat benefit summary about not doing math in your head. A has the false starts, stacked 'you're going to want to' constructions and tangled run-on clauses of his real unscripted speech.
- **punctuation formatting** (ads, claude): A has Dan's real habits: mid-sentence ellipses stringing clauses together, an all-caps NEVER, and a long repeated "you don't need" run in one sentence. B flattens these into tidy short fragments, drops the ellipses and caps, and adds a staged "Listen -" opener and a neat "So click" close.
- **performs the voice** (conversion, gemini): Passage A relies on cliché copywriting filler like 'let me introduce myself' and 'here's what I want you to do,' which feels like an AI performing a generic marketer persona. It also breaks lists into artificially choppy, repetitive sentences rather than flowing naturally.
- **vocabulary** (conversion, gemini): Passage A uses slightly more formal phrasing like 'There are two reasons' and 'First, now that I've become financially free', whereas Passage B uses the author's typical conversational rhythm with ellipses and all-caps emphasis ('BLOWN AWAY', 'two reasons...'). Passage B also matches the author's exact phrasing habits from the references, such as 'No hassles, no questions asked' (A says 'No hassle, and no questions asked').
