# Voice bench: setup `pairs5`, 2026-10-10

Contrast pairs alone: no guide and no rules. The writer sees 5 worked examples from the bank, each one a neutral brief, how Claude wrote it cold, and what Dan really wrote for the same content, then writes Dan's version of a new brief.

Writer: claude-opus-5-5. Judges: a fresh claude-opus-5-5 and gemini-3.1-pro-preview, each shown 8 real reference passages of the same type and one real / generated pair in random order. A judge is "right" when it picks the AI piece. Chance is 50%. The finish line is 60% or less.

**Judges picked the AI piece 69 times out of 80: 86%** (95% range 77 to 92%).

In the judges' words (most confident right calls, one per cue):

- **punctuation formatting** (ads, gemini): Passage A uses perfect punctuation and groups sentences into dense, logical paragraphs. The real author writes in punchy, single-sentence lines and frequently omits periods at the end of his paragraphs.
- **too clean grammar** (ads, gemini): Passage B fixes the natural drafting errors present in Passage A, resulting in overly clean grammar. It also uses perfectly parallel sentence structures that feel too polished and robotic for a casual ad script.
- **vocabulary** (products, gemini): Passage A sanitizes the author's crude vocabulary by swapping 'girls' for 'women' and 'getting laid' for 'having sex'. It adopts a more clinical, polite tone that lacks the raw, conversational edge of the reference texts.
- **over explaining** (content, gemini): Passage B over-explains natural phrases, turning 'breaking out' into 'breaking out with acne' and 'double chin' into 'chin with a fold under it'. It also lacks the human typo ('look at a lot better') found in Passage A.
- **structure too organized** (content, gemini): Passage A is overly structured and formulaic, with perfectly parallel bullet-point-like sentences ('First, put...', 'Second, place...', 'Third, look...', 'And finally, squeeze...'). It also ends with a very generic, tidy call to action ('Comment below to let me know...'), which feels like a standard AI template for a social media post rather than the more conversational, slightly messy flow of the reference texts.
- **performs the voice** (content, claude): B smooths everything into complete, well-joined sentences, adds a staged "All right, guys" and a planted repeated phrase to look like a transcript. A has the looser spoken logic of real speech: rhetorical questions in a list, a pronoun slip on whose behavior must change, and a clipped fragmentary verdict.

Delta: function-word distance from Dan's Tier 1 average, lower is closer. LUAR: style-embedding similarity, higher is closer. "Dan's held-out" is the real side of the pairs, scored the same way: where the generated side should land, and the fair comparison (same amount of text). The ceiling and floor columns come from 2,500-word samples, so a bigger pooled sample can beat the ceiling. Under 1,500 words a number is noise and is marked ~.
