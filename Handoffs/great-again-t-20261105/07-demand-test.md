# 7. Launch a capped purchase test and decide whether to scale
Owner: Codex, GPT-6.1 Sol high, with Dan deciding the budget and scale. Inputs: approved assets, actual live page and checkout, approved price/terms, target and break-even acquisition cost from step 2. Output: launch sheet and dated decision note. This handoff does not turn on advertising or authorize spend.

Before launch, check a phone path from each first-wave ad → its promised content → selected pack → checkout. Confirm a purchase records once with the right value, currency, order identifier and creative attribution; exclude test orders and separate new customers from repeats. Verify policy eligibility for the actual ad/destination and audience, not competitor precedent. Confirm fulfillment and inventory, and check refund terms against the reserve.

Write down total and daily spend caps, purchase-attribution window, stop date and minimum evidence for a scale decision before traffic. Start with three distinct concepts from step 5 and one offer. Log spend, paid first orders, collected revenue, pack mix, cost per buyer and first-order contribution after advertising. Views, play-through and clicks diagnose where people leave. They cannot validate demand. If tracking breaks, pause; if the cap is reached, stop and reconcile settled purchases before interpreting it. For insufficient sample size, return “inconclusive” and price any next test; don't declare a winner from one order. Strong ad attention but no checkout starts calls for an ad/page match review. Checkout abandonment calls for verifying shipping, pricing and technical friction. Orders above the allowable cost call for revisiting creative, offer or margin.

Scale in increments only when new-customer acquisition cost remains below the agreed target at meaningful volume and refunds, fulfillment and repeat signals support it. Recheck at the chosen review date and when refund data mature. Keep a separate line for actual retention; no assumed subscriptions financing the pilot. Consider custom formulation and easier-to-swallow delivery after the stock offer earns demand. Greens samples continue separately.
