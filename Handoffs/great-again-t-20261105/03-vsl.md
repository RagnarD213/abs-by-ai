# 3. Write the VSL with Dan
Owner: Dan for voice and final words; Claude Opus 5.5 high for editorial work. Inputs: research and signed-off offer brief. Output: vsl-script.md and a one-page shooting outline. First read project video rules. No filming or editing in this step.

Ask Dan for a short voice note in response to: What did buyers ask you when you sold Test Reload? What do men get wrong when shopping this category? Show me the bottle: what can you tell me about it without asking me to take your word? What would make you tell someone to skip it? Use only anecdotes he confirms. His past role helps explain why he can guide a buyer; it does not demonstrate results from the current product.

Use `creative-brief.md` v1.1 and Dan's latest November 6 direction: demographic fit is uncertain; men 40–60 are a working hypothesis, with historical 50–70 buyers a comparison to test. Spokesperson fit may change who buys. Test Dan and one or two inexpensive talents similar to the successful male presenters and female interviewer. Keep the desire to get their body back and inexpensive production. No pool, “Have a Seat” recreation or elaborate opening. The hard-to-take-tablet objection must be resolved in the offer/product choice before scripting the demonstration; do not assume the current SKU is locked.

Pitch two 30–45-second openings: a direct, empathetic presenter lead with a simple whiteboard action, and a skeptical buyer's question answered by Dan or the selected male presenter at the whiteboard. A female interviewer in the Amy role is an option, not necessarily a buyer character. Borrow the pool ad's message, not its production. Audition talents on the same short passage; separate talent fit from the opening comparison. Give both leads the same bridge into one script. Draft in his spoken voice: viewer's actual problem → useful insight → show evidence → why this specific product → who it suits / does not suit → actual product/serving demonstration → offer, terms, objections and one clear call to action. Time the script with a real read-through, rather than forcing an arbitrary runtime.

Deliver clean spoken copy plus separate shot cues and proof notes. Mark every product claim with its source in the notes; remove anything unsupported. Do not imply Dan's body or results came from this product, or that he takes it daily unless he confirms that separately. Ensure the opening pays off before the pitch. Read aloud with Dan, cut anything he would not say, and lock one main take and two leads. Pass the version number to steps 4 and 5.
