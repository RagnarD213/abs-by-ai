# Great Again T: Verified Claims Pack

Built 2026-11-09. Every study was found and checked by web search; numbers come from the published abstract (PubMed, via Europe PMC) unless marked otherwise. Food numbers: USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy, per 3 oz cooked or 1 oz unless stated) or NIH Office of Dietary Supplements (ODS) tables. "WEAK" = weaker than a human trial or solid human observational study, or too small, uncontrolled or contradicted to carry a claim alone.

**Corrections to the brief:** Naghii 2011 used **10 mg** boron a day, not 6 mg. Habito 2001 is in *Metabolism*. The Mínguez-Alarcón trans fat study is *Asian J Androl* 2017 (Spanish men). Al-Dujaili 2012 (pomegranate) is a conference poster, not a peer-reviewed paper.

## A. Food swaps

### A1. Whole eggs instead of egg whites (hook)
- **Claim:** Whole eggs after lifting beat egg whites for testosterone.
- **Study:** Bagheri R et al., 2021, *J Strength Cond Res* 35(2):411-419.
- **Who:** 30 resistance-trained young men, Iran (with US and Canadian co-authors).
- **Result:** 3 whole eggs vs 6 egg whites (same protein) after each session, 3 sessions a week, 12 weeks. Significantly greater rise in serum testosterone, greater strength gains and more fat loss with whole eggs; muscle mass did not differ. The abstract gives no testosterone numbers; a "+2.4 vs +0.7 ng/mL" figure on a supplement blog could not be verified. Do not use it.
- **Strength:** Small RCT.
- **SAFE TO SAY:** In a 12-week study of 30 young men who lift, the ones who ate three whole eggs after training saw a bigger rise in testosterone than the ones who ate six egg whites.

### A2. Extra virgin olive oil instead of butter (hook)
- **Claim:** Switching from butter to extra virgin olive oil raised testosterone about 17 percent.
- **Study:** Derouiche A et al., 2013, *Nat Prod Commun* 8(1):51-53.
- **Who:** 60 healthy men aged 23 to 40, Morocco.
- **Result:** 2 weeks on butter, then randomized to extra virgin olive oil or argan oil for 3 weeks. Testosterone rose 17.4% (olive) and 19.9% (argan), p < 0.0001; LH rose 42.6% on olive oil. Weight and calorie intake unchanged.
- **Strength:** Small, short trial; before vs after, no group stayed on butter; total T only.
- **Butter itself:** No human study shows butter lowers T (saturated fat correlated *positively* with T in Volek 1997, 12 men, r = 0.77). Claim that olive oil raised T, not that butter lowers it.
- **SAFE TO SAY:** In a study of 60 healthy young men, testosterone rose about 17 percent after three weeks of using extra virgin olive oil instead of butter.

### A3. Steak instead of dry chicken breast (hook): WEAK (indirect)
- **Claim:** Steak has far more zinc and fat than dry chicken breast.
- **Evidence:** No human study compares the two for testosterone. Indirect support:
  - **Zinc (USDA):** top sirloin 4.4 mg (FDC 169458), beef chuck roast 7.3 mg (FDC 170617), chicken breast 0.85 mg (FDC 171477). Sirloin has about 5 times the zinc.
  - **Low-fat diets lower T:** Whittaker J, Wu K, 2021, *J Steroid Biochem Mol Biol* 210:105878: 6 intervention studies, 206 men. Low-fat vs high-fat: total T SMD -0.38 (95% CI -0.75 to -0.01), free T SMD -0.37; stronger in European and American men (-0.52); largest drop 26%, in two low-fat vegetarian studies. Press coverage put the average drop at 10 to 15%.
  - **Hamalainen E et al., 1984, *J Steroid Biochem* 20:459-64:** 30 healthy Finnish men cut fat from 40% to 25% of calories (mostly animal fat replaced, more polyunsaturated fat) for 6 weeks. Testosterone fell from 22.7 to 19.3 nmol/L (about 15%); reversed afterward.
  - **Habito RC, Ball MJ, 2001, *Metabolism* 50(5):505-11:** 15 healthy men (University of Tasmania). Testosterone dropped more 2 hours after a lean meat meal than after the same meal with added animal fat.
- **Against overselling:** Diets with 35% or more of calories from protein cut resting T by about 5.2 nmol/L (Whittaker J, Harris M, 2022, *Nutr Health*: 27 studies, 309 men). In 7 men, 10 days high-protein vs high-carb gave 371 vs 468 ng/dL (Anderson KE 1987, *Life Sci*). A 2026 study of 2,546 Finnish men aged 42 to 60 (Kuopio cohort, *Int J Food Sci Nutr*) linked the highest animal protein intake with about 1.2 nmol/L lower T (cross-sectional).
- **SAFE TO SAY:** Ounce for ounce, a sirloin steak has about five times the zinc of a chicken breast, and studies show that cutting dietary fat way down can lower a man's testosterone.
- **Do not say:** red meat or more protein raises testosterone.

### A4. Water instead of soda or juice
- **Claim:** A big dose of sugar drops testosterone fast.
- **Study:** Caronia LM et al., 2013, *Clin Endocrinol* 78(2):291-6.
- **Who:** 74 men aged 19 to 74 (mean 51); US and Swiss authors.
- **Result:** A 75 g glucose drink cut mean testosterone 25%; still lower at 2 hours (13.7 vs 16.5 nmol/L). Of 66 men with normal T at baseline, 10 (15%) fell into the low range at least once. Same across weight and blood sugar status.
- **Backup:** Chen L et al., 2018, *Reprod Biol Endocrinol*: 545 US men aged 20 to 39 (NHANES). 442+ calories a day of sugary drinks (roughly three 12-oz regular sodas) vs 137 or fewer: 2.29 times the odds of low T (p = 0.041). Cross-sectional.
- **Strength:** Acute human study plus one survey. Pure glucose was tested, not orange juice. A 12-oz regular Coke has about 39 g sugar (label figure, secondary sources).
- **SAFE TO SAY:** When 74 men drank a sugary glucose drink, their testosterone dropped an average of 25 percent, and about 1 in 7 men with normal levels briefly fell into the low range.

### A5. Alcohol-free beer instead of regular beer
- **Study:** Sierksma A et al., 2004, *Alcohol Clin Exp Res* 28(5):780-5.
- **Who:** 10 healthy middle-aged men (plus 9 women), Netherlands.
- **Result:** Randomized crossover, all food supplied: beer with 40 g alcohol a day at dinner (about 3 US standard drinks) vs alcohol-free beer, 3 weeks each. Men's testosterone fell 6.8% (95% CI -1.0 to -12.5).
- **Strength:** Small controlled trial; modest effect.
- **SAFE TO SAY:** In a controlled study, men who drank about three beers a day with dinner for three weeks had testosterone about 7 percent lower than when they drank alcohol-free beer.

### A6. Real-fat foods instead of low-fat diet foods
- **Study and who:** Whittaker and Wu 2021 (6 studies, 206 men) and Hamalainen 1984 (30 Finnish men); details in A3.
- **Strength:** Small meta-analysis; best dietary-pattern evidence here.
- **SAFE TO SAY:** When researchers pooled the diet studies, men on low-fat diets had lower testosterone than men eating more fat.

### A7. Pumpkin seeds instead of pretzels or chips: WEAK (nutrient logic)
- **Numbers (USDA, per oz):** roasted pumpkin seed kernels 2.2 mg zinc, 156 mg magnesium (FDC 170557); hard pretzels 0.3 mg zinc, 8 mg magnesium (FDC 167555); potato chips 0.3 mg zinc, 18 mg magnesium (FDC 169677).
- **Strength:** No testosterone study of pumpkin seeds; link runs through zinc and magnesium (B1, B3).
- **SAFE TO SAY:** An ounce of pumpkin seeds has about seven times the zinc of an ounce of pretzels, and about nineteen times the magnesium.

### A8. Salmon instead of canned tuna: WEAK
- **Numbers:** sockeye salmon 14.2 mcg (570 IU) vitamin D (USDA FDC 173692, matches NIH ODS); canned light tuna in water 1.0 mcg (40 IU) (NIH ODS, as reproduced by NDSU Extension).
- **Strength:** Vitamin D trials are mixed (B2). Fish oil users among 1,679 young Danish men (Jensen 2020, *JAMA Netw Open*) had larger testes and lower LH, but the abstract reports no testosterone rise.
- **SAFE TO SAY:** Three ounces of salmon has about 14 times the vitamin D of the same amount of canned tuna.

### A9. Real licorice: WEAK, and conflicts with the product
- Armanini D et al., 1999, *N Engl J Med* 341:1158 (letter): 7 men aged 22 to 24, Italy, 7 g a day licorice preparation for 4 days; testosterone fell (P < 0.001). Armanini 2003, *Exp Clin Endocrinol Diabetes* 111:341-3: down 26% after a week. Josephs RA et al., 2001, *Lancet* 358:1613-4: failed twice to replicate (saliva T).
- **Flag:** Great Again T's blend contains licorice extract. Do not use licorice as a "bad food."

### Checked and rejected
- **Soy:** does NOT lower testosterone. Reed KE et al., 2021, *Reprod Toxicol* 100:60-67: 41 studies, 1,753 men, no effect at any dose or duration; Hamilton-Reeves JM et al., 2010, *Fertil Steril*, same. Keep soy out.
- **Spearmint/mint:** No study in men. Only women with excess androgens (Grant 2010, *Phytother Res*: 42 women with PCOS, 30 days; Akdogan 2007, *Phytother Res*: 21 hirsute women, 5 days) and rats. WEAK.
- **Flaxseed:** Demark-Wahnefried W et al., 2001, *Urology* 58:47-52: 25 prostate cancer patients on low-fat diet plus 30 g flax a day for about 34 days; T fell 422 to 360 ng/dL. No control; flax and low-fat confounded. WEAK.
- **Pomegranate juice:** Al-Dujaili E, Smail N, 2012, *Endocrine Abstracts* 28 P313 (Queen Margaret University): poster only; saliva T up about 24% in 2 weeks. WEAK.
- **Dairy:** No low-fat vs full-fat comparison. Maruyama 2010 (*Pediatr Int*, 7 men): T fell after cow's milk. WEAK.
- **Seed oils vs olive oil:** Hamalainen 1984 changed fat amount and type together. Volek JS 1997 (*J Appl Physiol* 82:49-54, 12 men): T correlated with monounsaturated (r = 0.79) and saturated fat (r = 0.77), negatively with polyunsaturated to saturated ratio (r = -0.63). But Mínguez-Alarcón L 2017 (*Asian J Androl* 19:184-90, 209 Spanish men) linked monounsaturated fat to *lower* T. Contradictory; WEAK.
- **Trans fats:** Mínguez-Alarcón 2017 linked trans fat with lower total and free T (p trend 0.01, 0.02). One small observational study. WEAK.

## B. Nutrients linked to testosterone

**Honest number: five.** "Five nutrients human studies link to testosterone": zinc, vitamin D, magnesium, boron, dietary fat. Only **two** reach "proven": zinc (in men low in it) and enough dietary fat. Never say "proven" for vitamin D, magnesium or boron.

### B1. Zinc
- **Study:** Prasad AS et al., 1996, *Nutrition* 12(5):344-8 (Wayne State University, Detroit).
- **Who and result:** 40 men aged 20 to 80: cell zinc tracked T (r = 0.43). 4 young men on zinc restriction for 20 weeks: T fell 39.9 to 10.6 nmol/L (p = 0.005). 9 marginally deficient men around 64 given about 30 mg zinc a day (as gluconate) for up to 6 months: T rose 8.3 to 16.0 nmol/L (p = 0.02).
- **Limit:** Koehler K et al., 2009, *Eur J Clin Nutr* 63:65-70: 14 zinc-sufficient young men on ZMA for 8 weeks; no T change.
- **Strength:** Small human studies; zinc matters when you are low.
- **SAFE TO SAY:** When researchers cut zinc from young men's diets, their testosterone fell sharply, and when older men low in zinc took it for six months, their testosterone nearly doubled.
- **Food sources:** oysters 32 mg (NIH ODS, farmed raw) to 67 mg (USDA FDC 171980, wild cooked); beef chuck roast 7.3 mg; Alaska king crab 6.5 mg (FDC 174202); sirloin 4.4 mg; pumpkin seeds 2.2 mg per oz. Daily Value 11 mg.

### B2. Vitamin D: mixed
- **For:** Pilz S et al., 2011, *Horm Metab Res* 43(3):223-5: 54 overweight men low in vitamin D in a weight-loss trial; 3,332 IU a day for a year (n = 31) vs placebo (n = 23). T rose 10.7 to 13.4 nmol/L (p < 0.001); placebo unchanged.
- **Against:** Lerchbaum E et al., 2017, *J Clin Endocrinol Metab*: 98 healthy Austrian men, 20,000 IU a week for 12 weeks, no effect; Lerchbaum 2019, *Eur J Nutr*: 94 men with low T, no effect; Marnani 2019 meta-analysis (*Andrologia*, 8 studies): no effect.
- **Strength:** Conflicting RCTs; may help only if deficient.
- **SAFE TO SAY:** In one year-long study of overweight men low in vitamin D, those given vitamin D saw their testosterone rise, while the placebo group saw no change.
- **Food sources:** sockeye salmon 14.2 mcg; large egg 1.1 mcg (yolk); canned light tuna 1.0 mcg.

### B3. Magnesium
- **Study:** Cinar V et al., 2011, *Biol Trace Elem Res* 140(1):18-23, Turkey: sedentary men and taekwondo athletes, 10 mg per kg body weight for 4 weeks (group sizes not in abstract). Free and total T rose, more in athletes.
- **Backup:** Maggio M et al., 2011, *Int J Androl* 34:e594-600: 399 Italian men 65+ (InCHIANTI); blood magnesium strongly linked to T (p = 0.001). Cross-sectional.
- **Strength:** One small trial plus one observational study; moderate to WEAK.
- **SAFE TO SAY:** In a Turkish study, men who took magnesium for four weeks had higher free and total testosterone afterward.
- **Food sources:** pumpkin seeds 156 mg per oz (USDA, matches NIH); almonds 80 mg per oz; boiled spinach 78 mg per half cup (NIH ODS figures as reproduced by Cleveland Clinic). Daily Value 420 mg.

### B4. Boron: WEAK
- **Study:** Naghii MR et al., 2011, *J Trace Elem Med Biol* 25(1):54-8, Iran.
- **Who and result:** 8 healthy men, 10 mg a day for one week: free T rose, estradiol fell significantly. Secondary sources (not the abstract) give free T 11.83 to 15.18 pg/mL (about +28%), estradiol 42.33 to 25.81 pg/mL (about -39%).
- **Strength:** Tiny, one week, no real placebo group.
- **SAFE TO SAY:** In a small one-week study, men taking boron saw their free testosterone go up and their estrogen go down.
- **Food sources (NIH ODS):** prune juice 1.43 mg per cup; avocado 1.07 mg per half cup; raisins 0.95 mg per 1.5 oz; peanuts 0.48 mg per oz. Typical US intake is about 1 mg a day, so food cannot reach the trial dose. Adult upper limit 20 mg.

### B5. Dietary fat
- See A3 and A6. Sources: olive oil, whole eggs, fattier beef. No human trial tests dietary cholesterol itself; do not claim cholesterol raises T.

No other nutrient found with real human trial evidence.

## C. Product ingredients (per 2 tablets)

**Dose gap:** most trials used far larger doses or concentrated extracts than Great Again T. They show what an ingredient can do at the tested dose, not what this product does.

### C1. Tongkat ali root, 400 mg (plain root)
- **Story:** *Eurycoma longifolia*, Southeast Asian shrub (Malaysia, Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand). Name usually glossed "Ali's walking stick"; also "Malaysian ginseng"; pasak bumi in Indonesia. Roots used in Malay and Indonesian folk medicine for strength, stamina and male vitality.
- **Talbott SM et al., 2013, *J Int Soc Sports Nutr* 10:28:** 63 moderately stressed adults (32 men, 31 women), standardized hot-water extract vs placebo, 4 weeks. Saliva cortisol -16%, saliva testosterone +37%; tension, anger, confusion down 11 to 15%. A critique notes the tongkat group started with higher T.
- **Chinnappan SM et al., 2021, *Food Nutr Res* 65:5647:** 105 men aged 50 to 70 with T under 300 ng/dL; Physta extract 100 or 200 mg vs placebo, 12 weeks, double-blind. Total T rose vs placebo; free T not different vs placebo; aging-male symptoms and fatigue improved; strength up. Lead author works for the extract maker.
- **Tambi MI et al., 2012, *Andrologia* 44 Suppl 1:226-30:** 76 men with late-onset hypogonadism (Malaysia), 200 mg extract, 1 month, no placebo: men with normal T went from 35.5% to 90.8%.
- **Henkel RR et al., 2014, *Phytother Res* 28:544-50:** 13 men and 12 women aged 57 to 72, 400 mg extract, 5 weeks, no placebo: T and grip strength rose.
- **Leisegang K et al., 2022, *Medicina* 58:1047:** 5 RCTs pooled: total T up (SMD 1.35, 95% CI 0.57 to 2.14).
- **Strength:** Best-supported ingredient (RCTs plus meta-analysis), but all used standardized water extracts, not plain root.
- **SAFE TO SAY:** Tongkat ali, a root used for centuries in Malaysia and Indonesia, raised testosterone in men over 50 with low levels in a 12-week placebo-controlled trial of a concentrated extract.

### C2. Zinc, 50 mg (oxide)
- **Evidence:** Prasad 1996 (B1), about 30 mg a day.
- **Flag:** 50 mg exceeds the adult upper limit of 40 mg a day (NIH ODS, Food and Nutrition Board).
- **SAFE TO SAY:** Your body needs zinc to make testosterone, and in men who were low, getting enough brought their levels back up.

### C3. Maca, 250 mg
- **Story:** *Lepidium meyenii*, grown at about 4,000 to 4,500 meters in Peru's central Andes; traditional food and fertility aid, including for livestock at altitude. No reliable source for an "Inca warrior" story.
- **Gonzales GF et al., 2002, *Andrologia*:** men aged 21 to 56, Peru, 1,500 or 3,000 mg vs placebo, 12 weeks: desire improved from week 8; **testosterone unchanged**.
- **Zenico T et al., 2009, *Andrologia*:** 50 men with mild ED (Italy), 2,400 mg vs placebo, 12 weeks: IIEF-5 +1.6 vs +0.5 (p < 0.001).
- **Shin BC et al., 2010, *BMC Complement Altern Med* 10:44:** 4 RCTs, "limited evidence."
- **Strength:** Small RCTs; trial doses 6 to 12 times the product's.
- **SAFE TO SAY:** Maca, an Andean root grown above 13,000 feet, improved sexual desire in a 12-week placebo-controlled trial, without changing testosterone.

### C4. L-arginine, 250 mg
- **Rhim HC et al., 2019, *J Sex Med* 16:223-34:** 10 RCTs, 540 men with mild to moderate ED; 1,500 to 5,000 mg a day improved ED (odds ratio 3.37, 95% CI 1.29 to 8.77); desire unchanged. No testosterone evidence. Doses 6 to 20 times the product's.
- **SAFE TO SAY:** At higher doses, the amino acid arginine improved erections in men with mild to moderate erectile problems across ten clinical trials.

### C5. Panax ginseng plus eleuthero, 125 mg
- **Story:** Panax ginseng grows in Korea, Northeast China and the Russian Far East; Chinese renshen means "man root," for its human shape.
- **Jang DJ et al., 2008, *Br J Clin Pharmacol* 66:444-50:** 6 placebo-controlled RCTs of red ginseng for ED (349 men): risk ratio 2.40 (95% CI 1.65 to 3.51); low trial quality.
- **Hong B et al., 2002, *J Urol* 168:2070-3:** 45 men with ED (Korea), 2,700 mg a day, 8-week crossover: erection scores improved; 60% reported better erections.
- **Eleuthero ("Siberian ginseng," a different plant):** rigorous trials show no endurance benefit (2005 review); no testosterone evidence. WEAK.
- **Strength:** Suggestive for erections; no testosterone effect; trial dose over 20 times the product's.
- **SAFE TO SAY:** Korean red ginseng, called "man root" in Chinese, improved erections in several small placebo-controlled trials.

### C6. Proprietary blend, 745 mg (individual amounts unknown)
- **Tribulus ("puncture vine," Gokshura in Ayurveda, made famous by 1990s Bulgarian athletes):** Qureshi A et al., 2014, *J Diet Suppl*: "ineffective for increasing testosterone levels in humans." Kamenov Z et al., 2017, *Maturitas* 99:20-6: 180 men aged 18 to 65 with mild to moderate ED (Bulgaria), 1,500 mg a day standardized extract vs placebo, 12 weeks: IIEF improved 2.70 points more than placebo. SAFE TO SAY: Tribulus improved erections in a 180-man placebo-controlled trial, although it does not raise testosterone.
- **Pumpkin seed:** GRANU (Vahlensieck W et al., 2015, *Urol Int* 94:286): 1,431 men aged 50 to 80 with prostate urinary symptoms; whole seeds beat placebo descriptively over 12 months. No testosterone data. WEAK.
- **Nettle root:** binds SHBG in lab tests only; urinary symptom benefit in 620 men with enlarged prostate (Safarinejad 2005, *J Herb Pharmacother*). No testosterone trial. WEAK.
- **Muira puama:** Amazonian "potency wood"; only an old uncontrolled 262-man report that cannot be located, plus animal data. WEAK.
- **Catuaba:** Brazilian traditional aphrodisiac bark; no human trials. WEAK.
- **Sarsaparilla:** testosterone has never been found in any plant (RxList); no human evidence. WEAK.
- **Oat straw:** data called insufficient. WEAK.
- **Cayenne:** one correlational study (114 French men who chose more hot sauce had higher saliva T); capsaicin raised T only in mice. WEAK.
- **Orchic, oyster extract, astragalus:** no human testosterone trials (astragalus: lab and animal only). WEAK.
- **Boron:** see B4. **Licorice extract:** see A9 flag.

## D. Health stakes of low testosterone

- **Mortality:** Araujo AB et al., 2011, *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* 96:3007-19: 12 community studies (all-cause: 11 studies, 16,184 men; mean age 61; 9.7 years follow-up). Low T linked to higher death risk; pooled all-cause RR 1.35 (95% CI 1.13 to 1.62), cardiovascular 1.25 (0.97 to 1.60, not significant), figures from the full text as cited in a later review; authors warn underlying poor health may drive it. Yeap BB et al., 2024, *Ann Intern Med* 177:768-81 (individual data, 9 studies, 255,830 person-years, plus 11 studies, 24,109 men): T under 7.4 nmol/L (213 ng/dL) meant higher all-cause death; under 5.3 nmol/L (153 ng/dL), higher cardiovascular death. Strong observational; not proof of cause.
  - **SAFE TO SAY:** In large studies following men for years, those with low testosterone were more likely to die during the study.
- **Type 2 diabetes:** Ding EL et al., 2006, *JAMA* 295:1288-99: 43 studies, 6,427 men. Diabetic men averaged 76.6 ng/dL lower T; men with higher T (449.6 to 605.2 ng/dL) had 42% lower risk (RR 0.58, 95% CI 0.39 to 0.87).
  - **SAFE TO SAY:** Men with higher testosterone had about 42 percent lower risk of developing type 2 diabetes.
- **Fractures:** Meier C et al., 2008, *Arch Intern Med* 168:47-54: 609 men over 60, Dubbo, Australia, median 5.8 years. Lower T raised fracture risk (HR 1.33, 95% CI 1.09 to 1.62); hip fracture HR 1.88 after full adjustment.
  - **SAFE TO SAY:** In men over 60, lower testosterone was linked to a higher risk of broken bones, especially hip fractures.
- **Heart disease (mixed):** Ruige JB et al., 2011, *Heart* 97:870-5: 19 studies; RR 0.89 per standard deviation higher T; no link under 70 (1.01); over 70, 0.84. Low T "may indicate a poor general health."
  - **SAFE TO SAY:** In men over 70, higher testosterone was linked to somewhat lower heart disease risk. (Do not say low T causes heart disease.)
- **Decline with age:** Feldman HA et al., 2002, *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* (Massachusetts Male Aging Study): 1,709 men aged 40 to 70, 1,156 rechecked 7 to 10 years later; within the same men total T fell 1.6% a year, bioavailable 2 to 3%; across ages, 0.8% a year. Harman SM et al., 2001, *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* (Baltimore Longitudinal Study of Aging): 890 men; low levels in about 20% over 60, 30% over 70, 50% over 80.
  - **SAFE TO SAY:** After 40, a man's testosterone drops about 1 to 2 percent a year on average, and by 80 about half of men have levels in the low range.

## Final lists

### 8 strongest food swaps (hook swaps first)
1. Whole eggs instead of egg whites (small RCT)
2. Extra virgin olive oil instead of butter (small trial, +17%)
3. Steak instead of dry chicken breast (WEAK, indirect: 5 times the zinc; low-fat diets lower T)
4. Water instead of soda or juice (25% drop after glucose)
5. Alcohol-free beer instead of regular beer (6.8% drop)
6. Real-fat foods instead of low-fat diet foods (meta-analysis)
7. Pumpkin seeds instead of pretzels or chips (WEAK, zinc and magnesium content)
8. Salmon instead of canned tuna (WEAK, 14 times the vitamin D)

Keep out: soy, spearmint, flaxseed, pomegranate, dairy, licorice.

### The five nutrients and best food sources
1. **Zinc:** oysters, beef chuck roast, Alaska king crab, sirloin steak, pumpkin seeds
2. **Vitamin D:** sockeye salmon, egg yolks, canned tuna (small amount)
3. **Magnesium:** pumpkin seeds, almonds, spinach
4. **Boron:** prune juice, avocado, raisins, peanuts
5. **Dietary fat:** extra virgin olive oil, whole eggs, fattier beef
