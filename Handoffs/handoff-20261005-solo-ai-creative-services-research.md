## Research questions that must be answered

### Which service should earn the first payment?

For each of the six services, compare buyer demand, credible prices, scope, proof required, time to win a first job, payment timing, AI suitability, human review burden, revision risk, likely contribution after direct costs, repeat potential, and capacity for one person. Distinguish an easy first transaction from the best sustainable business.

Compare a narrow first offer with listing all six services. Recommend one primary entry service and, if justified, one complementary service. Give a concrete buyer, deliverables, required inputs, revision limit, delivery assumptions, initial price, and next purchase. Treat proposed prices as hypotheses until supported by actual buyer evidence.

### Where should he find buyers?

Compare Upwork with several relevant alternatives, such as Fiverr, Contra, PeoplePerHour, specialist creator/video job boards, and appropriate business communities. Research their actual discovery model, new-seller barriers, buyer budgets, costs to apply or list, competition, payment timing, and fit for written selling. Do not assume every platform has meaningful inbound leads for a new profile.

Inspect a manageable sample of recent jobs across the candidate services, ideally 12-18 useful examples. Record URLs, observation dates, deliverables, budgets, applicant activity where available, and buyer hiring/payment history where visible. Posted budgets are not completed-sale evidence. Check whether a large advertised budget covers one asset, a batch, a month, or an entire contract. Exclude suspicious or impractical listings from the recommended targets.

For marketplace clients moving to direct payment, give the current, platform-specific route: timing, conversion or buyout fees if applicable, restrictions, and the point at which it makes economic sense. Keep this practical and brief. Distinguish those relationships from clients independently acquired through Dan's later website. Do not build the plan around immediately moving marketplace buyers off-platform.

### What comparable businesses actually work?

Study approximately 5-8 relevant operators or businesses offering AI-assisted creative services, fixed-scope creative packages, or recurring production. Favor primary sources showing offers, prices, portfolios, acquisition channels, and delivery arrangements.

Verify solo status when possible. Label team size unknown when it cannot be established. A business calling itself an AI agency may still depend on employees or contractors. Separate demonstrated practices from founder marketing, course-selling claims, and unverifiable revenue screenshots. Extract what Dan could reproduce without a team and what he could not.

### Should the business specialize in fitness?

Compare fitness/wellness with a few realistic alternatives, such as established ecommerce brands, expert-led businesses, or other advertisers needing repeat creative work. Consider purchasing power, repeat demand, source-footage availability, how interesting Dan finds the products, and the advantage his past advertising experience provides. Do not choose fitness solely because that is the current project folder.

### Can campaign management fit a solo AI business?

Assess how much research, reporting, monitoring, creative testing, and routine account work can reliably be done with today's tools. Distinguish automation already working in this project from theoretical automation.

Compare a one-time account audit/setup, ongoing management, and creative plus management. Estimate Dan's weekly account workload, client communication, reporting, troubleshooting, and realistic capacity. Include proposed minimum account suitability and fees as explicit assumptions. Identify which actions need Dan's judgment. Do not claim AI can autonomously guarantee campaign performance or eliminate responsibility for client spend.

### How does it grow after the first jobs?

Propose a staged path from the first paid project to repeat buyers, a simple portfolio website, direct acquisition, and a manageable solo business. Explain the role of referrals, evidence from completed work, and recurring packages. Estimate the limits of a no-employees model and the tradeoffs of raising prices, narrowing scope, or productizing parts of delivery.

The website is a later sales asset, not a prerequisite for answering current marketplace demand. Do not make branding, a logo, a new software stack, or a large website the first milestone.

## Delivery economics and production reality

Use Dan's existing approved creative work and reusable systems as potential portfolio assets. Inspect what is actually finished and attributable to him before making capability claims. Some assets were edited by outside editors; distinguish his contribution from theirs. Current app sales and organic growth do not establish that the recent creative is commercially successful. Historic advertising experience and visual production quality can still be presented accurately.

The project contains lengthy revision histories. AI involvement does not establish fast delivery, low labor, or scalable profit. Build the estimate around total Dan time: prospecting, briefing, outlining, generation supervision, review, revisions, export, communication, and invoicing. Include failed generations and direct tool costs. Separate existing subscription overhead from incremental project costs.

Use a small range of scenarios and show the assumptions. Include collected revenue, contribution before Dan's labor, and effective earnings per total hour. A small first job may be useful validation without representing a sustainable rate.
