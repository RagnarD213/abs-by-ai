## Upwork rules that shape everything (checked 2026-10-29, sources in the 10-07 handoff plus below)

- **No bots.** Scrapers, browser extensions, auto-refresh, page monitors, scripts using session cookies: banned, risk of a permanent block. The only allowed route for an agent is the official connector, `https://mcp.upwork.com/mcp` (works with Claude Code; free; Connects still apply). It can search jobs, read them, draft and submit proposals, read and send messages, submit work. **Every write is a draft Dan confirms.** Accepting offers and money happen on upwork.com. Its terms forbid continuously monitoring job listings: run searches as specific tasks a few times a day, not a polling loop. Rate limits: searches 15/min, 150/hour, 750/day; proposals 20/min, 60/hour, 150/day. If a client would reasonably think a message came from a human, AI origin must be disclosed, so Dan reads and owns every message he sends.
- **No feedback building.** Never offer free or discounted work in exchange for a review, never ask friends to hire him for reviews (Upwork deletes "insider" feedback and can ban). Low launch prices are fine as long as they are never tied to a review.
- Minimum fixed price $5. Freelancer service fee 0 to 15% per contract (shown before submitting). Connects $0.15 each; per-proposal cost varies by job (third parties say about 16 to 32; check live). Boosted proposals bid extra Connects for the top four slots.
- Rising Talent badge: 4.8+ rating and $250 earned in 12 months, or an Upwork invite. Consultations unlock only with a badge.

## Two engines

### Engine 1: Upwork bids

- **Search (Claude through the connector), 3 times a day:** direct-response ad scripts, video ads, YouTube ads, VSL, ad editing, ad thumbnails, AI video ads, Google/YouTube Ads management. Also single-part jobs that touch ads (edit an ad, thumbnail for an ad, AI footage for an ad). Skip vlogs, weddings, general channel editing (the profile excludes them). Freelancer Plus job alerts are a second trigger; speed matters (replies roughly halve after 30 minutes, per bid-tool data).
- **Score each job (record the reasons):** fit with the profile 30, clear brief and usable assets 20, budget 20, client signals (payment verified, spend history, hire rate) 15, repeat potential 15. Shortlist at 75+. Missing data is unknown, not zero.
- **Proposal formula (short):** first line names their product and one specific thing about their current ad or page (watch it first; brands' live ads are public in Google's Ads Transparency Center and Meta's Ad Library). Then 3 new hook ideas for their product, the most relevant portfolio piece with its number, price and timing, one question. Lead with money results, never "AI". Answer screening questions directly.
- **Pricing while at zero reviews:** bid single-part and small jobs at launch prices (the client's budget or a little under; never below about $50 for real work). Never mention reviews. After 5 good reviews, bid at package prices for small jobs; after 10, full prices everywhere. Credit the $750 audit toward any follow-on order.
- **Volume:** month one about 3 to 5 excellent proposals a day, not a flood (an agency sent 1,689 proposals in 90 days and 94% got no reply). Boost only top-scored jobs.

### Engine 2: direct outreach to brands (off Upwork; fastest cash, no review problem)

Model: Greg Isenberg's "always-on ad studio" (video "Take Upwork jobs, let AI do them", 2026-09-23).
- Each week AI builds a list of fitness, supplement, health and info-product brands, plus ad-buying agencies, that are clearly spending on YouTube or Meta ads but whose ads all make the same argument. Sources: Google Ads Transparency Center, Meta Ad Library, the brands' reviews.
- For the best 5 to 10 a week: a 2-line teardown of their current ads plus 3 watermarked sample hooks or concepts built from their own customer reviews. For the top 2 or 3, Dan records a personal video (method below).
- Offer: a paid trial on one product (Project C or B pricing), then a monthly pack of new ads every week. The first one or two may be free or cheap in exchange for a case study and testimonial (allowed off Upwork); add those to the Upwork portfolio and request a client testimonial through Upwork's testimonial feature.
- Every outbound email or message is sent on Dan's behalf: get one batch approval for the first batch, with the exact list and text, then follow the batch-approval rule.
- Do not send from absbyai.com or Dan's main Gmail. Use a separate sending domain with warmed-up mailboxes (see budget).

## Personal video method (Dan asked for the easiest way)

- **Webcam at the desk, not the studio set.** For a pitch video, specificity beats picture quality; buyers want proof he looked at their ad. Decent light and the existing mic are enough.
- **Format:** Loom (free plan: 25 videos, 5 minutes each) or Mac screen recording uploaded unlisted. Screen shows the client's current ad or page and then the 3 sample hooks Claude made; Dan's face in the corner. 60 to 90 seconds, one take, no editing.
- **Script card:** Claude prepares a 3-bullet card per video: (1) "Hi <name>, I watched your <ad>" plus the one problem; (2) the 3 new angles on screen; (3) the one next step. About 5 minutes per video including prep.
- **No spliced generic intro.** The profile intro video already does that job; a reused opener reads as a template. No AI clone of his face or voice presented as a personal message (Upwork's disclosure rule, and buyers notice).

3. **Training loop (from job one):** after every proposal and job, record what Dan changed and why, every missing input, every revision, where time went. Turn those into rules in a new skill (`.claude/skills/upwork-pitch/`): scoring, proposal templates, sample recipes, intake questions. Corey Ganim's audit drafts went from 60 to 70% right to near copy-paste by job 4 to 6 this way.
4. **Month two (Dan 2 hours a day):** a scheduled Claude routine runs searches 3 times a day, drafts proposals and samples, and leaves an approval queue; Dan confirms in one sitting. Outreach lists, teardowns and follow-ups run on the same queue. Alert Dan only for replies, offers, approval-ready top jobs and failures.

## Tracking

One record per Upwork job ID or prospect: URL, found time, budget, scope, client signals, Connects, score and reasons, proposal version, sample, video yes or no, approval, submission receipt (reopen and confirm the proposal is live), reply, interview, hire, revenue, costs, Dan's minutes. Weekly: proposals sent, reply rate, hire rate, Connects spent, cost per hire, revenue, Dan's hours. Rough expectation to compare against: about 7 replies per 100 proposals and about 1 hire per 50.
